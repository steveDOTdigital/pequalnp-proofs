# A selected AND output for plane incidence

Independent research for L-meta-2608 by steveDOTdigital. The order-three plane has 13 points, 13 lines and 52 incident pairs. The selected input is line zero, denoted by `z`. The output gate is `A AND B`. Each operand must accept every flag. On every maximal incidence-free assignment, `A` must be false if `z=1`, and `B` must be false if `z=0`.

**A 65-gate circuit meets every condition. The posted budget is 64.** The [Lean proof](PlaneSelectedRoot.lean) checks the exact posted statement with only that numeral changed. With no internal sharing the exact minimum is **109** gates. Every 64-gate candidate must have at least **12 distinct internal gates in both operand cones**. An AND-output candidate cannot contain nine completed ordinary stars on either shore. The unrestricted 64-gate question is open.

## Exactly which inputs each operand must reject

Write `H` for the disjunction of the four points on line zero and `F` for incidence. Let `R` be incidence on the other twelve lines, retaining all thirteen points. Those lines form four groups of three according to their intersection with line zero. Let `P` mean that at least one line in every group is active. Every eligible pair of monotone operand functions, and only such pairs, satisfies the pointwise inequalities

```text
F ≤ A ≤ F OR H
F ≤ B ≤ F OR z OR P.
```

Monotonicity forces the lower inequalities from the 52 flags. Consider an incidence-free assignment. It extends to a maximal negative with `z=1` exactly when all four points on line zero are absent. Turn on that line and extend maximally. It extends to a maximal negative with `z=0` exactly when `z=0` already and at least one group has no active line. Turn on the corresponding point and extend maximally. Conversely, a maximal negative omitting line zero contains some point on it, since otherwise one could add the line. That point forbids every line in its group. The two maximal-extension equivalences are kernel-checked. Among 38,730 incidence-free inputs, 704 are maximal, with 197 selected by the first operand and 507 by the second.

The circuit computes `R` by twelve ordinary line stars (59 gates), computes `H` in three gates, and then computes `A = R OR H`, `B = R OR z`, and their AND. This uses 65 gates. Its only shared internal wire is `R`. The verified file gives the actual 65 legal gates, distinct operands, register numbers and evaluation on arbitrary inputs. The [independent replay](plane-selected-root.py) checks all flag and maximal-negative rows.

## Why a formula needs exactly 109 gates

A formula is a binary tree with raw input leaves. Separate occurrences of the same raw input are allowed. On the high slice the first operand computes precisely the OR of the four line-zero points and the edge function of the affine plane obtained by deleting that line and its points. This affine incidence graph has 36 edges and a nine-edge matching (pair each surviving point with the line of its own index). The [degree-four formula lower bound](cubic-formula-leaves.md) gives 44 gates for affine incidence and four more for the singleton alarms. This bound of 48 is attained by the nine affine point stars joined to the four raw points.

On the low slice the second operand must accept all 48 flags of the twelve nonzero lines and reject every incidence-free input using at most three lines. Consider any formula doing just that. Assign its leaf occurrences to cells of the monotone formula comparison protocol. Rows are flags. Columns are required negatives. Each cell reports an endpoint of its flag that is absent from its column. Each leaf receives a rectangle of cells. At an endpoint with fewer than its degree many raw leaves, give each incident flag the nonempty mask of local leaves whose rectangle contains that row.

For any three incident rows a single column forces **all three** to report the common endpoint. Switch on just their other endpoints. These are at most three lines or a pure point input and hence are an allowed negative. The leaves active on that column meet each of those three masks exactly once. A row is dominated at its endpoint when its mask contains another incident row's mask (equality counts). For degree `d ≤ 4` and `r < d` local leaves, the triple-column property forces at least `d-r+1` dominated rows. With one leaf every row is dominated. The other possibilities are (degree, slots) = (3,2), (4,2), (4,3), where the respective minima are 2, 3 and 2. The Lean file checks every sorted mask tuple and the Python checker independently enumerates the stratum counts. Requiring a single common column for all four rows would give the wrong proof for this desk case.

An edge cannot be dominated at both ends. For a flag `pw` and witness flags `pb,aw`, the column containing point `a` and line `b` is independent by the absence of four-cycles. It forces distinct reports for the two witnesses, which would both be inherited by `pw`. The [leaf charging argument](cubic-formula-leaves.md#charging-the-leaves) now gives at least 48 plus 12 raw leaves, using the matching of the twelve nonzero lines with their identically indexed points. The second operand thus needs at least 59 gates on the low slice. It also genuinely depends on `z`: a point of line zero alone is a required negative for this operand and that point with `z` is a flag. Pinning `z=0` deletes at least one more gate. The bound of 60 is attained by `R OR z`.

The branch bounds are simultaneous. Independent copies of the described 48-gate and 60-gate formulas, joined by the final AND, cost exactly 109 gates. In particular, fully unfolding any 64-gate candidate must duplicate at least 45 gate occurrences.

## Restrictions on arbitrary shared circuits

Let `t` be the least number of binary monotone gates for the 21-input affine incidence graph above. The high operand needs at least `t+4` gates. Set `z=1` and then pin the four essential alarm inputs to zero. For the other operand choose a point `p` of line zero. Pin all four lines through `p` and the point itself to zero. This gives the dual affine incidence graph: nine remaining lines and twelve remaining points. All 26 raw inputs were essential in the second operand. For a nonzero point-line flag the one-line inputs force both endpoints to matter in the low slice, and the flag argument above forces `z`. Pinning the five inputs therefore costs at least five gates. In a connected binary output cone, the cycle rank equals gates minus distinct sources plus one. Zero propagation and pruning cannot increase this rank. This proves the claimed cost even when several pins meet at one gate. Relabeling `i ↦ -i modulo 13` and exchanging the shores gives a polarity of the plane, and a cyclic translation takes any chosen point to zero, so both affine restrictions have the same optimum.

The [gate-elimination recurrence](cubic-pair-restrictions.cpp) on the 21-input affine graph gives `33 ≤ t ≤ 44`. It visits 833,099 masks in monotone mode and also gives 32 for the signed lower bound. Its restrictions and first-gate accounting are described in the [source argument](cubic-formula-leaves.md). Thus the first operand has at least 37 distinct gates in its cone and the second at least 38. Their union in a 64-gate circuit contains at most 63 before the output AND. Inclusion-exclusion forces at least twelve shared gates.

A 64-gate witness must contain a shared internal signal that accepts some flag and some incidence-free input. Otherwise each shared signal is safe on all negatives or false on all flags. The [cut-into-formulas argument](plane-shared-formulas.md#cutting-a-circuit-into-formulas) then gives at least 65 raw operand occurrences. With at least one excess internal read, the binary circuit identity gives at least 65 gates. With no excess reads the 109-gate formula bound applies. The 65-gate control attains the bound for this class because its sole shared signal implies incidence.

The same 65-gate minimum holds even if all the other shared signals are arbitrary disjunctions of points. It also holds if they are all disjunctions of lines. To see this, use the [point-helper charging argument](plane-shared-formulas.md#any-number-of-disjunctions-on-one-shore) with a perfect matching. Write `σ` for the total excess internal fanout, and `k` for the number of genuine shared point disjunctions. The argument gives at least `64 + σ − t` gates, where `t` counts the helpers that might destroy a matched edge. Each helper can affect at most one matched edge. A collinear helper can affect its own line only if that line is matched to a point in the helper. Every other matched point belonging to this helper has a masking partner outside its matched line. Noncollinear helpers cannot cause a loss. There are four cyclic perfect matchings, one for each difference 0, 1, 3 and 9, so every point of any given line can be its matched partner.

At 64 gates equality requires `t = k = σ` for **every choice of matching**. Thus each shared gate has exactly two readers, none is safe or inactive, and each helper consists of all four points of one line. A shared copy of a raw wire can be bypassed at a gate saving, and equal helper functions can be identified with the earlier copy. These replacements stay in the same class and give a smaller unrestricted circuit, contrary to its 64-gate bound. Hence at most thirteen distinct helpers remain.

Cut the DAG at those helpers. The private tree for a helper on a line must have a raw leaf for each of its four points. Compare a true singleton on the line against the false assignment with all vertices outside the line active. Every earlier helper true at the singleton is also true on this comparison column, since its line is different. Thus the separating leaf must be the singleton's own raw bit. Each private tree costs at least three gates. Replace all helper trees by three-gate raw ORs for their respective lines. This computes exactly the same functions at no increase in total cost. The output tree stays intact. The new helpers do not feed each other, and each has at most two readers. Fully unfolding therefore costs at most `64 + 3 × 13 = 103` gates. The selected output needs 109 in a formula, a contradiction. Exchanging shores gives the line version. This allows arbitrarily many safe or flag-inactive shared signals in the original circuit. The single safe shared signal of the 65-gate construction attains the bound in both classes.

## Nine ordinary stars on one shore force a different output

An ordinary line star combines its four raw points with three ORs and then ANDs with its line bit. Intermediate outputs may be reused arbitrarily. This exclusion applies to **every** AND-output circuit computing incidence, even with no prescribed choice of rejecting operand.

Move nine distinct line stars to the front, costing 36 gates. Pin all nine completed line bits to zero. As in the [nine-star restriction proof](nine-plane-stars.md), each completed line pins at least one live suffix source (its line bit or its star output). Each point on an unfinished line must feed the suffix directly too. A completed-line point OR containing that point can be saturated by another point outside the unfinished line. The unfinished line alone then witnesses the essential raw read. Pinning additional such points to zero costs at least one gate apiece by the same cycle-rank argument.

Only zeros have been fixed. A nonconstant output AND cannot lose one operand to a true constant in this restriction. Propagate zeroes, contract copies and discard unused gates while retaining the final AND. If it now reads the same wire twice, eliminating it gives a smaller unrestricted circuit, which the ordinary core lower bound already excludes at equality. The same reasoning applies to duplicate free helper wires. This preserves the final-gate information through the count.

* For three concurrent unfinished lines and a transversal, discard seven private points. The core consists of three disjoint length-three paths between two hubs. Its ordinary optimum is 12, and the remaining budget is 12. No optimal solution has an AND output.
* For four unfinished lines in general position, discard four private points. The core is the subdivision of the complete graph on four vertices. The old point ORs yield at most three free ORs of complementary edge pairs. The ordinary optimum is 15 even with all three free, and the remaining budget is 15. Each possible helper-subset size was excluded with a final AND.
* For four lines through one point, the suffix has 28 gates at most and at least 26 compulsory sources: nine completed channels, four line bits and thirteen point bits. It can use at most three further point helpers. If it uses three, its 29 sources force a read-once tree. The common point and an unfinished line each have only their own raw leaf. Their restricted function is AND when the other bits are zero, and OR when another unfinished line and a private point on the chosen line are active. A read-once formula cannot give both operations at their fixed lowest common ancestor. With at most two helpers, choose one of the three private points per line outside both helpers. Pin its two other private points to zero. This leaves four subdivided arms with no nontrivial helpers. The ordinary forest optimum is 11. The remaining eleven gates cannot give an AND output.

These exhaust all 715 choices of the four unfinished lines. Shore duality also excludes nine point stars. With eight same-shore stars, the same zero-only argument applies when four of the five unfinished lines are concurrent. Retaining their common point and the four intersections with the transversal costs eight private points. The four length-three paths have unrestricted optimum 16 and no 16-gate AND-output circuit. This covers 117 choices of five unfinished lines.

The [live-wire search](four-line-live-search.cpp) takes helper count, gate budget, state limit, time limit, geometry and optional final-gate condition. Use `k4` at budget 15 with zero through three helpers, `triple` at 12, `pencil` at 11, and `theta` at 16, each with final argument `and`. The ordinary lower bounds and the reason the live-wire search covers every optimum appear in the [earlier core proof](nine-plane-stars.md#exhaustive-search-on-live-wires). A previously computed wire with a superset of the positive rows and a subset of the negative rows can replace a later one throughout any monotone suffix. A smaller unrestricted circuit resulting from such a simplification is already excluded in the listed equality cases. With no remaining rereads, both children of the final AND require a distinct live wire on each flag. The 15-gate unrestricted positive control was recovered and checked on all 1,024 inputs. Run the Python checker with `--affine-bound` to reproduce the separate affine calculation. The finite core exclusions and formula arguments are external to the kernel-checked near-witness.

The additional mode `two-triples` tests the six-point intersection core for the 702 five-line sets with two triple intersections. The helper mask selects the two complementary free point ORs. Removing the line through both triple points leaves the subdivided complete graph on four vertices. A prefix saving three gates on that zero cofactor cannot extend to an 18-gate AND output if all three savings occur before its final gate. It cannot extend to a 17-gate unrestricted output either. This optional search has no bearing on the completed nine-star exclusions.
