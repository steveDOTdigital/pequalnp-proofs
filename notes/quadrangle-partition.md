# A 64-gate formula lower bound on the order-three quadrangle promise

Independent research on PEqualNP desk case L-meta-1692 by steveDOTdigital.

Every binary monotone formula accepting the 52 flags and rejecting the 234 quadrangles has at least 65 leaves, hence 64 gates. The minimum formulas are exactly the thirteen point stars joined by OR, or the thirteen line stars joined by OR, with arbitrary binary OR trees. More generally, a formula accepting any specified m flags while rejecting all quadrangles needs at least 5m/4 leaves.

The 64-gate bound also holds with arbitrary sharing if every shared signal is constant on at least one of the two input classes. With just one shared signal the bound holds for every one-gate helper, every helper of at most nine gates that accepts no singleton, and every raw disjunction on at least five distinct variables. The general circuit lower bound remains open.

## Counting leaves on the actual promise

The points and lines are indexed modulo 13. A flag (p,l) has p−l in {0,1,3,9}. Its positive input sets just those two variables. A quadrangle is a set of four points with no three collinear. Its negative input sets the four points and all three lines disjoint from them.

Fix any subset E of m flags. A formula partitions E times the 234 negative columns into rectangles, one per leaf occurrence. On a positive flag and a negative column, route at an OR to its first true child on the flag and at an AND to its first false child on the column. Each decision depends only on its corresponding input. The leaf variable is present on the flag and absent from the column. The argument applies to all partitions with these properties, including partitions that do not arise from formulas.

At a vertex v, enumerate its four incident flags and keep the positions in E. Each rectangle labelled v has a row set within those positions. Empty rectangles can be dropped, and rectangles with identical row sets can be merged by union of their columns. Neither operation increases the count. Write k for the number of local rectangles. Call a retained flag e dominated at v if there is a different retained flag f at v such that every local row set containing f also contains e. In every column, reporting v on f then forces reporting v on e.

A flag cannot be dominated at both ends. Suppose the two witnesses adjacent to e=(p,l) are (p,b) and (a,l). The incidence graph has no four-cycle, so (a,b) is a nonedge. A quadrangle column contains both a and b, as certified on the actual inputs. The first exterior flag must report p and the second must report l. Their implications would report both endpoints on e. Thus the total number of dominated local flags is at most m.

Let d be the local dominated count, r the number of retained incident flags, and h the number of cells reporting v over all columns. The local inequalities are

    432k + 216d + h ≥ 486r    at points,
    432k + 216d + h ≥ 504r    at lines.

Here is the finite local argument. At an absent point, each individual neighbor is the unique active neighbor on 27 columns and each pair is the active pair on nine. At an absent line, the counts are 18 and 18. These account for all columns on which the vertex can report. Project the active set onto the retained rows. Any active collection of rectangle row sets must be disjoint, and its union must contain every projected active neighbor. Its contribution to h is the size of that union. For each of the ten nonempty singleton or pair patterns take the smallest possible contribution, multiply by its frequency and sum.

When k is at least r, forced reports alone give h ≥ 54r at points and h ≥ 72r at lines. For k less than r there are at most three distinct nonempty row sets. The Lean kernel checks all 576 increasing families of zero to three four-bit masks, including feasibility, the domination count and the minimum costs just described. The separate Python check repeats the calculation for all 15 nonempty retained-row subsets, including four-rectangle families.

Sum the inequalities. There are m slots per shore, at most m dominated slots, and exactly 234m reports. If L is the total number of rectangles, then

    (486 + 504)m ≤ 432L + 216m + 234m,
    5m ≤ 4L.

In particular, m=52 gives L ≥ 65. Computing each of thirteen four-neighbor disjunctions with three ORs, taking thirteen star ANDs, then joining their outputs with twelve ORs achieves 64. Zero-input constants, unary identities, dead gates and out-of-range outputs cannot improve this bound. The desk's positive tests require a true output, and reachable constant and unary gates can be simplified.

## All minimum formulas

At full degree the only zero-slack local families are one rectangle with all four rows, and four rectangles with singleton row sets. For four or more rectangles, equality requires k=4 and no extra reports beyond the forced reports. Each individual neighbor is the only active neighbor on some column, forcing each of the four singleton row sets to occur. The smallest strictly positive local slack, in the integer units above, is 27 at a point and 36 at a line.

A 65-leaf formula has thirteen single-occurrence variables and thirteen quadruple-occurrence variables. Every flag at a single-occurrence vertex is dominated. Consequently the single-occurrence vertices are independent and their degrees cover all 52 flags. Their complements are independent too, so connectedness makes these classes the two shores. Call the single-occurrence vertices the centers. At every column at which a center is absent, at least one of its neighbors is present; hence its rectangle reports that center on all four flags. At columns containing the center, each of the four opposite-endpoint leaves reports on its own flag. Thus the rectangle partition is fixed, with no empty leaves.

The binary protocol tree now forces the formula shape. With two or more complete center stars and all columns still available, an AND could only split columns. Both the columns containing a center and the columns missing it must remain intact, since each is the full column set of one or more leaves. Distinct centers have column indicators that are neither equal nor complementary. Thus no nontrivial column split is available. An OR can only split whole stars, since the center rectangle spans all four flag rows. Once only one star remains, no row split can precede its center test. An AND separates columns containing the center from those missing it. In the missing-center branch only the center leaf occurs. The other branch contains four singleton-row leaves, all using its entire column set, so only OR joins are possible. No unused protocol branch can occur in a minimum formula. In particular no unordered pair of raw variables appears twice as the operands of a gate, since any two distinct stars have at most one common neighbor.

## Sharp hereditary cases

The same local enumeration shows that at degrees one through three every feasible family with k below the degree has strictly positive slack. At equality for an arbitrary nonempty retained E, such vertices therefore use one singleton rectangle per row: if k equals the degree, equality leaves no extra reports, and each flag can be forced by itself. At degree four the two equality families were determined above. Equality also requires exactly one dominated endpoint on every edge. Hence equality in 5m ≤ 4L occurs precisely when E is a union of full four-edge stars whose centers do not share an edge. One implication follows by taking the degree-four single-rectangle vertices as centers. Conversely the star formula uses five leaves per full star and partitions the tests with exactly that many rectangles.

If E has maximum degree three, there is an exact bound without rounding. It needs m + τ(E) leaves, where τ(E) is its minimum vertex-cover size. To see this, let each edge record, across all 234 columns, whether its point was reported. Disjoint edges have distinct vectors, since at least one crossed point-line pair is a nonedge and a quadrangle contains that pair. Group the flags at each vertex by equal vectors. There are at most k groups. This is immediate for k at least the local degree and for k=1. For k=2, three distinct nonempty row-membership patterns would have to be “first rectangle only,” “second only” and “both.” The first two flags can be forced together at this vertex by a column containing both neighbors. Both rectangles would then overlap on the third flag. Equal row-membership patterns give equal orientation vectors, proving the claim.

Split each vertex into these groups. No retained edge can connect two groups of degree greater than one, since the two exterior witnesses would be disjoint edges with equal orientation vectors. Each nonempty component of the split graph is thus a star. It has one more vertex than edges, and one center per component projects to a vertex cover of E. This proves L ≥ m + τ(E). In the other direction assign each edge to one endpoint in a minimum cover and use the resulting star formula. These conclusions also give the exact minimum number of rectangles in the indicated cases.

## Eliminating class-constant shared gates

This transformation applies to any finite monotone separation problem. Trim unused gates. Say an internal signal is shared when its output occurs at least twice among the remaining input slots. Raw input reuse is unrestricted. Suppose each shared signal is constant on the positive class, the negative class, or both.

First mark each signal that is zero on every positive for substitution by zero, and each that is one on every negative for substitution by one. Process all such substitutions together, keeping the original gates in the count until the end. Induction in gate order shows that values on positive inputs can only increase and values on negatives can only decrease relative to the original circuit. This remains true with interleaved substitutions: a substituted zero is already the original value on positives, and a substituted one is already the original value on negatives. A signal originally zero on every negative therefore remains so, and one originally true on every positive remains so.

Every remaining shared signal is now a certificate of one answer: either a one implies positive, or a zero implies negative. Give the former default zero and the latter default one. Cut all of their outgoing occurrences to these defaults. The residual circuits rooted at each such signal and at the original output are disjoint formulas apart from raw inputs. Combine their roots in reverse topological order. For a zero default, OR its residual value with the accumulator. For a one default, AND its value with the accumulator. On any promised input, the first certificate that differs from its default was evaluated with the correct predecessor values and decides the answer. If all agree, the residual output decides it. The Lean lemma `priority` checks this argument for an arbitrary ordered list of signals.

With K shared gates there were at least 2K input slots using them. At most two such slots belong to a single reader, so at least K distinct reader gates simplify away after the substitutions. The reconstruction adds at most K gates. Hence there is a formula no larger than the circuit. The accompanying 105-gate control interleaves all four classes of shared signals. Its reconstructed 83-gate formula has the same output on all 286 tests. Consequently any circuit of size at most 63 for the desk must share at least one signal that takes both values on flags and both values on quadrangles.

## Lower bounds with one shared result

Suppose H is the only shared signal. Its input is a g-gate formula and it has at least two uses. In a circuit of size at most 63 the outer formula, with H treated as an extra input, has at most 62−g raw leaves. Let F be the set of flags where H is true, and W the negative columns where H is true. The raw rectangles must cover each cell on a cold flag (outside F) or a warm column (in W). On any such cell H cannot be the reported difference. On other cells the raw rectangles may still be active but cannot overlap.

The hereditary bound on the cold rows gives 5|F| ≥ 4g + 12. If H accepts no singleton, every member of F has both ends within the at most g+1 variables used by its formula. The largest induced edge counts on two through eight vertices are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 9, respectively. This excludes g at most six.

For stronger bounds, retain the cold-or-warm cells as compulsory. For a local family, charge only the reported rows compulsory in the current column, and require it to cover every compulsory neighbor that is active. At a flag e keep the domination charge when each of the nine pairs of exterior witnesses has a column containing both exterior endpoints where both witness cells are compulsory. Then domination at both ends remains impossible, even if the middle cell itself is optional, because the two raw rectangles would overlap. At other flags drop the domination charge. Minimize 432k plus 216 times the eligible dominated count plus charged reports at each vertex. For four or more rectangles the four singleton masks suffice for this minimization. Subtract 216 per eligible edge and one per compulsory cell from the sum and divide by 432, rounding up. This yields a lower bound on the number of outer raw leaves. It also works using any certified subset of the warm columns.

For seven through nine inner gates, enumerate the dense input supports up to the 5,616 verified projective symmetries. For each possible F mark a set R of at most g+1 minus the support size that includes every repeated variable. Set R to zero. The remaining formula is read-once on its variables and accepts all flags of F avoiding R. Split its top ORs. In each resulting AND, a flag accepted on two variables must have one singleton accepted by each of its two children, since their supports are disjoint. Any connected component of the retained flags therefore forces the product of the disjunctions of its point and line vertices. This makes every quadrangle meeting both shores of that component warm. A top raw literal would accept a singleton, excluded here.

There are 19, 106 and 793 support/flag/repetition cases in the three gate counts. Those forced warm columns alone suffice in all but zero, two and four cases respectively. Each remaining case has exactly one repeated variable. The verifier enumerates both operations at all root splits on the exact multiset of leaves, retaining inclusion-minimal warm masks among expressions accepting the required flags. It may omit a root split with a single raw child when an OR would admit a singleton and an AND would miss a required flag. All residual masks exceed the raw budget. Thus a sole shared result with no accepted singleton needs at least ten gates to compute.

For a raw disjunction on n distinct inputs, g can be reduced to n−1, the warm columns contain at least one of those inputs, and the hot flags meet its support. At n=1 through 6 the numbers of support orbits are 2, 4, 10, 25, 56 and 128. The direct bound excludes all five-variable disjunctions. Any six raw singleton inputs force at least 61 rectangles on the warm columns alone. This excludes all larger raw disjunctions and, more generally, any sole shared formula accepting six different singleton inputs, since computing it needs at least five gates.

## A shared pair disjunction

A one-gate AND either accepts no flag or is zero on all quadrangles. The other one-gate possibility is H = u OR v. The four pair orbits have raw lower bounds 60, 60, 64 and 60 for two points, two lines, incident mixed and nonincident mixed pairs. With four or more uses at most 59 outer raw leaves would be available. With two uses, unfolding adds just one gate, so a 63-gate circuit would unfold to a minimum formula repeating the same raw pair; the equality case rules this out.

With three uses the unfolding has at most 66 leaves. For each vertex let its slack be 432k + 216d + h minus its local right side. The sum of all local slacks is at most 432, by the global count. Each of u and v has three helper leaf occurrences. They require at least one additional private occurrence apiece: a quadrangle containing the other variable and a neighbor of the vertex can force its report on that flag when the helper is unavailable. If one vertex has five or more leaves, its slack is at least 432. With exactly four leaves its sole private row mask must contain all rows forced on columns containing the other variable. The other three leaf rectangles are unavailable on those columns. Enumerating their four-bit masks gives minimum slacks of 165 at either member of a point pair, 144 at either member of a line pair, and 403 and 442 for the two ends of a nonincident mixed pair. Thus both vertices must have exactly four leaves, and the nonincident mixed pair is already excluded. The incident mixed pair already exceeded the outer raw budget.

Finally the two raw leaf rectangles of each helper occurrence share the same column set: the OR choice depends only on the flag row. The verifier aligns the three pairs of masks and enumerates their common active subset per column, allowing the private rectangle at either vertex to activate independently. All 96 point-pair alignments and 144 line-pair alignments within the separate slack budget fail the joint test. This excludes every one-gate shared result, with any number of uses.
