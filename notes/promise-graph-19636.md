# Exactly ten gates for the 5 by 5 promise graph

[L-meta-19636](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-19636) asks whether nine gates suffice for the graph mask 8130428. The evaluator receives five row indicators followed by five column indicators, exactly one of each set true. In increasing column order the target rows are `00111`, `11011`, `11000`, `00011` and `11100`. **Exactly ten gates are necessary and sufficient.** We constructed a ten-gate circuit, ruled out all nine-gate formulas and excluded every remaining nine-gate circuit by a finite search. A second calculation checked all 19 remaining cases by a different route.

Let A report column 0 or 1, and C report column 3 or 4. Write r0 through r4 for the row indicators. Set U = r4 OR A and V = r0 OR C. The output is

    (U OR V) AND NOT ((r2 OR U) AND (r3 OR V)).

Computing A and C costs two gates, U and V two more, and the displayed expression six more because it reuses U and V. Direct evaluation of the ten single gates reproduced all 25 posted entries. Row 1 and column 2 require no input pin of their own in this construction.

## Lower bound and exact search

The five row signatures are different. At most one row indicator can go unread, since two unread rows would look identical at a fixed column. The three column classes have sizes two, one and two, so at most two column indicators can go unread. At least seven distinct raw signals must therefore feed the output cone. A connected binary cone fed by seven distinct signals needs six binary gates, even with free unary gates.

We searched irredundant AND/OR/NOT circuits directly against all 25 assignments. A bounded synthesis run excluded sizes seven and eight. Six gates with seven raw inputs must be a read-once formula. A separate enumeration excluded formulas through size nine; the support-partitioned replay has also excluded each of the five feasible support representatives at size six. Only completed exclusions enter the proof below.

The [replay](promise-graph-19636.py) includes the ten-gate witness and a SAT formulation. Each gate chooses one operation and two preceding wires, with repeated selectors denoting the single effective input of NOT. Its value on each promise input is constrained by the gate truth table. AND and OR need two distinct operands. Unknown wires evaluate to false in the project evaluator. Missing inputs, invalid references, unary copies, repeated binary operands and constant wires can all be folded from a least circuit for this nonconstant target: constants propagate, neutral arguments disappear, and downstream uses and the output selector can be redirected. An irredundant circuit has every internal gate used and its output last under a topological ordering. A lexicographically least order by smaller input index cannot contain two adjacent independent gates with that index descending, since swapping them lowers the first changed index.

The replay groups possible raw-input supports under exchanges of the identical columns in each pair and the simultaneous exchanges of the outer column classes and corresponding rows. It checks each permutation against the actual target. A binary cone with s gates uses at most s+1 distinct raw inputs. These conditions determine the support representatives at each size. Each satisfying circuit is decoded and independently evaluated on the original truth table. Run `python promise-graph-19636.py` with the Python-SAT package installed, or select a single size and support with `--size` and `--case`.

## The remaining nine-gate cases

The public checker has separately established [a ten-gate upper bound with NOT](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-19446) and [the impossibility of even ten gates without NOT](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-10195) for the identical mask. We developed and tested the ten-gate construction above before consulting those verdicts.

An independent formula search rules out every nine-gate tree, even allowing the same raw signal at several leaves. Its smaller binary subtree has at most four gates. The replay enumerates every distinct small truth table by increasing formula cost. At an OR root the smaller operand must reject every required zero; the other operand must meet the remaining required ones and all required zeros. The AND rule is dual, and NOT exchanges the required ones and zeros. A branch doing no work for the partial requirement can be removed. This recursion accounts for highly unbalanced trees and can be repeated with `--formulas`.

Let r be the number of distinct raw inputs read by a hypothetical least nine-gate circuit. A live connected circuit with b binary gates and k unary NOTs has 2b+k input edges and r+b+k vertices. If b=r−1 the underlying graph is a tree, so the circuit is a formula. Thus b≥r. Together with r≥7, b+k=9 and the checked monotone exclusion, the only possibilities are r=7 with one or two NOTs, or r=8 with one NOT. These leave 19 combinations of NOT count and inequivalent raw-input support. All 19 combinations returned UNSAT. This calculation can be replayed with `--nine-cases --backend glucose4`. The broader searches were stopped after the focused proof completed.

## Independent check of the lower bound

For a minimal nonformula circuit define the number of extra uses as the sum of (outdegree minus one) over its nonoutput vertices, including the used raw inputs. Counting the 2b+k input edges gives **b−r+1**. A least circuit of size at most nine has at least one NOT by the public monotone theorem. Thus either it has exactly one extra use, or it has exactly two and necessarily uses seven raw inputs, eight binary gates and one NOT. More extra uses would exceed nine gates.

With one extra use, some internal wire H feeds two consumers. If the repeated vertex were raw, the circuit would already be a formula with a repeated raw leaf. The cone of H is a read-once tree on a subset S of the selected raw inputs. Its leaves have no other use. The remainder is a read-once tree over the other selected raws and two copies of H. The case S equal to the entire support cannot give a minimal nonformula circuit: without any other leaf the suffix is a unary Boolean function of H, hence a constant, H or its complement.

The [cut verifier](promise-graph-cut-19636.py) reconstructs all possibilities for H without using a SAT solver. Two promise rows with the same outside-S indicators and opposite target bits must have different H bits. Build a graph of S-input patterns with an edge for each such pair. Its proper two-colourings give all possible assignments to H; each connected component may flip independently. For each colouring test whether a read-once prefix can realize H, and whether a suffix with two copies of H realizes the target using the remaining NOT budget. The recursion allows NOT at every root, and at each binary root tries every leaf partition and both operations. The least NOT cost of a feasible prefix leaves the largest possible suffix budget.

Across all 14 eligible supports, this considered 1,150 colourings. There were 505 feasible prefix choices and no feasible complete circuit. A constructed positive control with one repeated internal NOT passed the prefix and suffix verifier. For the other five cases (seven raw inputs and one NOT), a second SAT run restricted selectors to those seven inputs and required exactly one NOT. This run used CaDiCaL and omitted the gate-order and truth-table pruning from the Glucose replay. Each case was UNSAT. Together these give an independent check covering every case in the 19-case run, including any smaller nonformula circuit with a single extra use.

The [desk claim](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-19636) asks for a nine-gate witness. Our ten-gate construction and exhaustive exclusions establish its negation. The size-nine exclusion is an external finite calculation. It has no Lean certificate or upstream filing from this contributor.
