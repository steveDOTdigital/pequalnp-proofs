# Rectangle covers of the order-five projective plane under diagonal symmetries

The 31 points and 31 lines have 775 nonincident pairs. A zero rectangle consists of a set of points and a set of lines with no incidence between them. L-meta-10008 asks whether every cover of the nonincident pairs by zero rectangles needs more than 17 rectangles. Its opposite is L-meta-10007. The general case remains open in this work. [Exact posted statement and matrix](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-10008.a5a3ea2794ed.json)

## Results

* The minimum for a cover invariant under the full diagonal projective group is **24**. Six seed rectangles in the replay generate the 24 rectangles. Their union agrees with the complement of the posted matrix on every one of its 961 entries.
* A cover invariant under any index-two subgroup of this diagonal group needs **at least 19** rectangles. Thus even a cover of size 18 would have to break each of these symmetries.
* For the elation sending homogeneous coordinates to (x+z,y,z), an invariant cover of size at most 17 must have exactly three moving rectangle orbits of size five, leaving room for at most two fixed rectangles.

## Exhaustion argument

Enlarge each rectangle by including every point avoiding its columns and then every line avoiding the enlarged row set. This produces a maximal zero rectangle, contains the original rectangle, and commutes with every incidence-preserving symmetry. Consequently any invariant cover can be replaced by an invariant set of maximal rectangles of no greater cardinality.

A maximal rectangle is determined by its columns, which are the intersection of the zero neighborhoods of its rows. Starting with all columns, intersect with each row neighborhood, repeating until no new intersections occur. Simultaneous cyclic shift preserves the posted matrix. Keeping one representative of each cyclic orbit during enumeration is therefore exhaustive. The replay finds 50,508 nonempty rectangle representatives and expands them to 1,565,748 distinct maximal rectangles.

For each tested symmetry group, partition the rectangles and the nonincident cells into orbits. Selecting a rectangle orbit costs its cardinality. It covers precisely the cell orbits that meet one representative rectangle. Options with identical cost and coverage can be merged. The full diagonal group, generated by multiplying the first or second homogeneous coordinate by 2 over the field of five elements, has 67 cell orbits. Squaring the second generator gives an index-two subgroup with 117 cell orbits. The three index-two subgroups are conjugate under coordinate permutations since they test whether the ratio of one of the three coordinate pairs is a square.

The exact search fixes the number of largest orbits, enumerates those choices in increasing index order, and branches on every feasible remaining option covering a chosen uncovered cell orbit. It prunes when an upper bound on coverable weight falls below the uncovered weight. For each nonnegative integer weighting and orbit cost, the replay computes the maximum weight of any option of that cost directly from the complete catalogue. An unbounded knapsack over those maxima permits repeated options and ignores overlap, giving an upper bound. No floating-point optimizer or external certificate is needed. The search exhausts budgets 23 and 18 for the two diagonal cases, respectively.

For the elation, 100 cell orbits remain after taking the union of everything covered by fixed maximal rectangles. A moving orbit covers at most 50 of these cell orbits. Exactly 40 distinct orbit coverage masks attain 50, and no two together cover all 100. At least three moving orbits are necessary. Equivariant enlargement cannot increase the number of moving orbits, so this necessity also holds for covers by arbitrary rectangles.
