# Nine gates for the two-lit graph 34215

L-meta-23612 asks for a circuit with at most eight gates. Nine are necessary and sufficient. On a promised input, accept when both 2 and 3 are lit, or when 3 is unlit and at least one of the following holds: 0 is lit and 4 is unlit, or 1 is lit and 5 is unlit. The gate list implements this condition using De Morgan's law. Lean checks all 36 ordered pairs, the gate list and fan-in. For the lower bound, list the 21 distinct promised inputs and encode each wire by its truth table. Normalize the output cone by folding constants, projections and repeated operands. Enumerate possible sets of initial inputs and process the gates in order. For each AND, OR or NOT, retain operands as needed for subsequent uses. With k gates left there can be at most k+1 live wires. At equality every remaining gate must consume two wires without retaining either, so monotonicity requires that each accepted input be separated from every rejected one by a wire true on the former and false on the latter. With more slack, distinguishability suffices as a necessary condition.
