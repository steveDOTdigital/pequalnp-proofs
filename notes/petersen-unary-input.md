# Nineteen gates with a free complemented input on the Petersen graph

Independent computer-assisted proof of the mathematical statement in L-meta-1884, by steveDOTdigital. The target is the Boolean edge function of the ten-vertex Petersen graph. Its inputs are ten arbitrarily selected raw coordinates, with repetitions allowed, and any unary Boolean function of coordinate zero. Every AND, OR or NOT costs one gate, and gates read at most two wires. **Every such circuit needs at least 19 gates.** The known six-star construction costs 20. This result leaves the possibility of a 19-gate circuit open.

The [Lean reduction](PetersenUnaryInput.lean) proves the exact posted statement conditional on two finite circuit exclusions. The [Python driver](petersen-unary-profiles.py), [C++ prefix search](petersen-raw-prefix.cpp) and [restriction checks](petersen-profile-cuts.hpp) establish those exclusions computationally. This is a research-fork result. The Lean kernel has checked the transfer to the posted evaluator. The numerical exclusions still use the external implementation.

## The two finite problems

A signed binary gate computes the conjunction of any two earlier signals with independently chosen signs. Both signs of its result are available, and the output may be complemented. The signed budget counts binary gates only. The second model has only AND/OR gates, all raw positive literals, and both signs of one chosen raw variable. Giving either model both constants for free only helps a candidate.

Every supplied unary function is a constant, identity or complementation. Absorb all NOT gates and these unary pins into wire signs. A physical circuit of at most 18 gates using at least one NOT would give a signed circuit of at most 17 gates. In the absence of NOT, the second model contains the original circuit: relabel the ten raw pins and replace the unary pin with the appropriate canonical literal or constant. The Lean proof implements both transformations, including repeated operands, nullary and unary gates, arbitrary outputs and invalid indices. The theorem at the end of the file has the printed desk conclusion verbatim.

The [finite Lean model](PetersenUnaryBoundary.lean) proves that 38 labeled assignments suffice for the second model. With coordinate zero fixed to false, the list contains 12 minimal positive and 12 maximal negative inputs. With it fixed to true, the list contains nine minimal positives and five maximal negatives. Lean checks the covering relation on all 1,024 assignments and proves that every eligible physical circuit is monotone on each slice. It also checks the 13 packed input words and the target word 2,097,151.

The theorem `resultFromFiniteChecks` now accepts two purely finite claims. The signed claim uses at most 17 instructions and 1,024 truth-table bits per wire. Its selectors range from 0 to 56. The monotone claim uses at most 18 instructions and 38 bits per wire, with selectors from 0 to 31. The last selector in either model represents an invalid read returning false. The compilation covers empty, unary and binary gates. These definitions and transfers pass Lean's kernel. The two universal claims are still established by the external searches. A separate direct SAT encoding of the 38-row claim had 69,883 clauses and returned unknown after 120 seconds.

## Direct inputs and restrictions

Work in a smallest live circuit and remove constants, unary copies and repeated operands. Every raw coordinate is essential with any other one fixed at either value; the driver checks this on the whole truth table. The two operands of a raw-pair gate must therefore each have another direct reader. Fixing its companion to its controlling value would otherwise erase all dependence on that operand. The previously established [signed bounds on the vertex deletions](petersen-raw-bound.md), [six- and seven-vertex certificates](petersen-signed-deletion.md) and [ten-vertex bounds](petersen-free-or.md) exclude the smaller budgets needed to start the induction. For monotone induced graphs with at most seven vertices use the [edge-plus-cover calculation](seven-vertex-cover.md); the two eight-vertex types are checked in the nine-vertex work.

If fixing a raw variable removes \(r\) direct readers, its cofactor needs at most \(s-r\) gates. More generally, pin an arbitrary set of variables so the restricted output is nonconstant. One gate is saved per pinned raw **operand occurrence**, including when two pins meet at one gate. To verify this, split each pinned occurrence into a distinct constant source in the live graph. A binary output cone on \(s\) gates and \(a\) distinct sources has undirected cycle rank \(s-a+1\). Constant propagation deletes unused vertices and contracts copies or literals. These operations cannot increase cycle rank. Every split source disappears, so the gate decrease pays for all of them. Negations can be absorbed on a signed restriction. In the one-complement model the same contraction operates on physical uncomplemented gate outputs and any available raw literals.

Sum the direct raw read counts \(r_v\). The total excess fanout of non-output internal gates is \(s+1-\sum_v r_v\). A formula is already too large in each search, by the [signed formula lower bounds](petersen-free-or.md#formula-consequence). This leaves \(\sum_v r_v\le s\). The zero-pin bounds give at most three raw reads per variable in these computations. At the ten-vertex budget of 18 in the one-complement model, every zero deletion costs at least 16; hence each raw input has only one or two readers.

For a minimum circuit, among topological listings minimize the sum of operand indices. A reader can be chosen not to combine a gate with one of that gate's own operands. In the signed basis their composition is constant, a literal, or a single signed gate on the earlier pair. For positive AND/OR, absorption or associativity gives the same normalization. If the repeated operand is the exceptional raw variable \(z\), its two physical occurrences can have opposite polarities. For example \(z\vee(\neg z\wedge b)=z\vee b\); the dual identity and the cases with equal operations give the full list. Thus a direct reader forced constant by a pin loses a consumer in addition to the direct readers. Two forced readers lose at least two more gates if both are present: otherwise their only other consumer must combine the two forced signals. They factor through their common controlling literal, or have complementary controlling literals and collapse by the same elementary identities. Either case contradicts minimality. The direct readers can depend on other earlier raw readers through longer paths; eliminating those dependencies after pinning can only increase the savings.

The [prefix checker](petersen-raw-prefix.cpp) fixes the raw multiplicities, the graph orbit and the first gate. All 120 automorphisms are established on the displayed edge list. It considers every ordering of the remaining gates needed by sorting ready gates by their latest operand and then their other operand. Choosing the first gate as a seed restarts that ordering for its suffix. The driver covers the orbits of the raw count vectors and then the first gates under each vector's stabilizer. The low-pin budgets test every vertex-induced graph. A high pin yields the OR of its neighbors and the edge function on its remaining nonneighbors. Setting the independent singleton alarms to zero costs a gate per alarm whenever an edge remains.

For each induced nonconstant restriction, the prefix records removed gates, future pinned raw occurrences and as-yet unused prefix outputs that are constant after pinning. These unused constants must be consumed by the suffix. Replacing a restricted gate by a constant or earlier equal wire eliminates its gate; in the signed basis equality up to complementation suffices. With one raw complement both physical signs of any function that depends on another unpinned variable cannot simultaneously occur, since both would have to be increasing in that variable. Complementary duplicates on a subcube can thus be replaced by a constant or a literal of the exceptional variable. The program's parallel low-pin budget is the same accounting: consume a charge for each pinned raw operand or as-yet unused constant input, charge one for eliminating the current gate and one for its newly pending constant output, when applicable. It also checks that the available suffix sources can distinguish required pairs of truth rows within the remaining fanout budget.

When \(s=\sum_v r_v\), precisely one internal signal can be read twice. Its input cone is a formula. On an induced restriction, unfold that cone twice and add the remaining raw leaves outside it. If this upper bound on available formula leaves falls below the induced signed or monotone formula lower bound, the proposed shared wire cannot occur. A zero pin that turns the shared signal into a raw input or a constant also leaves just a formula. The earlier [leaf-count argument](cubic-formula-leaves.md) supplies the signed restriction estimate by charging three occurrences for each surviving variable used with both signs; known exact small circuit bounds also give formula lower bounds.

## Testing the exceptional polarity

A normalized wire is stored with value zero on the all-zero input. For each of the four signed two-input operations, the physical possibilities are the AND of the specified operands, or the OR of their complements with opposite output polarity. The Lean file checks this truth-table identity. At every wire the checker records, for every choice of exceptional \(z\), which physical output sign is available. Initially only raw \(z\) has both. By induction, two distinct operands of a new gate cannot both have both signs for the same \(z\). Thus the choice of physical sign is consistent across reuse. Even relaxing a sign choice would only admit extra candidates.

On a fixed \(z\) slice all remaining variables are monotone. The minimal positive and maximal negative assignments in both slices therefore suffice. If an earlier physical wire dominates a proposed one on these tests, replacing it eliminates a gate while preserving correctness. The same test applies after setting another vertex to zero when all permitted deletion savings are already owed by its direct readers. Direct readers themselves are exempt from that last comparison.

For the ten-vertex mixed case, the free complemented variable has two readers, one positive and one negative. Pinning \(z=0\) spends both permitted deletions. Neither reader can be forced constant there, so the positive occurrence enters an OR and the negative occurrence an AND. Whenever the target rises from zero to one on a pair differing only at \(z\), the **physical output of the sole positive reader must rise**. After cutting that wire, the remaining direct occurrence of \(z\) decreases, and the entire suffix is monotone in that occurrence and the cut wire. This rules out a raw companion for the positive reader: each other raw vertex occurs on some sensitive low row.

The companion of the negative AND must be true on some minimal positive with \(z=0\) and some maximal negative with \(z=1\). Replacing the negative literal by false at every input would delete its reader; this smaller monotone circuit must lose a positive row on the low slice. Replacing it by true would also delete the reader and must gain a false row on the high slice. On either witness the companion is true. For a reader which is an OR, the analogous neutral companion value is false.
