# Matching input uses to incident edges

For every finite simple graph, there is a minimum-size binary monotone circuit with the following property. Choose any accepting proof tree for each edge on the input consisting of its two endpoints. At each vertex, its direct input uses can be assigned to **distinct incident edges**, with the proof chosen for each assigned edge actually traversing that use. The same circuit works for all vertices and all choices of proof trees. In particular the fanout of each raw variable is at most its degree. An edge can receive a use from each of its two ends.

A stronger statement counts readers of internal signals concentrated on a star. For the four-cycle-free graphs of L-meta-3456, a counterexample requires a residual circuit with at least two shared internal signals and a raw input used both inside and outside the cone of the first shared signal. These restrictions still leave open whether all ANDs can be made false on singleton inputs.

## Separate finished stars

For a nonempty graph write its edge function as the OR of a residual circuit and a family of private stars. A star with center x and d assigned neighbors computes the AND of x and their disjunction, using d gates. Its assigned edges belong to no other extracted star. Require the residual to reject every extracted edge on its two-endpoint input. It must accept all remaining edges and reject every independent set of the original graph. It may accept a larger assignment containing an extracted edge. Raw variables are shared freely between the parts. The ORs joining the parts can be arranged in any order.

Start with no extracted stars. Among all minimum-size circuits and such decompositions, maximize the number of extracted edges. In a nonempty residual, call the remaining edge set U. Every vertex of U is essential to the residual since singletons are negative. No other raw variable need occur. Pinning all uses of such a variable to zero preserves every required positive and can only decrease the output. A live use would save a gate. A residual with no remaining edges can be deleted. Fold constants, repeated operands and unary gates first. The empty graph uses zero gates with the project’s out-of-range false output.

Choose any input slots in the residual and set just those occurrences to zero. Other occurrences of the same wire retain their usual values. Fold constants and discard dead gates. Let q be the number of gates saved and L the edges of U now rejected. Every distinct gate with a cut slot folds away, even when the same gate receives two cuts. If L is empty and q is positive, the original circuit was not minimum. Otherwise assign the edges of L to a minimum vertex cover of the graph on L. Its disjoint stars cost at most |L| + τ(L) gates including all their joins to a surviving residual. A zero residual saves one more join. The old protected edges stay rejected by the smaller residual, and it now rejects L as well. On every positive input, either an extracted star fires or an unlost edge survives by monotonicity. On every independent input both parts are zero.

Consequently **q is strictly less than |L| + τ(L)** when L is nonempty. If the residual disappears, q is strictly less than |L| + τ(L) − 1. Equality would increase the number of extracted edges at the existing minimum size. These comparisons concern the joint experiment. Two cuts can lose an edge that either cut alone preserves.

There is a sharper joint price when P is the set of edges already extracted. Its private stars use a minimum cover of P, since replacing them otherwise would reduce the circuit. Counting their joins to a live residual, they cost |P| + τ(P). Repartition all of P ∪ L at once. The increase in star gates, including joins to a surviving residual, is exactly

$$ |L| + \tau(P\cup L)-\tau(P). $$

If the residual vanishes, subtract one. These prices also hold starting with P empty. The difference of cover sizes can be smaller than τ(L). In particular a vertex x which occurs in **some** minimum cover of P can absorb a nonempty lost star at x at cost |L|. The previously chosen orientation of the stars is immaterial.

## A matching for every routing

Fix x. Call a residual signal local to x when all the edges of U on which it fires are incident to x. This definition tests only two-endpoint inputs. Pick any collection of distinct reader gates and designate at each one a slot fed by such a signal. This includes all direct readers of raw x. For any subset S of these gates, cut the designated slots. On an edge of U omitting x, each designated source was already zero. Induction through the circuit shows that every value stays the same on that input. Thus all lost edges lie in a single star. For a nonempty S the preceding budget gives

$$ |S| \le q \le |L(S)|. $$

Here and below the proof of an edge chooses one true child at each OR and both children at each AND. Gates with several uses may be unfolded in the proof. Fix one such proof for each edge. Every lost edge's proof used a selected slot, since a proof avoiding all selected slots still accepts. Join each designated reader to the incident edges whose chosen proofs traverse its slot. Every subset of readers has at least as many neighbors as its cardinality. Hall's theorem gives the injection. Conversely, for a fixed cut choose an avoiding proof for each surviving edge and any proof for each lost edge. For this routing the neighborhood of the cut is exactly L(S). Thus validity for **every** routing is equivalent to joint-loss expansion.

In the whole circuit an extracted edge can be accepted only by its own star on its pair input. The residual rejects it and the star sets are disjoint. A raw occurrence in an extracted star can therefore be assigned to its own edge if it is a neighbor, or to any one of that star's edges if it is the center. Those edges are unavailable to the residual matching. This yields the simultaneous statement for the complete circuit on both shores, including for nonbipartite graphs.

If x belongs to some minimum cover of P, every nonempty subset S satisfies |S| + 1 ≤ |L(S)|. The matching in the residual can avoid any one specified incident edge. In particular any vertex of uncovered degree one belongs to **no** minimum cover of P.

More generally choose an arbitrary slot at each of some distinct residual gates, without a locality requirement. Assign each uncovered edge an endpoint as center. For the fixed proofs let Γ(S) be the edges whose chosen proofs traverse one of the slots in S. A patch for L(S) uses at most one gate per lost edge and one additional gate per different assigned center. For every nonempty S, its size is therefore strictly less than |Γ(S)| plus the number of different centers of Γ(S). Hall assigns the gates distinct tokens, each either an edge whose proof traverses the slot or the assigned center of such an edge. One arbitrarily specified token can be reserved. This conclusion holds for every fixed edge orientation.

For a fixed routing the injection can depend on that routing. It need not assign a different edge on which each individual slot is indispensable. Two edge contexts with behaviors AND and OR of the same two slots satisfy the all-routings matching test, although only the AND context is individually lost by cutting either single slot.

## Consequences for a counterexample

* A cut at raw x also eliminates every downstream AND reached through ANDs only from a cut slot, and folds the next reader regardless of its operation. All of these distinct gates count toward q. If a set of x readers loses exactly as many edges as it contains readers, no additional gate can disappear.
* Cut all the uses of an internal signal H with r readers. This removes H as well as its r distinct readers. If its pivotal edges in this experiment lie in a star then there are at least r + 1 of them. In particular no internal residual gate can be true on at most one uncovered edge. For a root with at most one uncovered edge the whole residual can instead be replaced by the corresponding one-gate star or deleted. Thus there is no AND of two raw variables anywhere in a nonempty residual.
* A degree-one vertex of U has exactly one reader, an OR. If its unique reader were an AND with another input, any successor of this AND would count as another local reader at the vertex. A root AND of this kind would force all uncovered edges to contain the vertex, hence permit complete extraction. More generally any variable that reaches the rest only through an OR with another raw variable has its uncovered neighborhood contained in the other's. Rejection of both singletons forces the two inputs to be nonadjacent. Testing each edge at the first input and substituting the second proves containment.
* A shared residual gate must take both values on U and on the forbidden tests (original maximal independent sets together with the protected pair inputs). For a gate that is always zero on U or always one on the forbidden tests, substitute that same constant and fold. If H is zero on forbidden tests, pin its reads to zero and reattach H by one OR. If H is one on U, pin its reads to one and reattach H by one AND. Each operation preserves separation on the two test families. Two or more distinct readers pay for the one join with a strict size saving. Full input correctness after joining the protected stars follows by monotonicity.
* Suppose the residual has an internal shared gate, and take the first in a topological order. Its proper internal ancestors are private. Some raw variable of this cone must have another read outside the cone. Otherwise the cone's k distinct raw variables reach the rest only through its root H. If H rejects their singletons, no uncovered edge crosses the cone boundary. Each cone variable is essential on an uncovered edge, so an internal edge makes the residual accept with every outside variable zero. The outside circuit with H forced to one must then accept even on the empty outside assignment. By monotonicity H implies the residual, and it can be lifted out of its two or more readers by one OR, preserving the entire function at a strict saving. If H accepts some singleton, the outside circuit must reject at H = 1 with all other inputs zero. Thus no uncovered edge is internal. Each cone variable belongs to a crossing uncovered edge, so H must accept each of their singletons and is their disjunction. All k variables have identical nonempty uncovered neighborhoods. A nontrivial cone with k = 1 computes the raw variable and contracts. For k ≥ 2, four-cycle-freeness leaves just one common neighbor. Cutting all readers and dropping the cone saves at least k − 1 + 2 gates and loses only the k edges at that neighbor. This affords their extraction. Both alternatives contradict the chosen decomposition.

Let n count active residual variables, m the residual edges and g the residual gates. An output-connected binary circuit has total excess reads g − n + 1 when each live wire's first read is free and the output designation counts as a read. At most one shared internal wire permits a size-preserving singleton cleanup of the entire residual truth table by the [private-OR factoring argument](singleton-frontier.md). The joining ORs and all extracted stars are already clean. In a counterexample there must therefore be at least two shared internal wires. The escaping raw read just proved adds a third unit of excess, giving **g ≥ n + 2**. Repartitioning all stars on the entire edge set E would require m + τ(E) − τ(P) − 1 additional gates in place of the residual. Extremality gives **g ≤ m + τ(E) − τ(P) − 2**. In particular m + τ(U) ≥ n + 4. These inequalities concern the unprotected graph and residual of a counterexample.

## Two size-preserving normalization criteria

For any **nonconstant** monotone Boolean function, a circuit with at most two distinct multiply read wires, counting raw inputs as well as internal gates, admits a singleton-clean circuit of the same or smaller size. Fanout at either of the two wires can be arbitrarily large. In particular any circuit with at most n + 1 gates for a function depending essentially on n variables can be cleaned within its budget. Fold constant gates, pin inessential variables to zero and trim to the live output cone. Its total excess reads are at most two.

Apply the full-truth private-OR factoring argument to the first dirty AND. If neither operand can be factored at a singleton x, that argument gives two distinct shared ORs A and B firing at x and incomparable by full implication. Since earlier ANDs reject singletons, both A and B are reached from raw x along paths consisting only of OR gates. Neither can lie on such a path to the other, since that would give implication. Two paths from x to A and B must therefore diverge at a third wire, distinct from either terminal, and that wire has at least two reads. This contradicts the two-wire hypothesis. A successful factoring step retains at most the original number of multiply read wires, including raw ones. Each terminal occurrence in the two private OR trees is retained at most once. The sole replacement root inherits the old AND's readers. If the root aliases a private terminal and makes it shared, the removed AND itself had multiple readers. All newly made intermediate nodes are private. Factoring preserves the entire output and strictly decreases total singleton hits at ANDs, so it terminates. On the seven-variable example with H = a OR b, X = (H OR c) AND (H OR d), and output (X AND f) OR ((X AND g) AND (H OR e)), the two repeated wires have three and two reads. Full-truth factoring removes both singleton hits in one step, retaining nine gates. All 128 assignments agree.

There is also a criterion allowing arbitrarily many shared gates. Suppose all but at most one shared internal wire are constant on the positive edge tests or on the negative maximal-independent-set tests. Then a singleton-clean circuit of no greater size exists. Apply the [ordered constant substitution and reconstruction](quadrangle-partition.md#eliminating-class-constant-shared-gates) to all the class-constant shared wires, retaining the exceptional wire with all its original reads. The same proof applies because the evaluation at the first decisive certificate, or at the residual output if none decides, is unchanged on the remaining tests. Every cut shared wire supplies at least two input slots. At most two slots belong to one reader gate, so folding saves at least as many gates as the priority joins require. After cutting the designated reads only the exceptional internal wire can still have multiple readers. Each residual root has at most one new use in the priority construction. Folding aliases can transfer the fanout of a removed shared gate to a single other wire. Thus the reconstructed circuit has at most one shared internal wire, and full-truth factoring cleans it. Boundary agreement and monotonicity give the full graph function.

## Verification

The [Lean proof](StarPortPacking.lean) checks the multi-reader gate saving against the project's actual constant-folding compiler, including the semantics of the compiled tape. It also checks preservation when extending an extraction, the AND/OR/slot proof recurrences, the star budget implication, Hall's assignment, the equivalence with joint-loss expansion, the semantic alternatives for a one-exit block, and the residual arithmetic. The extremal decomposition, its circuit rewiring and the four-cycle-free application to the first shared cone are mathematical proofs in prose.

The [Python checks](star-port-packing.py) construct the cuts and corrections, compare full truth tables after every step and independently enumerate proof routings. An eight-gate matching circuit with a dirty shared fork reduces to its three-gate pair of stars. Replacing two of its variables by disjunctions produces two three-vertex paths, which reduce from ten gates to five. Both examples use a step at exactly the available budget. On a four-vertex path, reorienting a previously extracted edge lets one saved gate pay for an additional edge that would cost two with the original orientation. Run `python3 notes/star-port-packing.py` at the repository root and `lake env lean ../notes/StarPortPacking.lean` from the Lean directory.
