# Nine gates for the four-by-five matrix 40439

Column 0 is accepted in every row. Columns 1 and 2 are accepted in rows 0, 1 and 2. The two remaining accepted cells are row 0, column 4 and row 1, column 3. The desk asks whether eight binary AND/OR gates suffice. [Posted claim](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-21033.2a37eaea1779.json)

A nine-gate circuit computes `c₀ ∨ ((r₀ ∨ r₁ ∨ r₂) ∧ (c₁ ∨ c₂)) ∨ ((c₃ ∨ r₀) ∧ (c₄ ∨ r₁))`. In the final product, the promise makes both `c₃ ∧ c₄` and `r₀ ∧ r₁` false. This leaves exactly the two exceptional cells. Evaluation checked all twenty inputs.

For the lower bound, each live wire carries a twenty-bit truth table. To obtain one output with at most `b` remaining binary gates, at most `b+1` such wires can still be used. Monotonicity also requires that every accepting and rejecting input pair be distinguished by a live wire that is true on the accepting input and false on the rejecting input. A standalone search retained 2, 39, 139, 182, 88, 15 and 0 viable states with eight through two gates remaining. The Lean certificate includes redundant gates and reached 40 states with seven gates remaining, then the same empty final layer. For each accepting input, it intersects the truth tables of the wires that are true there to check the separation condition.
