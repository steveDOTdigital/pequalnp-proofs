# Shared signals in least circuits for edge functions

For [L-meta-5012](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-5012), call an AND dirty if it can turn on without turning on the output. The desk assumes that no least circuit in the specified graph class has exactly one dirty AND and asks for the corresponding exclusion of exactly two.

**Result.** In a least binary monotone circuit for a simple graph's edge function, every AND is below the output if the circuit reuses at most one distinct wire. That wire may be raw or internal and may have any number of readers. More generally, the same conclusion holds if every biconnected block of the undirected wiring graph contains at most one wire with multiple outgoing uses in that block. In particular it holds for cactus wiring, where every edge is on at most one undirected cycle. These are conditions on the circuit, distinct from the desk's no-four-cycle condition on the graph defining the function. The result holds for all simple graphs, independently of the desk's one-AND premise.

The proof uses the slightly larger class of nonconstant monotone functions whose minimal true input sets, or minterms, have size at most two. This permits singleton minterms when contracting subcircuits. Every input used by a least circuit must be essential to the output. An inessential input can be set to zero and simplified away, removing a reader. Every gate reaches the output; unused gates can be deleted. An optimum also has no gate using the same operand twice. All simplifications below use the ordinary identities for binary AND and OR. When a pin creates a constant, propagate it before forming the smaller circuit. The nonconstant output keeps the resulting circuit in the model without constant gates. A constant-false graph function has a zero-gate circuit with an out-of-range output in the desk model.

## A formula with one repeated raw input

Write z for the repeated input. Every other variable appears once. Pin z to b, either zero or one. The private variables in distinct sibling subtrees are disjoint. A node is observable at this pin if its output could affect the root with the sibling subtrees fixed suitably. This requires every AND sibling on the path to be capable of one and every OR sibling to be capable of zero; disjoint private variables make the conditions sufficient.

At an observable node take any local minterm Q of the pinned subtree. Choose one minterm from each AND sibling on its path, and take their union W, permitting the empty minterm for a constant-one sibling. Set all other private inputs to zero. The disjoint supports and the zero values of all OR siblings show that Q together with W is a minterm of the pinned output. Removing any chosen variable breaks the unique path or a required sibling. Every such Q can be combined with every such W. Both pinned outputs have minterms of size at most two. Moreover, a *two*-element minterm at pin one is an actual minterm of the original function on those two private variables: a minterm using z could leave at most one variable after pinning and would contradict minimality of the pair.

An AND in a z-free subtree has minterms of size at least two, since its two operands have disjoint variables and are zero on the empty set. If it is observable at either pin, every local minterm already has size two and is a true input of the original function. At pin zero this follows directly and at pin one it follows from the pair observation. If the AND is unobservable at both pins, bypass it to one operand and save a gate. Thus a z-free AND in a least circuit is clean.

Now let g be an AND with an occurrence of z below it. In a least circuit it is observable at *both* pins. If g were unobservable at one pin, choose a direct parent of a z occurrence in its subtree. Replace this gate by the other operand if the opposite pin makes z neutral, or by z if it makes z controlling. At that opposite pin its value is unchanged. At the unobservable pin no value of g affects the output. This saves at least one gate.

If g at pin zero can be true, a local minterm uses a private variable from each operand. Any strict AND ancestor would need a further private variable at pin zero, since all wires are zero on the empty input. The extension would have at least three variables. Therefore all ancestors of g are ORs, and g is clean at both pins.

It remains to treat g that is zero throughout pin zero. If g is dirty, it must have a pin-one minterm Q on which the pin-one output is false. If g is constant one at pin one, g equals z and can be erased. Otherwise all local pin-one minterms are nonempty. Every path extension W described above must also be nonempty, since Q alone is false for the output. Applying the extension argument to *every* local minterm and every W now shows that each such union is a pair, and is an actual edge minterm even with z set to zero.

Replace g by its own pin-one cofactor, simplifying at least one parent of z in its private subtree. This agrees at pin one. If an input at pin zero newly made the output true, the replacement would be true there and every AND sibling on the path would already be true. Choose a local cofactor minterm contained in that input. Each AND sibling, true at zero, remains true at one, so choose a pin-one sibling minterm also contained in the same private inputs. Their union is one of the actual edge minterms just proved. The original output was therefore already true at zero. This contradiction proves the replacement preserves the output, violating leastness. No dirty AND is possible.

For a formula with no repeated variables the argument is shorter: any AND minterm needs two variables and extends by disjoint variables to an output minterm. It already has size two, so the AND is below the output.

## Contracting a private subcircuit

Suppose a subcircuit computes a nonconstant signal s on variables V. All uses of those variables and all uses of internal gates stay inside the subcircuit, except for readers of its single output s. The external circuit therefore depends on V only through s. Both the subcircuit and the circuit obtained by replacing it by a fresh raw input are least for their respective functions: any cheaper realization can be substituted into the original.

If there is a minterm wholly inside V, setting all external variables to zero makes the output equal s. There can be no crossing minterm consisting of v in V and u outside. On external input u alone the dependence on V would have to be s again, since that input alone is false and the pair is true. But s is false on v alone by minimality of the crossing pair. Thus all minterms are either inside or outside V. The inside function is s, and the quotient target is the disjunction of the fresh input and the outside minterms. Any AND below s is below the original output.

Otherwise every variable in V participates in a crossing pair. Fix an outside neighbor u. With u alone on the outside the function of V is the OR of the neighbors of u in V, and must equal s. Every variable in V is essential, so all of V occurs in this OR. The same holds for every outside neighbor. The quotient target again has minterms of size at most two, with one fresh vertex representing V. A least circuit for OR(V) uses only ORs: it takes at least |V|-1 gates merely to connect its essential inputs to its output; equality gives a read-once formula, in which an AND minterm cannot extend to a singleton. In both cases a clean AND in the quotient lifts to a clean AND before contraction.

If the only reused signal is an internal wire, everything below it is a private read-once formula. Contract this formula and apply the repeated-raw-input result to the quotient. This proves the first assertion. A simple numerical consequence is that a counterexample on n essential inputs needs at least n+1 gates. With g gates there are 2g incoming operand occurrences, and n+g-1 nonoutput wires each need at least one use. At g at most n the total number of excess uses is at most one.

## Multiple wiring blocks

Forget directions and consider a biconnected block containing a cycle. The block has a unique exit e on the side of the circuit output: if the output belongs to the block, e is the output itself. Otherwise the component towards the output attaches at e. A path leaving a block cannot return at a different vertex, by maximality of the block. Every other vertex in the block therefore has a directed path inside it to e. The exit cannot have an outgoing edge inside that block, since the circuit is acyclic. Its undirected internal degree is at least two, so its two operand positions are both occupied by block edges.

For a nonoutput exit, the full ancestor cone of e is a proper private subcircuit. Its ancestors come from the block or components attached on its interior side. Every path from those ancestors to the output passes e. The output-side component cannot supply a separate operand to e, since both positions are occupied. Distinct cyclic blocks cannot both have the output as exit, for the same two-operand reason.

If there is more than one cyclic block, contract a nonoutput exit and use induction on the gate count for its cone and its quotient. Both wiring graphs are subgraphs of the original, so the block condition persists, and the private-subcircuit argument transfers cleanliness. If there is at most one cyclic block, every shared wire belongs to it: two distinct outgoing uses trace to the common output and give a cycle through those uses by taking paths up to their first meeting. The block condition then leaves at most one shared wire in the entire circuit. On a simple-cycle block the orientation towards its unique exit has exactly one vertex with two outgoing cycle edges, giving the cactus consequence.

The [finite replay](shared-wire-5012.py) independently compared 1,696 labeled graph and occurrence-count combinations on three through six essential inputs. In all 1,188 combinations admitting a dirty formula, an explicit star construction used fewer gates. It also enumerated 1,863 four-input formula trees with minterms of size at most two and one designated variable used one to three times. All 318 dirty trees admitted a strictly smaller, truth-table-equivalent edit. These checks support the structural proof; they do not cover unrestricted shared circuits.

The full rung-two implication remains open here. A least circuit witnessing its failure must contain a wiring block with at least two distinct branching signals.

## Using the one-AND hypothesis

Assume the desk's first rung and suppose a least circuit contains exactly two dirty ANDs. On an independent input, a trace witnessing a true dirty gate uses no clean AND. Choose a single true operand at each OR and both operands at each AND, keeping each traced wire only once. This connected witness graph has at most one more distinct source than binary branching gates, by counting edges. Thus the earlier dirty AND has an independent witness on at most two vertices; the later one has such a witness on at most three.

Two nonisolated vertices with identical open neighborhoods are nonadjacent twins. Remove one twin v while retaining another u. An optimum for the smaller graph extends to the original with just one gate, by feeding OR(u,v) wherever u was read. Conversely, pinning v low in any original optimum removes at least one gate, and the smaller graph still has an edge. The optimum drops by exactly one. The pin removes precisely one OR directly reading v. Multiple readers would remove multiple gates; an AND reader would turn false and force another simplification, since it cannot be the output of a circuit whose low cofactor is nonconstant. All other gates retain their values on the restricted rows.

Keep a fixed two-vertex-or-smaller independent witness for the first dirty AND. In each twin class keep one representative and the vertices of this witness. Remove the other twins one at a time. The first dirty AND remains dirty, and the first-rung hypothesis applied to each smaller optimum forces the second to remain dirty too. Clean gates stay clean. In the end **all twin classes are singletons except possibly one pair**. This applies with any number of clean AND gates and without restrictions on circuit sharing. In a four-cycle-free bipartite graph all nontrivial twin classes consist of leaves at the same center.

### Two dirty ANDs require at least two clean ANDs

A nonempty graph output must have a clean AND below its final ORs: an OR below the output has both operands below the output, and no raw input is below a graph function. If there is exactly one clean AND, this descent uses only that gate at its leaves. Thus the output equals that gate, which must itself be the output in a least circuit. Its live circuit now has exactly three ANDs: the two dirty gates and this final clean AND. Everything else is an OR.

The [five-clause argument](boundary-stars-4297.md#one-boundary-gate-and-two-unsafe-ands) applies directly. There is no additional condition on the ORs here: the single clean AND is already the output. For clarity, name the dirty gates H and J in circuit order. H is the intersection of two raw disjunctions. J has two such operands, each optionally ORed with H. Each final operand is a raw disjunction optionally ORed with H or J. An unsafe gate cannot be an OR disjunct of both final operands. When H and J share a final side, absorbing H outside J allows J's internal H disjuncts to be set false, giving at most four clauses there and one on the other side. When they lie on opposite sides, at most one of J's operands can contain H, since otherwise the final AND would be at least H. Those two sides use at most three and two clauses respectively. Omitting an occurrence does not exceed these bounds.

Consequently the graph has at most five maximal independent sets. The complement of a clause is a false input, and every maximal false input equals one of these complements. Different neighborhood unions on either shore give different maximal independent sets, by taking the opposite-shore complement of a union and all vertices on the first shore whose neighborhoods are contained in that union. Including the empty union, there can be at most four distinct nonempty neighborhoods on either shore.

Now apply twin reduction. Collapsing the single possible duplicate leaves two shores of size at most four. Deleting a duplicate cannot merge other distinct neighborhoods on the opposite shore, because the representative has exactly the same neighbors. The [finite replay](two-dirty-three-ands-5012.cjs) enumerates distinct nonempty rows and columns on these shores, checks four-cycle-freeness and the union count, and restores at most one duplicate leaf on either shore. Up to shore exchange and vertex permutations there are 14 candidates. The usual edge-star construction costs m plus the minimum vertex-cover size minus one. Eight candidates have this upper bound at most the number of vertices and are already excluded by the shared-wire theorem. Only six remain: two six-vertex trees with budget seven, the six-cycle with budget eight, and three seven-vertex trees with budget eight.

For those six graphs the program searches every budget above the shared-wire bound up to the star upper bound, with precisely three ANDs and the output an AND. To cover every DAG, retain one outgoing use of each nonoutput wire and cut its remaining uses into reference leaves. The retained uses form a rooted binary tree with g internal gates, n raw leaves and g+1-n reference leaves. The search tries every unordered full binary tree shape, every placement of reference leaves, and each possible reference destination except the output. It permits forward references and checks acyclicity after reconnection. All raw labelings of each target are represented. Duplicate operands and gates combining a wire with a direct operand of that wire can be bypassed in every AND/OR case, hence cannot occur in a least circuit. After testing all placements of the first two ANDs, singleton and two-vertex rows identify candidate targets. All independent rows then check the output and mark exactly which ANDs can fire there. Monotonicity supplies the remaining positive rows.

### What the last dirty AND must contribute

Fix the later dirty AND h and force its output to zero, propagating constants. Keep earlier gates, including the first dirty AND, even if a later reader disappears. Let q be the number of gates removed and L the edges whose two-endpoint inputs now fail. The reduced function is at most the original. Restore all lost edges by stars centered at a minimum vertex cover of L, then OR this with the residual output. This costs at most |L| plus the cover size of L when L is nonempty, one gate less if the residual output vanished. On all independent inputs the new stars are false and the surviving old gate values only decreased. The first dirty AND still has its independent witness; the later one has been erased. The resulting circuit computes exactly the original graph and has just one dirty AND. If restoration uses at most q gates, then it either beats the original optimum or attains it with one dirty AND, both impossible under the premise. If L is empty the cut alone beats the optimum.

In particular, with r distinct readers of h, q is at least r+1 and |L| plus its cover size is at least r+2. The later dirty AND must be indispensable on at least two distinct edges. Another useful test is to replace h at its original position by one clean gate covering L. On independent inputs this replacement is false, and on edge inputs it preserves each indispensable edge. It therefore yields a same-size circuit with precisely one dirty AND. Under the first rung no such gate on available earlier wires can exist.

The remaining desk case needs at least two clean ANDs as well as the two dirty ANDs, and a wiring block with at least two branching signals. None of these necessary conditions alone decides that case.

## Restrictions that preserve optimality

Let S be a set of vertices to turn off, with at least one graph edge surviving. Let L be the edges incident to S, let e be their number, and let t be the minimum vertex-cover size of L. Suppose e is positive. Propagate zero through the original circuit, replacing an OR with one zero operand by the other operand, erasing ANDs with a zero operand, and keeping all other gates even if some might subsequently be redundant. Write d for the number removed.

An optimum for the induced graph on the remaining vertices can be extended to the original graph at a cost of e+t gates by adjoining stars for L and joining their disjunction to the residual output. If d is at least e+t, leastness forces equality and the restricted circuit is itself least. A removed AND cannot turn on when S is zero. A surviving AND takes its original value on every restricted input. Thus the dirty gates in the restricted circuit are precisely the original dirty gates with an independent witness avoiding S. By the first rung they cannot number exactly one. For every such S, either both dirty gates have independent witnesses disjoint from S or neither does.

For a single vertex v of degree k, the restoration price is k+1. If v has r raw readers then d is at least r. If any direct reader is an AND, its output is at most v. A consumer combining that output with v would be redundant in a least circuit, whatever its operation. The AND reader cannot be the output, since an edge survives deletion of v. It therefore has a reader distinct from all r direct readers, which is also removed by zero propagation. Hence d is at least r+1 in this case.

It follows that r cannot exceed k+1. When r=k+1, all direct readers must be ORs and the restricted circuit is least. When r=k and a direct reader is an AND, the restriction is again least. In either case, the first rung forces v to belong to every independent witness of one dirty gate exactly when it belongs to every independent witness of the other. In particular two uses of a pendant input with a surviving edge elsewhere must both be ORs, and the witness test applies. This local test is valid in circuits with any number of clean ANDs.

## Bounded vertex covers

Every least circuit for a graph in the desk class with vertex-cover size at most two is clean. A cycle would have length at least six and would itself need a cover of size at least three. After isolations are discarded, a nonempty forest on n vertices and c components has n-c edges. Its star-cover upper bound is at most n-c+2-1, hence at most n. The shared-wire theorem applies regardless of the number of AND gates.

For a vertex cover with k members, kL on the left and kR on the right, twin reduction gives a finite bound without restricting the gate types. Outside the cover all neighbors lie in the cover. Degree-one vertices contribute at most k representatives and one extra witness vertex. Each outside vertex of degree at least two uses a pair of cover vertices on one shore; no pair can occur twice. Thus there are at most 2k+binom(kL,2)+binom(kR,2)+1 active vertices. A vertex of degree d at least two contributes at most twice the number of its cover pairs to the edge count, and there are at most kL times kR edges inside the cover. This gives at most k squared minus kL times kR plus one edges. In particular, at cover size three a counterexample under the first rung would give one on at most ten active vertices and at most twelve gates. This final finite search has not been completed.

On **at most six active vertices**, the exclusion of exactly two dirty ANDs is unconditional. Cover size at most two was settled above. Otherwise the smaller shore must have at least three vertices since that shore is itself a cover, so at six vertices the shores both have size three. The replay enumerates all 512 labeled edge sets, removes four-cycles and isolates, and uses the star-cover upper bound for each. Beyond the n-gate lower threshold, the only budgets are seven and eight. Allowing every placement and number of AND gates, including an OR output, the search tests 2,840,409 type assignments at size seven and 318,406,290 at size eight. It finds 1,152 and 64 exact target matches respectively; none has two dirty ANDs. Circuits with fewer than three ANDs cannot have two dirty ANDs as at least one clean AND is necessary. This finite exclusion uses no first-rung hypothesis.
