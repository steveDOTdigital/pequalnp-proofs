# Nine gates for the three-by-five matrix 7541

The desk asks whether eight binary AND/OR gates suffice on the one-row, one-column promise. The accepted columns are {0, 2, 4} in row 0, {0, 1, 3} in row 1 and {0, 1, 2} in row 2. [Posted claim](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-20532.0c39395d0e48.json)

A nine-gate circuit uses two shared signals, `a = c₁ ∨ r₀` and `b = c₂ ∨ r₁`. Its output is `c₀ ∨ ((c₄ ∨ b) ∧ (c₃ ∨ a)) ∨ (r₂ ∧ (a ∨ b))`. Two gates form the shared signals and seven more combine them. The circuit agrees with the matrix on all fifteen inputs.

For the lower bound, encode each wire by its truth table on the fifteen promised inputs. With `b` binary gates remaining, at most `b+1` live wires can contribute to a single output. For every pair of an accepted input and a rejected input, at least one live wire must be true on the accepted input and false on the rejected input. Every binary AND or OR preserves this necessity. The independent search applied both conditions after each possible next gate and each possible reuse of its operands. It reached 1, 29, 130, 166, 59, 4 and 0 states with eight through two gates remaining. The Lean certificate checked redundant transitions as well, reaching 30 states with seven gates remaining and the same empty final layer. Lean verified both bounds against the project definitions.
