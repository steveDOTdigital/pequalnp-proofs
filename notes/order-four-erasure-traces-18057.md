# Finite gate-erasure traces for the order-four restriction

Independent work on L-meta-18057. The [desk catalogue](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/desk/gaps.json) gives the two zero masks and the required erasure counts. The [residual graph calculation](order-four-residuals-18057.md) gives the function left by each restriction.

## Gates with a pinned raw operand

A monotone gate of arity at most two directly reading a raw input pinned to false has the value of an earlier wire throughout the restriction. An AND can redirect to the pinned raw input. An OR with a second operand can redirect to that operand when it exists at evaluation time, and otherwise to the pinned raw input. A unary OR can redirect to the pinned raw input. The redirect is chosen from the gate alone and works on every restricted row.

The [Lean proof](OrderFourErasure18057.lean) checks this using the project's actual `eraseGate`. The simulator respects the default-false convention for nonexistent wires. Erasing a gate keeps its original size minus one and preserves the allowed gate type and arity. The same semantic proof allows a dead gate whose output is unused. For any marked subset of gates that read pinned inputs, erase the largest remaining original gate index first. The definitions and indices of the earlier marked gates remain unchanged. Lean proves the entire batch and converts it to the dependent `Fin` trace and `List.finRange` fold used by the posted conclusion. As a direct sufficient condition for the desk implication, it suffices to find the prescribed number of gates reading the pinned inputs.

## A uniform lower count

The [finite replay](order-four-star-erasures-18057.cjs) verifies that each pinned vertex has at least two neighbors outside the entire set of pins. In particular, each can be eliminated in any chosen order by at least one gate erasure. To see this, fix a pinned vertex v and a neighbor u that survives all pins. Before v is pinned, the inputs containing only u and containing both u and v both respect all earlier pins. Their outputs differ. The output wire cannot be v because a completely surviving edge also makes the output true with v false. Lean proves that an input whose toggling changes the output must have a direct reader in these conditions. Pin v and contract such a reader. Induction gives 13, 14 or 15 erasures, according to the number of pinned vertices in the posted case.

There is also a bound in terms of the original circuit's direct readers. A pinned vertex v whose raw input has a sole reader cannot have a second, distinct pinned raw operand w in that reader. If it is an AND, set w false and enable a surviving neighbor of v. If it is an OR, set w true and enable a surviving neighbor of v nonadjacent to w. Such a neighbor exists because there are at least two choices, and two distinct vertices on one shore have at most one common neighbor. When w is on the opposite shore it is automatically nonadjacent to the enabled neighbor. Either setting saturates the only reader while toggling v changes incidence. Thus each gate used by a sole-reader pin belongs to that pin alone. Every remaining pinned vertex contributes at least two distinct direct gate incidences, while each gate can receive at most two. This proves there are at least as many directly erasable gates in the original circuit as pinned vertices.

## Exact replay on the star control

The usual 125-gate point-star circuit gives a concrete trace of the full requested deletion length for every mask. The replay symbolically computes the antichains of minimal true input sets, collapses identical signals and removes dead gates using the exact erasure indexing rule. Its residual circuits have 58, 53 or 49 gates. The trace lengths are 67, 72 and 76 on the respective groups. Equality of the antichains proves equivalence on the entire restricted cube. The control uses 125 gates and the posted bound is 124. The general erasure count under the posted activation and cold-OR hypotheses remains open.
