# Nine gates for the three-by-six matrix 14124

The first row accepts columns 2, 3 and 5, the second accepts 2, 3 and 4, and the third accepts 0 and 1. The desk asks whether eight binary AND/OR gates suffice on the one-row, one-column promise. [Posted claim](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-23084.92be6317628b.json)

A nine-gate circuit shares the signal `s = r₂ ∨ c₂ ∨ c₃` between two terms. Its output is `((s ∨ c₄) ∧ (c₁ ∨ r₁)) ∨ ((s ∨ c₅) ∧ (c₀ ∨ r₀))`. The shared signal costs two gates, each term costs three, and their union costs one. Direct evaluation and Lean checked all eighteen inputs.

For the lower bound, a wire is identified by its eighteen-bit truth table. With `b` binary gates left, at most `b+1` wires can contribute to the output. On every pair consisting of an accepted and a rejected input, at least one live wire must be true on the first and false on the second. This follows from monotonicity. Starting from every possible subset of the input wires, apply those two constraints after every gate, including each way to reuse its operands. The surviving state counts with eight through two gates left are 1, 12, 34, 32, 10, 1 and 0. Lean checked the exhaustive lower bound against the project definitions.
