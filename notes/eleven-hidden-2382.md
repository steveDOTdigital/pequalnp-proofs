# Hidden edges of the eleven-vertex circuit in L-meta-2382

Write U for the graph edges absent from every top's raw cross pairs, k for their number, D for the number of junk ANDs, c for the number of centreless tops and j for the number of OR gates in orN outside Lambda. On the exact desk graph we prove **k ≤ D+j+2c** in each of the following cases:

* There is no mixing OR, with arbitrary D, j and c.
* There is at most one OR in orN outside Lambda.
* There is a single mixing OR and every top has raw inputs on both sides, with arbitrary j.
* There are at most six hidden edges and at least two junk ANDs.

The first result uses an exhaustive lower bound on a relaxed circuit over neighborhoods of the graph. The [replay](eleven-hidden-2382.py) uses the 16 positive pairs and 24 maximal independent sets of the posted graph. It does not search all circuits in L-meta-2382. The cases proved here do not need the upper bound of 21 gates or the additional premise assigning a junk witness to each hidden edge.

## Free sources supplied by the raw cross pairs

Trim the circuit to its output; leastness ensures all gates are live. A safe gate is one that rejects every independent set. A safe OR has only safe operands. No safe AND can feed another AND in top form, so safe ORs are used only on the way to the final output. All ANDs are either safe tops or junk. Below the final ORs we may take the union of the flags accepted by the tops.

For any noncentreless top its two raw input sets are disjoint and every cross pair is a graph edge. Otherwise a singleton or an independent pair would turn it on. There is no four-cycle, so one side consists of a single vertex. Each vertex on the other side is its neighbor on an edge **outside U**. For the same reason, every Lambda OR's raw input set is contained in such a covered neighborhood, because it lies in the OR closure of the appropriate top operand. The raw projection of a wire is its disjunction of inputs reachable by an OR-only path, stopping at ANDs.

For any chosen set S of hidden edges grant as free sources all raw literals and every OR of a subset of the neighbors of a vertex v on edges outside S. After pinning all vertices except endpoints of S to zero, keep the corresponding projections. This includes all raw projections of Lambda wires. Decreasing S or shrinking free source supports when restricting variables cannot invalidate a lower bound derived with this generous menu. In particular, in an actual circuit with hidden set U the available neighborhood ORs are among those granted in every test on S contained in U.

## Arbitrarily many nonmixing gates

With no mixing OR, a raw-bearing OR wire below the tops cannot have an AND ancestor on an OR-only path: the first OR at which a raw-empty contribution enters a raw-bearing wire would be mixing. Thus every Lambda OR is a pure raw source. Every noncentreless top is just an ordinary star and accepts no hidden edge. To separate the hidden edges from the independent negatives, retain the dirty ANDs, centreless tops and the j exceptional ORs, supply the Lambda and raw wires for free, and OR the c centreless outputs together. When k is positive, c must be positive. The resulting circuit uses at most D+j+2c−1 binary gates and separates U. It suffices to exclude a separator of k−2 gates in the relaxed model.

The replay checks all edge sets of sizes three through sixteen. The smaller sizes already satisfy the desk inequality because D ≥ 2, or directly because c ≥ 1 if there is a hidden edge in this case. For each edge family, a monotone separator using g gates can have at most g+1 different input sources in its live binary cone. On each positive/negative pair, some used source must be true on the positive and false on the negative. We test the necessary source count as a hitting set, branching on every wire in a remaining constraint. For example, at six edges just one family survives this first test. At fourteen, all 120 families survive it.

For each survivor, pin the other variables low and keep the maximal projected negatives. Enumerate each feasible initial set of at most k−1 source signatures. During binary synthesis choose two available wires and their AND or OR. After that gate, retain either operand if it is needed again. Discarding both after their last use is also allowed. With b further gates there can be at most b+1 live signals still requiring a path to the output. These choices include a topological evaluation of every live binary circuit. A duplicate gate value can be bypassed. A wire that separates no positive/negative pair can be set to zero if it is false on every positive, or to one if true on every negative, then simplified. Both simplifications preserve separation. A two-gate star is a positive control; the same target fails at one gate. The search excludes every remaining case. The survivor counts, in order for k = 3 through 16, are 0, 0, 0, 1, 11, 65, 274, 808, 1668, 1820, 560, 120, 16 and 1. The conclusion is a lower bound of k−1 paid gates on these free sources.

## What a conjunction of free raw sources can cover

A conjunction accepting prescribed pairs S can be shrunk pointwise by taking **every** free source that hits each pair. Any maximal negative that still passes this strongest conjunction rules out all such separators. Among pairs of distinct hidden edges only four can share a safe conjunction, even when tested in isolation. For two disjoint pairs the menu must exclude the union of their four edges. The replay finds no two disjoint pairs that remain feasible together. In particular, the union of t safe conjunctions covers at most t+1 hidden edges: assign every covered edge to one term, so the assigned groups are disjoint.

Grant also a common extra clause that is the OR of two free sources. No triple of hidden edges can lie in one safe conjunction even with this clause. To check it, the new clause must miss every negative that all ordinary clauses pass, and must hit each required edge if it is needed. Both its inputs must avoid the union of these negatives. There are 48 individually feasible pairs, 49 feasible unordered choices of two disjoint pairs, and no three disjoint pairs feasible with the same extra clause. This gives a union bound of t+2 when t safe conjunctions share one extra raw OR. It holds in a larger hidden family because the free menu used in a test on fewer hidden edges is a relaxation.

## One mixing gate

Let the unique mixing OR be M with nonempty raw side A and raw-empty operand J. All raw-bearing top operands have the form Q or Q OR J, with Q their raw projection. A raw-bearing wire can acquire an AND signal on an OR-only path only at M. A noncentreless top cannot receive M in both operands, since any literal of A would activate both. On hidden edges the union of the noncentreless corrections is consequently J AND G, where G is the union of the opposite raw guards over the affected tops. For any fixed raw vertex a reaching M, every guard literal is adjacent to a by a covered edge. Thus the entire G is one allowed free source. The product is safe on every independent set, since an active J and guard turn on the corresponding top.

When c = 0 this correction covers all U. The cone computing J precedes M and contains no mixing. It uses at most D+j−1 paid gates, with Lambda raw wires supplied free. It must separate U from the negatives that hit G. For every hidden family hit by a free guard, the replay enumerates its inclusion-minimal guards. It suffices to shrink each guard to its retained endpoints: this preserves all positives while weakening the negative tests. The number of minimal-guard cases for k from two through seven is 141, 193, 137, 55, 12 and 1; no larger family is eligible. In all 539 cases at least k source wires are needed by the positive/negative hitting test alone. Hence J requires at least k−1 gates, giving k ≤ D+j. An empty or single-edge hidden family satisfies the inequality directly.

For arbitrary c and j = 1, J is a conjunction of free raw sources. Clamp J to zero, then to one. In each branch every raw-bearing OR reduces to a free raw projection or a constant. The centreless tops become conjunctions of free sources or constants. Each original centreless top is the OR of its zero-branch value and J conjoined with its one-branch value, by monotonicity. Both terms are safe. Including the single noncentreless correction gives at most 2c+1 safe conjunctions and k ≤ 2c+2. This establishes the second stated result together with the no-mixing case.

## Excluding six or fewer hidden edges

With D ≥ 2 a failure at k ≤ 6 has either c ≥ 1 and j ≤ 1, already settled, or c = 0 and j ≤ 3. With c = 0 zero or one mixing gate has already been handled. We give the remaining short expansion to track the common free clauses.

For two mixing gates label their raw-empty operands Z₁ and Z₂ chronologically. Any noncentreless top expands, away from its ordinary raw cross pairs, into a token conjoined with an opposite raw guard, or a product Z₁Z₂ when the tokens reach opposite operands. The union of raw guards per mixing gate is one free source Gᵢ by the same neighborhood argument as above. With no other exceptional OR, Z₁ is a conjunction of free sources. Fixing Z₁ to zero or one makes Z₂ a conjunction of free sources or a constant: before the second mixer, the first becomes respectively its raw side or one. After setting Z₁ to zero, direct uses of Z₁ also simplify. The whole correction is covered by the following three safe conjunctions:

1. Z₁G₁.
2. Z₂[0]G₂.
3. Z₁Z₂[1] when an opposite-token product occurs, and Z₁Z₂[1]G₂ otherwise.

This follows by expanding Z₂ as Z₂[0] OR (Z₁ AND Z₂[1]). Thus k ≤ 4, proving the inequality for j = 2 when c = 0.

If a failure at k ≤ 6 has j = 3 and c = 0, it must have D = 2. With two mixers and one further raw-raw OR the same three conjunctions use at most one common extra raw clause. Its projection is an OR of two free sources: after cutting the two mixer contributions, every other raw-raw OR is Lambda. Clamping the earlier token high either leaves that projection alone or makes the wire one, depending on whether it receives the first mixer through ORs. The bound is k ≤ 5.

With two mixers and one raw-empty OR, this extra OR can only combine the two dirty signals H₁,H₂, in chronological order, or copy one. This is because there is no other raw-empty OR, and a safe signal cannot feed an AND. A duplicated input can be bypassed. If the sum H₁ OR H₂ never enters a mixer it cannot contribute to a noncentreless output. Otherwise any mixer receiving that sum is downstream of both dirty gates. If neither dirty gate is influenced by a prior mixer, both are conjunctions of free clauses; each of the two mixer/guard products contributes at most two safe conjunctions. An opposite-token product involving the sum is impossible: the other token contains at least one of its two dirty summands and would make the top unsafe. If H₂ is influenced by a mixer, the first mixer carries H₁ and the other carries the sum. With H₁ clamped, H₂[0] is a conjunction as before, and H₁ OR H₂ equals H₁ OR H₂[0]. Therefore at most three conjunctions are needed in this case. The base union bound gives k ≤ 5 in both cases.

Finally suppose all three exceptional ORs mix. Each carries H₁ or H₂. An opposite-token product, when present, gives the single safe term H₁H₂[1]. There is then at most one further term per mixer/guard pair, using H₁ or H₂[0]; thus k ≤ 5. Without a cross product, if H₂ is unaffected by a mixer both tokens are conjunctions. Otherwise at least one mixer carries H₁ before H₂. If at most one mixer carries H₂, expanding its guard term into two gives at most four conjunctions in total, again sufficient. In the remaining case one mixer carries H₁ and two carry H₂. Combine the last two free guards with a single extra raw OR, G. The correction is the union of H₁G₁, H₂[0]G, and H₁H₂[1]G. The three terms share only this extra raw clause beyond the free menu, so they cover at most five edges. This completes the six-edge exclusion.

A counterexample to the full desk claim would therefore need at least seven hidden edges, at least one mixing OR and at least two ORs outside Lambda. The calculations here do not exclude all such circuits.
