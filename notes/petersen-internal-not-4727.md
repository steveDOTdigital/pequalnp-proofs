# Nineteen Petersen gates with one to four NOT gates

[L-meta-4727](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-4727) asks whether a circuit with at most 19 gates and between one and four NOT gates can compute the Petersen edge function. Its opposite is [L-meta-4728](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-4728). The positive proposition is the lower bound. Our search compares all 1,024 input rows, using the [same 15 labeled edges](petersen-unary-input.md) as our 17-signed-gate exclusion. The catalogue gives statement hash `sha256:d8fea2ed197a6599de5fbeb4a88f12fb16067a1334adc8b5e9306341621a3f5f`.

The enumeration is in progress. In the first 675 cases, 655 finished on the first pass and two more finished on retry. The remaining cases still matter to the exact desk claim. This checkpoint adds zero to the proved total.

## Which circuit could survive?

Absorbing NOT gates into signs leaves at most 17 binary gates when two or more NOT gates were paid for. Our [signed exclusion](petersen-unary-input.md#the-two-finite-problems) rules out that entire case, including shared NOT outputs. One paid NOT with at most 17 binary gates fails for the same reason. With 18 binary gates, a NOT on a raw input is covered by the separate exclusion allowing both signs of any one raw input for free. A constant, unused gate, unary copy, or two equal or complementary operands in the remaining circuit would save at least one signed binary gate. An unused NOT leaves a monotone circuit of at most 18 gates, also excluded. These observations cover invalid references (the evaluator returns false) and an output chosen before the end of the gate list. The remaining possibility is an 18-binary-gate circuit with one actual intermediate H available in both polarities.

H is monotone in the raw inputs. Cutting its negation gives a suffix monotone in the raw inputs and the cut bit, including all shared earlier intermediates. If H is true only on positive assignments, pin its negation high. Every independent input stays unchanged and all positive inputs can only increase. If H is true on all 15 minimal positive rows, pin its negation low. The result still accepts every edge and rejects every independent set, and the specialized circuit is monotone. Either pin would yield the excluded monotone 18-gate circuit.

More generally, a substitute for the complemented H suffices if it is one on every edge missed by H and zero on every independent input accepted by H, provided the resulting circuit is monotone. In particular this excludes any earlier uncomplemented wire with those properties. For a raw *complement* substitute, the resulting circuit is monotone separately on its two raw slices, so the positive and negative row tests must instead use the minimal positives and maximal negatives on each slice.

A second restriction concerns any vertex v appearing in three direct operand positions. Pin v low. Its three readers disappear, and the nine-vertex restriction still needs at least 16 binary gates with both signs of one raw variable available. Hence H on this restriction depends on at least two variables, is true on an independent input and misses an edge. Indeed, a constant or literal reduces to the earlier nine-vertex exclusion, and either of the two pinning arguments above eliminates a safe or universally edge-active marker. The same conclusion applies whenever the zero restriction eliminates at least three binary gates by physical constant propagation.

## Enumerating the position of the inversion

The [replay](petersen-internal-not-4727.py) uses the independently developed [Petersen signed enumerator](petersen-raw-prefix.cpp), [restriction tests](petersen-profile-cuts.hpp) and [symmetry and reader profiles](petersen-unary-profiles.py). Read counts, cofactor bounds, signed formula bounds and the positive control come from the [earlier proof](petersen-unary-input.md#direct-inputs-and-restrictions). A hypothetical 18-binary-gate circuit would be a minimum signed circuit because the unrestricted 17-gate exclusion already passed. The signed formula needs at least 20 leaves, so there is genuine sharing. The zero restrictions give at most three raw reads per vertex. A raw-pair gate is AND or OR, as the distinguished inverted wire is internal. This leaves 1,581 combinations of raw-reader profile and first gate modulo the 120 graph automorphisms.

The replay keeps a separate bit for every possible marked internal gate z. Each normalized wire equals zero at the empty input. If the next gate's signed-AND selectors are a,b, its two physical realizations are AND on its specified signs or OR on both opposite signs, with opposite output polarities. The truth table at zero determines which output sign each realization supplies. Before z each raw wire and each existing intermediate is available only in its positive physical orientation. At z its complement becomes available, and after z both admissible cases are propagated at every gate. Only a physically positive final target may survive. Distinct gates equaling one another up to sign can be merged for the unrestricted signed lower bound. With distinct operands only z can have both physical orientations for the same mark.

For a fixed mark, all physical wires are monotone within each level of H. Both levels are order-convex. A true row is minimal in its level if it has no single-bit downward true neighbor in that level. A false row is maximal if it has no single-bit upward false neighbor in the level. These boundary rows suffice for an entire monotone suffix. For a gate strictly after z, replacing it by a constant or earlier available physical wire saves a gate if the replacement is at least as high on every boundary positive and at most as high on every boundary negative. H and its input cone are unchanged by this replacement. If a solution existed, it would contradict the 17-signed-gate bound.

One detail matters when adapting the signed enumerator. If H=a AND b, the physically legal gate a AND (NOT H) uses an operand of its other argument. Rewriting it as a AND (NOT b) could introduce a second distinct complemented wire. The replay explicitly allows these adjacent reads. When pinning a raw input, it charges only one guaranteed extra gate for one or more direct readers forced to a constant. Follow any forced signal to a consumer. If that consumer reads the pinned raw input directly it too becomes constant. Since the cofactor is nonconstant and all original gates are live, some consumer outside the direct-reader set must eventually fold. This justifies the one extra charge without an adjacency assumption. All unchanged signed restriction and separation tests are necessary conditions on the unrestricted signed representation.

The 20-gate ordinary star construction is the positive control. The solver decodes and independently evaluates it on every row before enumerating the lower-bound cases. A timeout leaves its case open; only completed exclusions count.

Run `python petersen-internal-not-4727.py` with a C++17 compiler. Set `CXX` to a compiler path if necessary. For a Zig installation, set `PETERSEN_ZIG` to the executable and optionally set `PETERSEN_TARGET`. To retry a case, pass `--case PROFILE OP A B --seconds N`. All inputs to that case stay identical apart from the time limit. The independent checks cover all 1,024 rows for 2,756 possible marker functions and both physical orientations on each binary truth table.
