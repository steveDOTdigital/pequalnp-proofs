# Nineteen Petersen gates with one to four NOT gates

[L-meta-4727](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-4727) asks whether a circuit with at most 19 gates and between one and four NOT gates can compute the Petersen edge function. Its opposite is [L-meta-4728](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-4728). The positive proposition is the lower bound. Our search compares all 1,024 input rows, using the [same 15 labeled edges](petersen-unary-input.md) as our 17-signed-gate exclusion. The catalogue gives statement hash `sha256:d8fea2ed197a6599de5fbeb4a88f12fb16067a1334adc8b5e9306341621a3f5f`.

The enumeration is in progress. At 12:00 UTC on 26 November, 1,398 cases had finished on the first pass and another 39 on retry. In all, at least 1,437 of 1,581 cases were excluded. The exact desk claim remains open.

## Which circuit could survive?

Absorbing NOT gates into signs leaves at most 17 binary gates when two or more NOT gates were paid for. Our [signed exclusion](petersen-unary-input.md#the-two-finite-problems) rules out that entire case, including shared NOT outputs. One paid NOT with at most 17 binary gates fails for the same reason. With 18 binary gates, a NOT on a raw input is covered by the separate exclusion allowing both signs of any one raw input for free. A constant, unused gate, unary copy, or two equal or complementary operands in the remaining circuit would save at least one signed binary gate. An unused NOT leaves a monotone circuit of at most 18 gates, also excluded. These observations cover invalid references (the evaluator returns false) and an output chosen before the end of the gate list. The remaining possibility is an 18-binary-gate circuit with one actual intermediate H available in both polarities.

H is monotone in the raw inputs. Cutting its negation gives a suffix monotone in the raw inputs and the cut bit, including all shared earlier intermediates. If H is true only on positive assignments, pin its negation high. Every independent input stays unchanged and all positive inputs can only increase. If H is true on all 15 minimal positive rows, pin its negation low. The result still accepts every edge and rejects every independent set, and the specialized circuit is monotone. Either pin would yield the excluded monotone 18-gate circuit.

More generally, a substitute for the complemented H suffices if it is one on every edge missed by H and zero on every independent input accepted by H, provided the resulting circuit is monotone. In particular this excludes any earlier uncomplemented wire with those properties. For a raw *complement* substitute, the resulting circuit is monotone separately on its two raw slices, so the positive and negative row tests must instead use the minimal positives and maximal negatives on each slice.

A second restriction concerns any vertex v appearing in three direct operand positions. Pin v low. Its three readers disappear, and the nine-vertex restriction still needs at least 16 binary gates with both signs of one raw variable available. Hence H on this restriction depends on at least two variables, is true on an independent input and misses an edge. Indeed, a constant or literal reduces to the earlier nine-vertex exclusion, and either of the two pinning arguments above eliminates a safe or universally edge-active marker. The same conclusion applies whenever the zero restriction eliminates at least three binary gates by physical constant propagation.

## Enumerating the position of the inversion

The [replay](petersen-internal-not-4727.py) uses the independently developed [Petersen signed enumerator](petersen-raw-prefix.cpp), [restriction tests](petersen-profile-cuts.hpp) and [symmetry and reader profiles](petersen-unary-profiles.py). Read counts, cofactor bounds, signed formula bounds and the positive control come from the [earlier proof](petersen-unary-input.md#direct-inputs-and-restrictions). A hypothetical 18-binary-gate circuit would be a minimum signed circuit because the unrestricted 17-gate exclusion already passed. The signed formula needs at least 20 leaves, so there is genuine sharing. The zero restrictions give at most three raw reads per vertex. A raw-pair gate is AND or OR, as the distinguished inverted wire is internal. This leaves 1,581 combinations of raw-reader profile and first gate modulo the 120 graph automorphisms.

The replay keeps a separate bit for every possible marked internal gate z. Each normalized wire equals zero at the empty input. If the next gate's signed-AND selectors are a,b, its two physical realizations are AND on its specified signs or OR on both opposite signs, with opposite output polarities. The truth table at zero determines which output sign each realization supplies. Before z each raw wire and each existing intermediate is available only in its positive physical orientation. At z its complement becomes available, and after z both admissible cases are propagated at every gate. Only a physically positive final target may survive. Distinct gates equaling one another up to sign can be merged for the unrestricted signed lower bound. With distinct operands only z can have both physical orientations for the same mark.

For a fixed mark, all physical wires are monotone within each level of H. Both levels are order-convex. A true row is minimal in its level if it has no single-bit downward true neighbor in that level. A false row is maximal if it has no single-bit upward false neighbor in the level. These boundary rows suffice for an entire monotone suffix. For a gate strictly after z, replacing it by a constant or earlier available physical wire saves a gate if the replacement is at least as high on every boundary positive and at most as high on every boundary negative. H and its input cone are unchanged by this replacement. If a solution existed, it would contradict the 17-signed-gate bound.

One detail matters when adapting the signed enumerator. If H=a AND b, the physically legal gate a AND (NOT H) uses an operand of its other argument. Rewriting it as a AND (NOT b) could introduce a second distinct complemented wire. The replay explicitly allows these adjacent reads. When pinning a raw input, it charges only one guaranteed extra gate for one or more direct readers forced to a constant. Follow any forced signal to a consumer. If that consumer reads the pinned raw input directly it too becomes constant. Since the cofactor is nonconstant and all original gates are live, some consumer outside the direct-reader set must eventually fold. This justifies the one extra charge without an adjacency assumption. All unchanged signed restriction and separation tests are necessary conditions on the unrestricted signed representation.

A verified first-gate exclusion also excludes that same raw AND or OR anywhere in a candidate with the same raw-reader profile. Both operands are raw, so the gate can be scheduled first, preserving the marked inversion even when it is this gate's complement. Each graph automorphism preserving the profile transports the exclusion to its entire orbit. The retry option `--exclude-verified PROFILE OP A B` removes only these proved raw-gate orbits. Each strengthened retry records the bit mask of previously verified first-gate cases on which it relies. Replaying those dependencies in verification order keeps the argument acyclic.

The 20-gate ordinary star construction is the positive control. The solver decodes and independently evaluates it on every row before enumerating the lower-bound cases. A timeout leaves its case open; only completed exclusions count.

## What one NOT can recover from earlier wires

At any cut, record the Boolean features still available to the rest of the circuit. Order two raw assignments x and y by componentwise comparison of their feature vectors, writing x ≼ y. Assume the target is determined by these features, so equal feature vectors have equal target values.

There is a suffix of unrestricted size with at most one NOT **if and only if** there is no chain p ≼ i ≼ q ≼ j with target values 1, 0, 1, 0. For necessity, cut the suffix at its NOT input H. On each of the two drops the monotone H must rise from 0 to 1, since otherwise every physical input to the monotone remainder increases. Monotonicity between i and q makes two such rises impossible.

For sufficiency, call a negative feature vector bad if a positive vector lies below it. Let H be the indicator of the upward closure of all bad negatives. Every bad negative has H=1. Every positive below a bad negative has H=0, since a bad negative below that positive would supply the forbidden four-row chain. Thus for every formerly ordered positive-negative pair, the extra coordinate NOT H is 1 on the positive and 0 on the negative. In the enlarged feature space no positive lies below a negative. The upward closure of the positive vectors now computes the target. Both upward closures are monotone Boolean functions on the whole feature cube. A monotone disjunction of conjunctions realizes each one.

### Two hidden Petersen coordinates

Choose distinct hidden vertices a and b. Choose each of U and V to be the OR of two of the eight visible raw variables, allowing U=V. Suppose the only uses of a are as an operand of a AND U or a OR U, and similarly the only uses of b are in b AND V or b OR V. Give the suffix both of each pair of outputs and all eight visible variables, with no size or fanout restriction. This contains any circuit with the stated raw-reader pattern, even if some permitted outputs were never used. Its feature vector determines the entire raw input: when U=0 its OR output reveals a, and when U=1 its AND output reveals a. The same recovers b.

**32,190 of the 35,280 labeled configurations require at least two NOT gates**, excluding them from the remaining one-NOT Petersen case at every gate budget. In the other 3,090, one NOT suffices with unrestricted size. Their 18-binary-gate question still depends on the main search. The configurations are grouped into 290 and 36 graph automorphism orbits, respectively. This is a classification of circuit patterns, so its counts cannot be added to the main search totals.

For example, take a=0, U=x1 OR x2, b=3, V=x1 OR x4. The input sets {0,5}, {2,5}, {2,3,5}, {2,4,5} are ordered on every retained feature. The first contains edge (0,5), the third contains edge (2,3), and the second and fourth are independent. These four assignments alone exclude a one-NOT suffix.

The [independent replay](petersen-frontier-4727.py) performs two exact checks on every orbit. The first finds the largest number of drops along paths in the feature order. When a visible raw bit rises, a hidden coordinate may fall exactly when its companion OR changes from zero to one. Such falls may happen together. A hidden increase can always be performed separately. Any pair of ordered feature vectors can be connected by these moves: increase the visible bits in any order, perform each required hidden decrease when its companion first rises, and make any remaining hidden increases at the end. Each companion changes at most once, giving at most two drops. The second check independently computes the upper and lower sets of every negative vector using all 1,024 rows and tests directly for a four-row obstruction.

## What a single wire must retain

Let A contain at least two private input vertices. Suppose the rest of the circuit can see the inputs outside A and one bit H, and every influence of A reaches the output through H. For any proposed set B of outside inputs available to H, such a representation exists **exactly when B contains every outside neighbor of A**. This holds for arbitrary Boolean gates and any number of NOTs.

For sufficiency, H reports whether an edge incident to A has both endpoints on. All the inputs this needs belong to A or its outside neighborhood. The rest of the circuit also checks edges disjoint from A.

For necessity, suppose H cannot see an outside neighbor u. Choose its neighbor a in A and another vertex b in A. Fix all other inputs as below. As a and b vary, the output on the two settings of u takes three different pairs of values: (0,0), (0,1) and (1,1). With H independent of u, its single bit could select only two functions of u.

* If a and b are adjacent, fix all other vertices besides a, b and u to zero. Set the private pair successively to the empty set, {a}, and {a,b}.
* If a and b are nonadjacent, choose a neighbor w of b adjacent to neither a nor u, with w different from u. Such a choice exists: b has three neighbors. The nonadjacent pair a,b has one common neighbor. If b,u are nonadjacent they too have only one common neighbor. If b,u are adjacent they have none, and u itself was already removed as a common neighbor of a,b. Set w to one, the other fixed vertices to zero, and select the empty set, {a}, then {b}. The three functions of u are again zero, u and one.

In particular H must depend on every vertex of A and every outside neighbor. The inputs in A are essential to the Petersen function and have no other route to the output. A connected circuit depending on k distinct raw inputs needs at least k−1 binary gates even with free unary gates. Thus a wire that gathers a private adjacent pair requires at least five binary gates in its input cone. A private nonadjacent pair requires at least six. For a private triple the bound is seven, eight or nine gates, attained as a *support bound* by 90, 20 and 10 choices of triple respectively. These are lower bounds on the cone's size.

Direct evaluation on the 1,024-row Petersen table verified all 210 private-pair/outside-neighbor obstructions. Choosing a private pair for each larger set gives 4,450 set-and-neighbor obstructions in total, including the pairs. In the 18-reader profiles of the main search there is only one shared intermediate. This theorem applies to its input cone whenever at least two raw variables have no reads outside that cone. The existing search already checks whether the resulting one-bit factorization is possible on its truth table, so these obstructions are not counted a second time.

### Exact cost for a private pair

With fan-in-two AND and OR gates and free NOTs, the minimum binary cost of H is **six for an adjacent pair and seven for a nonadjacent pair**. This is the cost of the single signal that carries both private inputs. Gates used by the rest of the circuit are outside this cost.

To attain six for an adjacent pair a,b, name their other neighbors p,q and r,s. Use H = (a AND (b OR p OR q)) OR (b AND (r OR s)). For a nonadjacent pair, let c be their common neighbor, and let p,q and r,s be their other neighbors. Use H = (a AND (c OR p OR q)) OR (b AND (c OR r OR s)). The second circuit costs seven binary gates. Each H reports exactly the edges incident to the pair.

The support bounds above leave just one smaller budget in each case. A circuit on k essential variables using k−1 binary gates has a tree as its connected input cone after unary gates are suppressed. Each variable appears once. Pushing negations down gives an AND/OR formula with signed leaves. Fix all variables except a and b. Whenever the target is nonconstant as a function of these two inputs, the signal H must equal that two-input function or its complement: after fixing the outside inputs the suffix sees just one bit. In a read-once formula, whenever both variables remain essential, all side subtrees on their paths specialize to neutral values. The operation at their lowest common ancestor and their literal signs are fixed.

For adjacent a,b, setting every other input to zero leaves a AND b. Turning on p and r instead leaves a OR b. The vertices p and r cannot be adjacent because the Petersen graph has no four-cycle. The two possible encodings of AND and the two possible encodings of OR are disjoint. A read-once H cannot supply both.

For nonadjacent a,b, turn on p alone, then p and c. This changes the private function from a to a OR b. Turning on r alone, then r and c, changes it from b to a OR b. The visible inputs in each pair are independent because the graph has no triangles. In the formula tree let L be the lowest common ancestor of a and b. All four restrictions leave H nonconstant, so gates above L pass its value. At L the other branch can never block an essential private input by taking a controlling constant, since this would make the whole output constant on the pair. Thus changing c in the first comparison must change whether the b branch of L depends on b, and in the second it must change whether the a branch depends on a. A single leaf c cannot occur in both branches. This rules out the last smaller budget.

The displayed cofactors were checked on all 45 labeled pairs. An independent finite enumeration also excluded every read-once monotone encoding for one representative of each pair type after orienting the output. In this enumeration every required variable has two forced rows witnessing a positive rise, so a signed read-once encoding could only be monotone after that orientation. Both upper-bound circuits were checked directly on all 1,024 inputs.
