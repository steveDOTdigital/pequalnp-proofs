# Eleven gates for matrix 36058

[L-meta-21119](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-21119.705beb25706b.json) asks whether ten binary AND/OR gates can represent this four-by-five matrix on its one-per-side promise. Lean verified an eleven-gate circuit. An independent exhaustive calculation excludes ten.

Four rectangles cover the ones: row 0 in columns 3 and 4, rows 0 through 2 in column 1, row 1 in column 2, and rows 2 and 3 in column 0. Building their indicators takes two, three, one and two gates. Three OR gates combine them. Lean checked the resulting circuit on all twenty row-column pairs. [Lean proof of the upper bound](Mask36058Upper.lean)

To test ten gates, enumerate the truth tables of the wires still needed by the output. Start with every possible subset of the nine inputs. Try AND and OR for each pair of retained wires and each possibility for reusing the operands. At most one more wire than the remaining gate count can feed a single output. Furthermore, for each accepted and rejected pair, at least one retained wire must be true on the former and false on the latter, by monotonicity. A constant or repeated operand can be eliminated or replaced by a projection without increasing the size of a circuit computing this nonconstant function.
