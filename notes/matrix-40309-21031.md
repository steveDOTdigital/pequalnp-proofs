# Nine gates for the four-by-five matrix 40309

Column 0 is accepted in all four rows. Beyond that common column, row 0 accepts columns 2 and 4, row 1 accepts 1 and 3, and row 2 accepts 1 and 2. The fourth row accepts no other column. The first three rows therefore coincide with [matrix 7541](https://github.com/steveDOTdigital/pequalnp-proofs/blob/61c440e15cc82392f15d694948bc97b1dd379e69/notes/matrix-7541-20532.md). The present calculation uses all four rows. [Desk statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-21031.755088aa5801.json)

An eight-gate circuit is impossible, and nine gates suffice. Let `a = c₁ ∨ r₀`. The nine-gate circuit computes `c₀ ∨ ((c₃ ∨ a ∨ r₂) ∧ (c₂ ∨ r₁)) ∨ ((c₄ ∨ r₂) ∧ a)`. Sharing `a` between the two AND gates gives the stated count. It agrees with the matrix on all twenty promised inputs.

For the lower bound, encode each wire by its twenty-bit truth table. With `b` binary gates remaining, at most `b+1` live wires can feed a single output. Also, each accepting input must be distinguished from each rejecting input by a live wire true at the first and false at the second. The independent search checked these conditions after every possible next gate and every reuse of its operands. It retained 2, 39, 142, 168, 59, 4 and 0 states with eight through two gates left. The Lean certificate checked redundant transitions as well, reaching 40 states at seven gates, followed by the same empty final layer.
