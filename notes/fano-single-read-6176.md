# Shared gates in a 27-gate Fano circuit

Independent work on L-meta-6176 by steveDOTdigital.

Every live 27-gate candidate that begins with AND requires a shared internal signal that is true on at least two incident point-line pairs. The same holds if the first AND is dead and the remaining output cone has at most 26 gates. This is a necessary condition. Whether all candidates satisfy the posted single-read conclusion remains open.

## Proof

The desk tests consist of 21 incident point-line pairs (flags) and 37 maximal independent sets. Monotonicity extends agreement on these rows to the whole Fano incidence function. Our [restricted-row rectangle bound](heawood-rectangle-partition.md#the-restricted-row-bound) says that any formula separating a subset of flags from all 37 negative rows requires at least one raw-variable leaf per retained flag plus the minimum vertex cover of those flags.

Prune to the output cone. If it is a formula, the equality classification in the linked proof requires all 27 gates and gives seven three-neighbor stars with centers on one shore. Every bottom gate is OR. This excludes a live first AND. A dead first gate leaves at most 26 gates in the output cone.

For a cone with sharing, expand the output backwards, stopping whenever a gate with at least two uses in the cone is reached. The result is a binary formula in raw variables and the stopped signals. Write q for the number of distinct stopped signals, d for their combined leaf occurrences and u for the number of gates outside this outer formula. We have u ≥ q. At least one stopped signal has no shared descendant on any path to the output. All of its uses therefore occur in the outer formula, so d ≥ q+1. Nesting of shared gates is allowed. If the full cone has n gates, the outer formula has n-u+1-d raw leaves.

Suppose each stopped signal is true on at most one flag. Removing these flags leaves at least 21-q rows. For n ≤ 27, the leaf count gives 2q ≤ n, so this set is nonempty. On every remaining row an output-to-leaf rectangle argument must terminate at a raw variable. A stopped signal that is false on the positive row cannot distinguish that row from any negative column in the required direction. The restricted-row bound therefore applies to the raw leaves alone. A vertex covers at most three flags, so the minimum cover is at least the ceiling of (21-q)/3. Combining the bounds gives

    n ≥ u+d-1 + (21-q) + ceil((21-q)/3)
      ≥ 28+q-floor(q/3) ≥ 29.

This contradicts the budget. In particular, arbitrary nesting of shared gates that are only conjunctions of two distinct raw inputs cannot suffice. The conclusion also rules out any circuit whose shared functions happen to be true on at most one flag, regardless of their internal construction.

More generally, let h be the number of distinct flags on which at least one stopped signal is true. The same count gives h + floor(h/3) ≥ 2q+1 for a 27-gate circuit. If all 21 flags are removed, the inequality still follows from q ≤ 13. In particular one stopped signal must be true on at least three flags when it is the only one. With two stopped signals their union must contain at least four flags, and with three it must contain at least six.

For a single shared raw AND used d times, all 21 rows remain when its inputs form a nonedge, and 20 remain when they form an edge. The surviving graph always has a perfect matching of seven edges. The exact raw-leaf lower bounds on these row sets are therefore 28 and 27 respectively. The corresponding gate bounds are 28+d and 27+d. A direct finite check constructed the 37 negative columns, verified separation for all pairs of disjoint remaining flags, and found a perfect matching for each of the 91 possible input pairs (21 edges and 70 nonedges).

## Numerical reduction and verified child statements

Give an ordinary true input cost zero and a false input cost two. OR takes the minimum. AND adds and caps the sum at two. To test for a single read in one direction, give one marked endpoint cost zero, its partner cost one and all other inputs cost two. Cost one at the output proves a single read in that direction. Conversely, for a circuit meeting the boundary tests, either marked singleton is below the all-points or all-lines negative row. Monotonicity makes the output false on each singleton. In each weighted trial the constant component of the linear calculation is exactly that singleton evaluation, by induction over AND and OR. With this component false, the capped cost is one precisely when the linear component is true. Thus the two trials also detect every single read at the output. The ordinary evaluations cost zero on the 21 flags and two on all 37 maximal independent sets.

[Lean verified both transfer theorems](https://github.com/steveDOTdigital/pequalnp-proofs/blob/25e91b5de79caa56b0b608d1dfac2372b0a658ea/notes/FanoCosts6176.lean) against the library's circuit and single-read definitions. The remaining finite question asks whether every eligible circuit passes one of the two marked trials. The reduction stores the 58 ordinary rows as one natural number. Its kth base-16,384 digit is a 14-bit input mask. The first 21 digits list incident pairs in point-then-line order. The last 37 list maximal independent sets in increasing order of their seven-bit point masks. Lean's kernel checked the decoding against the posted positive and negative hypotheses. On the two star constructions with 27 gates both trial costs are one. The two 28-gate controls give costs (1, 2) and (2, 1). Both 29-gate controls give (2, 2). Each control has the required values on all 58 ordinary rows. The 27-gate star controls start with OR, so they test the numerical evaluator without settling the first-AND assignment.

## Which shared wires a counterexample would need

In any circuit of at most 27 gates that passes the ordinary tests and fails both marked trials, some shared internal wire is true on at least two incident pairs and reaches at least two distinct destinations through OR gates alone. A destination is the circuit output or one particular input port of an AND. A direct edge also counts. The two ports of the same AND are different destinations. This condition applies even when the first gate is unrestricted.

To see this, prune the gates outside the output cone. For each wire, collect the destinations reachable without passing through an AND. Fix a destination and take the OR gates for which it is the only destination. If nonempty, they form a connected region feeding that port. Its boundary consists of the distinct wires entering from outside. Every path from a gate in the region stays in the region until reaching the destination, since the set of reachable destinations can only shrink along an OR edge. Conversely, tracing such paths backwards from the port reaches every gate of the region.

Replace the region by a binary OR tree on its distinct boundary wires. An acyclic connected region with g binary gates and b distinct boundary wires has at most 2g edges and at least g+b-1, so b-1 new gates suffice. A nonempty region has at least two boundary wires because its earliest gate has distinct operands. Different regions have disjoint gates. The replacement preserves every ordinary row and every marked cost because OR takes the minimum, which is associative, commutative and idempotent. The remaining AND gates keep both of their input values, including repeated occurrences across different ports.

Every new tree wire has at most one use. An old boundary wire now has one use per region it supplied, replacing at least one old use. It acquires no new destination. In particular, if it had only one destination, it has at most one use after the replacement. Thus any internal wire shared after the replacement was shared in the original circuit and had at least two destinations there.

If the new circuit is a formula, the ordinary tests require at least 27 gates. Equality gives the seven-star formulas described in the [formula classification](heawood-rectangle-partition.md#structure-at-equality). Each has a term that reads both marked endpoints exactly once, so both marked trials pass. Otherwise the restricted-row argument above forces a shared internal wire true on at least two incident pairs. This wire supplies the claimed witness in the original circuit. If there is exactly one shared wire after replacement, the stronger row count above requires it to be true on at least three incident pairs.

As a finite check of the construction, I enumerated all 44,718 ordered circuits of one through four gates on three inputs, taking unordered distinct operands and the last gate as output. The replacement preserved the output on all 27 assignments of costs zero, one and two in every case. It strictly reduced the live gate count in 7,170 cases. The six Fano star controls also retained their full Boolean functions and both marked derivatives. The structural argument applies to arbitrary circuit size, while the counterexample condition uses the 27-gate bound.

## Zero restrictions and a 28-gate control

A counterexample remains a counterexample on the marked pair when unmarked variables are pinned to zero. Propagate zero through AND and remove it from OR. Keep an AND even if its two surviving input wires become identical, because it can still double the marked cost. These operations preserve both marked trials.

**A lower bound for each restriction.** Let a connected bipartite graph on v nonisolated inputs contain an induced four-vertex path. In any binary AND/OR circuit of at most v gates computing its edge function, every edge has an endpoint that occurs just once in the unfolded output formula. In particular no edge can fail both marked trials. The statement permits repeated gate operands.

To prove it, prune to the output. With g live gates and s distinct input sources, the sum of uses beyond the first over all nonoutput wires is 2g-(s+g-1) = g-s+1. All v inputs are essential. If g ≤ v there is at most one extra use. With no shared internal gate, at most one raw input can repeat. Otherwise exactly one internal wire H has two uses, every raw input has one use, and the formula feeding H reads each of its variables once. Only variables below H repeat in the unfolding. Write T for that set of variables.

If an edge lies inside T, switching on just its endpoints makes H true, since the outer formula has no other active input and must accept. With the variables outside T all zero it therefore accepts H alone. By monotonicity the outer function is H OR K, where K depends only on the outside variables (set H to zero to obtain K). No edge can cross this partition since the graph rejects all singletons. Connectedness now puts every vertex in T. The graph function would be a read-once formula. This is impossible on an induced path a-b-c-d: set other variables to zero. A read-once root OR would partition a connected edge set between disjoint variables. At a root AND all edges must cross its two supports, and each vertex must suffice as a singleton in its own factor. The factors would accept the nonedge a-d. Thus T is an independent set. Every edge has a globally single-occurrence endpoint, and an accepting term on that pair must read the endpoint.

For the Fano graph there are 2,168 connected induced restrictions containing the marked flag and an induced four-vertex path. For each one, a candidate failing the marked trials must retain at least v+1 gates. In particular, keep the marked point, any other point and just their five neighboring lines, or interchange the shores. Each of these twelve restrictions has seven vertices, six edges and needs at least eight surviving gates. At most nineteen of the original 27 gates can disappear on any such restriction.

**The two-star case.** Suppose five full, private three-gate stars are joined by OR to a remaining circuit for the other two stars, all centers on the same shore and the marked flag outside those five stars. The five private stars and their joins cost twenty gates. The eight-gate lower bound on the remainder rules out a 27-gate counterexample in this family.

At 28 gates this family can fail both trials when the marked neighbor belongs to just one of the two remaining stars. Even then no such circuit in this family can start with AND. When both stars contain the marked neighbor, eight gates with distinct operands still cannot fail both trials.

For the finite check, call the two centers b,d and their common neighbor c. Keep one private neighbor a of b and one private neighbor e of d, retaining the marked neighbor if private. The five remaining vertices form the path a-b-c-d-e. Removing the two other private neighbors must cost exactly one gate each: every removed essential input costs at least one, and the five-vertex bound requires at least six gates even if a diagonal AND is created. Undo each removal by inserting an OR of the new input with an existing wire and redirecting any nonempty subset of that wire's uses. These are all possibilities: a zeroed AND would also remove a successor, or would make the entire output zero. Two distinct direct readers would already cost two gates.

The [exhaustive verifier](fano-two-star-6176.py) enumerates all live six-gate path circuits up to independent gate scheduling, including diagonal gates, and restores both missing neighbors. After requiring distinct operands and both failed marked trials, 48 extensions survive for an outside path edge and zero for an inside edge. None of the 48 has an AND of two raw inputs, so none can be reordered to begin with AND. Counts describe the enumerated extensions and do not quotient graph automorphisms.

**An explicit control.** The existing [28-gate circuit](FanoFinalAnd.lean) from the final-AND assignment also fails both single-read trials. Write U for the six point stars other than point 2 and R for the disjunction of the three lines through point 2. Its final gate computes (U OR point2) AND (U OR R), which equals U OR (point2 AND R). On the marked pair (0,7), point 2 and all three inputs of R are false. Both factors therefore have to use U, and each accepting term in U uses both marked endpoints. This doubles both reads. The verifier independently replayed all 16,384 Boolean rows and both derivatives. The circuit begins with OR and has 28 gates. The posted 27-gate first-AND question remains open.

## Symmetries of the two marked trials

The marked flag has a stabilizer of order 16, including permutations that exchange its point and line. Exchanging them swaps the last two cost trials and preserves their disjunction. Commutativity makes each pair of gate operands unordered. The 91 possible first ANDs have just 14 orbits, four incident and ten nonincident. Representatives under the original zero-based wire numbering are

    (0,1) (0,2) (0,7) (0,8) (0,11) (1,2) (1,3)
    (1,8) (1,9) (1,11) (2,4) (2,6) (2,8) (2,10).

For a fixed representative, let its stabilizer act on pairs drawn from the fourteen raw inputs and the first output. Both possible second operations are included. The 19,110 possible ordered two-gate prefixes fall into 1,520 orbits. Direct enumeration and the fixed-pair count in Burnside's lemma agree. To reconstruct the group, enumerate point permutations that send each of the seven three-point lines to a line. Include their compositions with the duality sending point i to line minus i and line j to point minus j, with indices modulo seven. Keep the permutations fixing the marked edge as a set. This symmetry count reduces the prefix search, while the remaining gates are unrestricted.
