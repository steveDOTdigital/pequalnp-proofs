# Shared gates in a 27-gate Fano circuit

Independent work on L-meta-6176 by steveDOTdigital.

Every live 27-gate candidate that begins with AND requires a shared internal signal that is true on at least two incident point-line pairs. The same holds if the first AND is dead and the remaining output cone has at most 26 gates. This is a necessary condition. Any such first-AND circuit has [at most two complete stars on either shore](#three-complete-stars-already-force-all-seven). Whether all candidates satisfy the posted single-read conclusion remains open.

## Proof

The desk tests consist of 21 incident point-line pairs (flags) and 37 maximal independent sets. Monotonicity extends agreement on these rows to the whole Fano incidence function. Our [restricted-row rectangle bound](heawood-rectangle-partition.md#the-restricted-row-bound) says that any formula separating a subset of flags from all 37 negative rows requires at least one raw-variable leaf per retained flag plus the minimum vertex cover of those flags.

Prune to the output cone. If it is a formula, the equality classification in the linked proof requires all 27 gates and gives seven three-neighbor stars with centers on one shore. Every bottom gate is OR. This excludes a live first AND. A dead first gate leaves at most 26 gates in the output cone.

For a cone with sharing, expand the output backwards, stopping whenever a gate with at least two uses in the cone is reached. The result is a binary formula in raw variables and the stopped signals. Write q for the number of distinct stopped signals, d for their combined leaf occurrences and u for the number of gates outside this outer formula. We have u ≥ q. At least one stopped signal has no shared descendant on any path to the output. All of its uses therefore occur in the outer formula, so d ≥ q+1. Nesting of shared gates is allowed. If the full cone has n gates, the outer formula has n-u+1-d raw leaves.

Suppose each stopped signal is true on at most one flag. Removing these flags leaves at least 21-q rows. For n ≤ 27, the leaf count gives 2q ≤ n, so this set is nonempty. On every remaining row an output-to-leaf rectangle argument must terminate at a raw variable. A stopped signal that is false on the positive row cannot distinguish that row from any negative column in the required direction. The restricted-row bound therefore applies to the raw leaves alone. A vertex covers at most three flags, so the minimum cover is at least the ceiling of (21-q)/3. Combining the bounds gives

    n ≥ u+d-1 + (21-q) + ceil((21-q)/3)
      ≥ 28+q-floor(q/3) ≥ 29.

This contradicts the budget. In particular, arbitrary nesting of shared gates that are only conjunctions of two distinct raw inputs cannot suffice. The conclusion also rules out any circuit whose shared functions happen to be true on at most one flag, regardless of their internal construction.

More generally, let h be the number of distinct flags on which at least one stopped signal is true. The same count gives h + floor(h/3) ≥ 2q+1 for a 27-gate circuit. If all 21 flags are removed, the inequality still follows from q ≤ 13. In particular one stopped signal must be true on at least three flags when it is the only one. With two stopped signals their union must contain at least four flags, and with three it must contain at least six.

For a single shared raw AND used d times, all 21 rows remain when its inputs form a nonedge, and 20 remain when they form an edge. The surviving graph always has a perfect matching of seven edges. The exact raw-leaf lower bounds on these row sets are therefore 28 and 27 respectively. The corresponding gate bounds are 28+d and 27+d. A direct finite check constructed the 37 negative columns, verified separation for all pairs of disjoint remaining flags, and found a perfect matching for each of the 91 possible input pairs (21 edges and 70 nonedges).

## Numerical reduction and verified child statements

Give an ordinary true input cost zero and a false input cost two. OR takes the minimum. AND adds and caps the sum at two. To test for a single read in one direction, give one marked endpoint cost zero, its partner cost one and all other inputs cost two. Cost one at the output proves a single read in that direction. Conversely, for a circuit meeting the boundary tests, either marked singleton is below the all-points or all-lines negative row. Monotonicity makes the output false on each singleton. In each weighted trial the constant component of the linear calculation is exactly that singleton evaluation, by induction over AND and OR. With this component false, the capped cost is one precisely when the linear component is true. Thus the two trials also detect every single read at the output. The ordinary evaluations cost zero on the 21 flags and two on all 37 maximal independent sets.

[Lean verified both transfer theorems](https://github.com/steveDOTdigital/pequalnp-proofs/blob/25e91b5de79caa56b0b608d1dfac2372b0a658ea/notes/FanoCosts6176.lean) against the library's circuit and single-read definitions. The remaining finite question asks whether every eligible circuit passes one of the two marked trials. The reduction stores the 58 ordinary rows as one natural number. Its kth base-16,384 digit is a 14-bit input mask. The first 21 digits list incident pairs in point-then-line order. The last 37 list maximal independent sets in increasing order of their seven-bit point masks. Lean's kernel checked the decoding against the posted positive and negative hypotheses. On the two star constructions with 27 gates both trial costs are one. The two 28-gate controls give costs (1, 2) and (2, 1). Both 29-gate controls give (2, 2). Each control has the required values on all 58 ordinary rows. The 27-gate star controls start with OR, so they test the numerical evaluator without settling the first-AND assignment.

## Which shared wires a counterexample would need

In any circuit of at most 27 gates that passes the ordinary tests and fails both marked trials, some shared internal wire is true on at least two incident pairs and reaches at least two distinct destinations through OR gates alone. A destination is the circuit output or one particular input port of an AND. A direct edge also counts. The two ports of the same AND are different destinations. This condition applies even when the first gate is unrestricted.

To see this, prune the gates outside the output cone. For each wire, collect the destinations reachable without passing through an AND. Fix a destination and take the OR gates for which it is the only destination. If nonempty, they form a connected region feeding that port. Its boundary consists of the distinct wires entering from outside. Every path from a gate in the region stays in the region until reaching the destination, since the set of reachable destinations can only shrink along an OR edge. Conversely, tracing such paths backwards from the port reaches every gate of the region.

Replace the region by a binary OR tree on its distinct boundary wires. An acyclic connected region with g binary gates and b distinct boundary wires has at most 2g edges and at least g+b-1, so b-1 new gates suffice. A nonempty region has at least two boundary wires because its earliest gate has distinct operands. Different regions have disjoint gates. The replacement preserves every ordinary row and every marked cost because OR takes the minimum, which is associative, commutative and idempotent. The remaining AND gates keep both of their input values, including repeated occurrences across different ports.

Every new tree wire has at most one use. An old boundary wire now has one use per region it supplied, replacing at least one old use. It acquires no new destination. In particular, if it had only one destination, it has at most one use after the replacement. Thus any internal wire shared after the replacement was shared in the original circuit and had at least two destinations there.

If the new circuit is a formula, the ordinary tests require at least 27 gates. Equality gives the seven-star formulas described in the [formula classification](heawood-rectangle-partition.md#structure-at-equality). Each has a term that reads both marked endpoints exactly once, so both marked trials pass. Otherwise the restricted-row argument above forces a shared internal wire true on at least two incident pairs. This wire supplies the claimed witness in the original circuit. If there is exactly one shared wire after replacement, the stronger row count above requires it to be true on at least three incident pairs.

As a finite check of the construction, I enumerated all 44,718 ordered circuits of one through four gates on three inputs, taking unordered distinct operands and the last gate as output. The replacement preserved the output on all 27 assignments of costs zero, one and two in every case. It strictly reduced the live gate count in 7,170 cases. The six Fano star controls also retained their full Boolean functions and both marked derivatives. The structural argument applies to arbitrary circuit size, while the counterexample condition uses the 27-gate bound.

## Zero restrictions and a 28-gate control

A counterexample remains a counterexample on the marked pair when unmarked variables are pinned to zero. Propagate zero through AND and remove it from OR. Keep an AND even if its two surviving input wires become identical, because it can still double the marked cost. These operations preserve both marked trials.

**A lower bound for each restriction.** Let a connected bipartite graph on v nonisolated inputs contain an induced four-vertex path. In any binary AND/OR circuit of at most v gates computing its edge function, every edge has an endpoint that occurs just once in the unfolded output formula. In particular no edge can fail both marked trials. The statement permits repeated gate operands.

To prove it, prune to the output. With g live gates and s distinct input sources, the sum of uses beyond the first over all nonoutput wires is 2g-(s+g-1) = g-s+1. All v inputs are essential. If g ≤ v there is at most one extra use. With no shared internal gate, at most one raw input can repeat. Otherwise exactly one internal wire H has two uses, every raw input has one use, and the formula feeding H reads each of its variables once. Only variables below H repeat in the unfolding. Write T for that set of variables.

If an edge lies inside T, switching on just its endpoints makes H true, since the outer formula has no other active input and must accept. With the variables outside T all zero it therefore accepts H alone. By monotonicity the outer function is H OR K, where K depends only on the outside variables (set H to zero to obtain K). No edge can cross this partition since the graph rejects all singletons. Connectedness now puts every vertex in T. The graph function would be a read-once formula. This is impossible on an induced path a-b-c-d: set other variables to zero. A read-once root OR would partition a connected edge set between disjoint variables. At a root AND all edges must cross its two supports, and each vertex must suffice as a singleton in its own factor. The factors would accept the nonedge a-d. Thus T is an independent set. Every edge has a globally single-occurrence endpoint, and an accepting term on that pair must read the endpoint.

For the Fano graph there are 2,168 connected induced restrictions containing the marked flag and an induced four-vertex path. For each one, a candidate failing the marked trials must retain at least v+1 gates. In particular, keep the marked point, any other point and just their five neighboring lines, or interchange the shores. Each of these twelve restrictions has seven vertices, six edges and needs at least eight surviving gates. At most nineteen of the original 27 gates can disappear on any such restriction.

**The two-star case.** Suppose five full, private three-gate stars are joined by OR to a remaining circuit for the other two stars, all centers on the same shore and the marked flag outside those five stars. The five private stars and their joins cost twenty gates. The eight-gate lower bound on the remainder rules out a 27-gate counterexample in this family.

At 28 gates this family can fail both trials when the marked neighbor belongs to just one of the two remaining stars. Even then no such circuit in this family can start with AND. When both stars contain the marked neighbor, eight gates with distinct operands still cannot fail both trials.

For the finite check, call the two centers b,d and their common neighbor c. Keep one private neighbor a of b and one private neighbor e of d, retaining the marked neighbor if private. The five remaining vertices form the path a-b-c-d-e. Removing the two other private neighbors must cost exactly one gate each: every removed essential input costs at least one, and the five-vertex bound requires at least six gates even if a diagonal AND is created. Undo each removal by inserting an OR of the new input with an existing wire and redirecting any nonempty subset of that wire's uses. These are all possibilities: a zeroed AND would also remove a successor, or would make the entire output zero. Two distinct direct readers would already cost two gates.

The [exhaustive verifier](fano-two-star-6176.py) enumerates all live six-gate path circuits up to independent gate scheduling, including diagonal gates, and restores both missing neighbors. After requiring distinct operands and both failed marked trials, 48 extensions survive for an outside path edge and zero for an inside edge. None of the 48 has an AND of two raw inputs, so none can be reordered to begin with AND. Counts describe the enumerated extensions and do not quotient graph automorphisms.

**An explicit control.** The existing [28-gate circuit](FanoFinalAnd.lean) from the final-AND assignment also fails both single-read trials. Write U for the six point stars other than point 2 and R for the disjunction of the three lines through point 2. Its final gate computes (U OR point2) AND (U OR R), which equals U OR (point2 AND R). On the marked pair (0,7), point 2 and all three inputs of R are false. Both factors therefore have to use U, and each accepting term in U uses both marked endpoints. This doubles both reads. The verifier independently replayed all 16,384 Boolean rows and both derivatives. The circuit begins with OR and has 28 gates. The posted 27-gate first-AND question remains open.

## Four complete stars force all seven

Suppose a binary AND/OR circuit on the fourteen Fano inputs computes incidence with at most 27 gates. If it contains four ordinary stars centered on distinct lines, the whole circuit consists of seven ordinary line stars joined by an OR tree. The same holds on the point shore by duality. Here an ordinary star is the AND of its raw center with a binary OR tree on its three distinct neighbors. The hypothesis allows arbitrary later reads of all three gates of each star. This rules out even one raw first AND in this family, independently of the marked trials.

**The gate budget.** The four stars contain twelve distinct gates: two lines cannot share a disjunction of two different points. Schedule these gates first. Their suffix has at most fifteen gates and must read either the raw bit or the star output of each completed line. It must also read the raw bits of the three unfinished lines and of every point in their union U. For the last assertion, take an unfinished line through the point, activate only that line, clear its other points and switch on all points off it. Every completed-line point disjunction containing the varied point is saturated by an off-line point. Only the raw bit can communicate the change.

The three unfinished lines either meet at a point or form a triangle. In the first case retain their common point and one private point on each line. In the second retain their three intersections. In each case exactly three mandatory raw points in U are discarded. Turning off the four completed line bits and setting these three points to zero removes at least seven suffix gates. Indeed a connected binary output cone with g gates and s sources has undirected cycle rank g-s+1. Pinning k live sources and keeping a nonconstant output leaves a connected minor with at most s-k sources, hence removes at least k gates. Apply this to the abstract source wires before specializing correlated inputs.

The remaining core costs eight gates. For the triangle it is a six-cycle; an old line contains at most one retained corner. For concurrent lines it is three length-two arms. Even free ORs of the retained private points cannot lower this last cost. All seven raw inputs are necessary: saturate any nontrivial private OR containing a given private point by turning the other private points on while testing its line. With two distinct extra helpers, nine live sources already require eight gates. With one, seven gates would give a read-once tree. Its common point and one line can be restricted to AND or OR by setting the other inputs, which is incompatible with their fixed lowest common ancestor. With no helper the forest bound gives eight. The [existing eight-gate core classifications](plane-ten-star-equality.md#the-two-eight-gate-cores) also determine the equality circuits without helpers: the pencil has its three line stars, and the six-cycle has all three stars on one shore, in both cases joined by OR.

All fifteen suffix gates are now accounted for. An eighth constant source under any of the core restrictions is impossible. Thus each completed line supplies exactly one channel, either its raw line bit or its star. No raw point outside U is used. A used intermediate OR must lie entirely in U (turn outside points on) and meet every choice of retained core (turn them off). In a pencil each old line meets each unfinished line at a private point; the other private point on each unfinished line can be retained instead, so no old OR survives. In a triangle the only possibilities are a corner c OR the private point a on the opposite unfinished line. The finite geometry check covers all seven pencils and 28 triangles.

**Restoring the three private points.** Keep the completed channels off. Pinning any k private points being restored costs at least k gates in addition to the core eight. In a triangle each raw private input is necessary even with its free helper: activate its own line and the opposite corner, with the other points on its line off. The helper is saturated and the output changes on the raw input. The analogous pencil test uses no helper. Equality at every level forces each restored point to have exactly one OR reader. An AND pinned low would remove its own gate and a successor, since the pinned target remains nonconstant. Reverse the pin by inserting that OR on a wire and redirecting any nonempty subset of its uses.

The [private-point restoration proof](plane-ten-star-equality.md#restore-the-private-points) applies directly to these same cores. A point may extend only the point OR of its own line star. At a different line it would create a false edge, while an insertion above the stars would accept a singleton. The alternative orientation of the six-cycle cannot acquire a private point for just one line. Replacing a previously used corner c by the newly available c OR a would also create a false edge at a line through c, since a lies on the opposite line. Consequently the eleven-gate residual is exactly three full line stars joined by OR. It uses no intermediate helper from a completed star.

**Restoring the four completed channels.** This step must allow a completed line to lie entirely inside U. Every subset of t restored channels costs at least 11+t suffix gates, since setting their line bits low pins t mandatory channels and leaves the eleven-gate problem. The original fifteen-gate suffix attains this bound at every stage. Thus each channel returns by one OR insertion, with all its actual, possibly correlated values.

Activate just the line being restored and one of its incident points. An insertion in a point-side wire cannot turn on any of the old stars because their line bits are off. An insertion on an old raw line bit reaches just that line's star. Choose a point on the new line outside that old line to see the failure. This leaves the star outputs and the OR tree above them, including any previously restored stars. Inserting a raw line there would accept the line singleton. Its channel must therefore be its full star, joined by OR to the existing stars. Induction restores all seven. Their 21 star gates and six joining gates exhaust the budget. Every flag has both single reads, and the first gate of every legal schedule is OR.

The [Fano-specific verifier](fano-four-stars-6176.py) also tested the first channel insertion directly. It covered every choice of the three unfinished lines, the three-point OR shapes in each star, upper OR shapes and restored line: 11,340 cases. It evaluated every insertion on all 2,048 assignments to seven points and four lines. In each case exactly the five upper positions accepted the new star, and none accepted its raw line as a substitute. The core classifications are reused from the earlier finite proofs. The [three-star theorem](#three-complete-stars-already-force-all-seven) strengthens this restriction.

## Three complete stars already force all seven

Fix any three ordinary line stars in a Fano incidence circuit of at most 27 gates. The circuit must be the seven ordinary line stars joined by OR. Point stars give the dual result. Intermediate gates of the three given stars may have arbitrary readers. In particular a circuit starting with AND and passing the ordinary boundary tests could contain at most two complete stars on each shore.

The three stars cost nine distinct gates and can be scheduled first, leaving at most eighteen. As in the [four-star proof](#four-complete-stars-force-all-seven), the suffix must use a channel for each completed line, all four unfinished line bits, and every raw point on those four lines. All four unfinished lines cannot meet at one point. The following are the two possible configurations.

| Four unfinished lines | Choices | Points retained | Paid core gates | Surviving minimum circuits in the search |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| Three concurrent and a transversal | 28 | The triple point and the three transversal intersections | 12 | 45, all ordinary stars on one shore joined by OR |
| No three concurrent | 7 | The six pairwise intersections | 15 | 405 without point helpers, zero using any complementary pair helper |

**Three concurrent.** Their union with the transversal is all seven points. Pin the three completed channels and the three private points to zero. The four retained points and four lines form three internally disjoint paths of length three between two hubs. Every pair of retained points lies on an unfinished line. Each completed line therefore meets the retained set just once, at a transversal intersection. Each of its other two points is private to a different unfinished line from that intersection. After pinning, its point ORs are constants or copies. The twelve-gate lower bound on the core saturates the budget: nine star gates, six pins, twelve core gates. Each completed line has one channel. Every used intermediate point OR must contain its retained corner, since an additional zero source would leave only eleven gates.

Restore the three private points while keeping the channels zero. At each step a restored point needs its raw wire even given the helpers: turn on its unfinished line, clear the other points of that line, and activate every point off it. Every allowed helper involving the restored point is already true at its retained corner. Pinning back to the core therefore gives a lower bound of twelve plus the number of restored points. Equality allows just the one OR insertion described in the [four-star proof](#four-complete-stars-force-all-seven). A helper that newly acquires the private point cannot take over a read of its corner: the private point is off every unfinished line through that corner, so this would turn a nonedge on. If it already contained a different restored private point it would have been a genuine helper at the preceding level, excluded inductively. An ordinary OR insertion can attach the private point only to its own line star. In a point-centered core every center has at least two neighbors, so inserting at a point center cannot add a point with only one neighbor. Inserting on its line side requires an old point to fire. Thus the opposite orientation disappears on the first insertion. The full residual consists of four ordinary line stars joined by OR.

**No three concurrent.** The four lines cover six points. View the lines as the vertices of a complete graph on four vertices and their pairwise intersections as its six subdividing vertices. The three completed lines run through the seventh point and the three pairs of complementary edges, one pair per line. Turn off the three completed channels. On setting the seventh point low, the only nontrivial free point wires that can remain are the ORs of complementary pairs. The fifteen-gate lower bound, even with all three such helpers available, saturates the 27-gate budget. Turn the seventh point high instead: the unfinished target is the same and any source containing that point that becomes constant would reduce the budget below fifteen. Thus neither that raw point nor a point OR containing it is a suffix source. The minimum-circuit classification in the table excludes use of even one complementary OR and gives the four ordinary line stars on the remaining inputs.

In both configurations fifteen suffix gates implement the four unfinished stars. Restoring each of the three completed channels costs exactly one OR insertion, since pinning it back costs at least one. With all old lines off, an insertion on a point-side wire cannot transmit the incidence of the new line. Inserting on a raw line can reach only that line's star, which misses some point of the new line. The remaining positions join the channel above the stars, where a raw line would incorrectly accept a singleton. Each channel must therefore be its completed star. This accounts for all 21 star gates and all six upper ORs.

**Verification.** The [standalone Node verifier](fano-three-stars-6176.cjs) enumerates the legal minimum circuits of both cores and recognizes the actual gate trees at every successful leaf. It checks the boundary truth tables, retaining only choices that can still separate every positive row from every negative row by an available wire. At each read it tries consuming and retaining the operand. With s live sources and g gates it must spend exactly g-s+1 extra reads. Distinct operands are sorted and an initial pair is chosen up to the automorphisms preserving the used helpers. Bypassing any repeated boundary value, any OR agreeing with an operand on all positives, or any AND agreeing on all negatives would give a smaller ordinary circuit. The lower-bound runs exclude that possibility. On the subdivided complete graph they reproduce all four earlier [fourteen-gate state counts](nine-plane-stars.md#exhaustive-search-on-live-wires). Counts in the table use the verifier's scheduling and symmetry conventions. The full first-AND assignment remains open.

## Two complete stars and the five remaining lines

Suppose a Fano incidence circuit with at most 27 gates contains ordinary stars for two lines. The circuit must use all 27 gates. Moreover, if its first gate is AND, its two raw inputs must form an incident pair whose point lies on neither completed line. This leaves just six of the 91 possible unordered input pairs for any specified two line stars. The dual conclusion holds for two point stars. Whether these remaining first gates can occur at equality is open.

**The exact five-line cost.** The completed lines meet at a point x. Among the five unfinished lines, x lies on exactly one. Retain the other six points and the five unfinished line bits. Up to relabeling, the neighborhood of each unfinished line is as follows:

| Line | Retained point inputs |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | 6, 7, 9 |
| 1 | 7, 8, 10 |
| 2 | 5, 8, 9 |
| 3 | 9, 10 |
| 4 | 5, 6, 10 |

An old point OR on either completed line can become a constant, a raw input, or one of the two disjunctions 5 OR 7 and 6 OR 8. The eleven-input incidence function needs **exactly eighteen additional gates** even if both disjunctions are available for free. Five ordinary stars and four upper ORs attain eighteen. The [verifier](fano-five-lines-6176.cjs) checks this construction on all 2,048 inputs and excludes seventeen gates for each of the four possible subsets of actually used helpers.

Here is the exclusion. It uses the live-wire search from the [three-star proof](#three-complete-stars-already-force-all-seven). On the pins that set each raw vertex to zero, the verified ordinary gate lower bounds are 14 for an outer line, 15 for line 3, 16 for a degree-two point and 15 for either triple point. The optional `--cofactors` run rechecks each vertex orbit and each surviving helper choice, also covering the smaller circuits through the live-wire budget. The line-3 pin leaves the subdivided complete graph on four vertices. Its fifteen-gate circuits all have the ordinary star-tree form established above, even with complementary ORs available.

For each prefix and each pin, suppress a gate whose projected value is zero or equals a previous wire on the restricted positive and negative boundary. Such replacements preserve the two boundary values all the way to the output. Keep a count of the suppressed gates. Each currently live projected zero forces at least one more suppression: both its possible uses yield either zero or a copy, and one binary gate decreases the number of live zeros by at most one. The final output is nonconstant. If the remaining budget falls below the pin's lower bound, this prefix is impossible. On the line-3 pin, if the budget has fallen to fifteen, every surviving gate must have a boundary value occurring in an ordinary star tree: two or three points of one line joined by OR, a complete star, or a union of complete stars. A surviving gate outside these sets would either contradict the classification or be dead, in which case fewer than fifteen gates would suffice. This eliminates prefixes without assuming their unpinned gates form stars.

The four exhaustive seventeen-gate runs visited 85,398 states without helpers, 737,374 for each individual helper, and 1,491,419 with both used. No run found a circuit. Arithmetic on the 31 ordinary test rows uses bits 0 through 30. Rectangles distinguishing positives from negatives use arbitrary-precision integers.

**The first AND.** The two ordinary stars cost six distinct prefix gates. Their suffix needs a channel from each completed line and the raw point x, as in the mandatory-source argument above. Pin the completed lines low and then pin x low. The lower bound eighteen accounts for all 27 gates: six in the prefix, two for the channels, one for x and eighteen in the restricted suffix. In particular, removing either channel while the other is still present can remove only one gate. Its raw line bit, if used as the channel, cannot feed a raw AND: pinning it low would also remove a successor, since the output must remain nonconstant. Each channel is removed by contracting an OR. If x occurs in a raw first AND that survives these contractions, its own zero pin would likewise remove at least two gates. Hence a raw first AND must survive in the minimum eighteen-gate core.

In that core the two inputs of any raw AND must be incident, since otherwise it is false on all positive rows. Replacing it by zero and propagating the constant would give a smaller ordinary circuit. Suppose one input p is a degree-two point, with the other input l one of its incident lines. The other incident line still detects p with l off. If a free helper contains p, also turn on its partner, which lies on neither of p's lines. This saturates the helper without making an edge. Thus p needs a second direct reader besides the raw AND. Pinning p low removes both readers and at least one successor of the AND. If this costs only two gates, that successor must be the second reader, the only successor of the AND. Its value is then either p OR (p AND l) or p AND (p AND l). Absorption removes a gate in the original circuit, contradicting minimality. The zero pin must therefore save at least three, contradicting the sixteen-gate lower bound after deleting p. This leaves only the six flags at the two triple points, precisely the points outside the two completed lines.

## Symmetries of the two marked trials

The marked flag has a stabilizer of order 16, including permutations that exchange its point and line. Exchanging them swaps the last two cost trials and preserves their disjunction. Commutativity makes each pair of gate operands unordered. The 91 possible first ANDs have just 14 orbits, four incident and ten nonincident. Representatives under the original zero-based wire numbering are

    (0,1) (0,2) (0,7) (0,8) (0,11) (1,2) (1,3)
    (1,8) (1,9) (1,11) (2,4) (2,6) (2,8) (2,10).

For a fixed representative, let its stabilizer act on pairs drawn from the fourteen raw inputs and the first output. Both possible second operations are included. The 19,110 possible ordered two-gate prefixes fall into 1,520 orbits. Direct enumeration and the fixed-pair count in Burnside's lemma agree. To reconstruct the group, enumerate point permutations that send each of the seven three-point lines to a line. Include their compositions with the duality sending point i to line minus i and line j to point minus j, with indices modulo seven. Keep the permutations fixing the marked edge as a set. This symmetry count reduces the prefix search, while the remaining gates are unrestricted.
