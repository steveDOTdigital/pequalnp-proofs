# Sharing gates on the generic order-three plane tests

L-meta-2625 asks for a binary monotone circuit with at most 63 gates. It must accept the 52 point-line incidences and reject the 468 distinct negative inputs generated by the desk's eight seeds. The familiar thirteen-star circuit uses 64 gates and passes these tests. We obtain the same lower bound in the following classes.

* There is only one shared gate output, and its input formula uses at most six gates, counting the shared gate itself.
* There is only one shared output, and it computes a disjunction of any number of raw inputs.
* The two shared gates each read two raw inputs, or the second reads the first and one raw input. This includes all AND/OR choices.
* Every shared signal agrees on the tested inputs with a monotone function of at most two raw inputs from a common set of four. The number of shared gates and their fanout are unrestricted.

Here and below a circuit shares a gate output when more than one live operand refers to it. Raw inputs may be read arbitrarily often. The unrestricted 63-gate existence question remains open. The [Lean file](GenericHelperPrice.lean) proves the general pricing inequality, singleton extraction, formula-size accounting and certificate relocation. The [numerical verifier](generic-helper-price.py) enumerates the concrete plane cases and recomputes each bound in integer arithmetic.

## Pricing the leaves outside the shared gates

Cut each shared output where the outer formula reads it. An outer leaf is now either a raw input or a reference to a shared result. Given a positive flag $e$ and a negative input $T$, route through the outer formula to an input that is true on $e$ and false on $T$. At OR gates choose the first true child on $e$. At AND gates choose the first false child on $T$. Each leaf receives a rectangle of flag rows and negative columns. Call the cell $(e,T)$ compulsory when none of the cut signals is true on $e$ and false on $T$. A raw leaf must then report one of the flag's endpoints.

At vertex $v$ a raw leaf can report on a nonempty subset of the four incident flag rows. Merge rectangles with the same vertex and row set by taking the union of their column sets. For a fixed column $T$, the active row sets at a vertex must be disjoint. If $v$ is absent from $T$ and a neighbor is present, their flag must report $v$ whenever its cell is compulsory. The verifier enumerates all families of up to three of the fifteen nonempty row subsets. Four singleton row sets give a valid lower-cost relaxation of every larger family. Their minimum charge is just the forced reports, and they have no dominated rows.

Within a family, a flag is dominated if another flag has a nonempty membership pattern contained in its own. Every report of the second flag at this vertex then forces a report of the first. Consider a flag $(p,l)$ dominated at both ends, with witnesses $(p,b)$ and $(a,l)$. If a negative column contains $a,b$ and both witness cells are compulsory, the first witness reports $p$ and the second reports $l$. This would report both endpoints on the same cell, which is impossible even if that cell is optional. On every flag where such a column exists for every choice of witnesses, at most one endpoint can count a domination.

Give each eligible domination a nonnegative price. Also choose a subset of flag rows and a set of negative columns and charge a nonnegative amount for each compulsory raw report inside that region. The total number of reports there is exactly its number of compulsory cells. Regions may overlap. For each vertex and row family compute its leaf cost, its eligible domination prices, and the minimum possible report charge in each region, subject to all compulsory forced rows. Different regions may minimize independently, which preserves a lower bound. Take the minimum over the vertex's families. Sum the vertex minima and subtract the global report and domination capacities. This gives a lower bound on raw leaves for any outer formula.

The solver chooses prices. The verifier rounds them to nonnegative integer multiples of $1/100000$, recomputes every local minimum, subtracts the exact capacities, and rounds the resulting lower bound up to the next integer. It checks that its integer operations cannot overflow. The initial eight seeds generate 624 listed inputs and 468 distinct columns. The enumeration uses actual projectivities preserving the given supports and checks coverage of the indexed cases.

## One shared output

Suppose the input of a shared wire is a $g$-gate formula. A circuit with at most 63 gates and at least two outer uses has at most $62-g$ outer raw leaves. For raw disjunctions on two, three or four distinct variables, the new calculation gives respectively at least 62, 62 and 61 raw leaves, over four, ten and twenty-five projectivity classes. Each exceeds the relevant budget. This includes three points on one line and the complete four-point line. The dual cases are included. The [previous quadrangle bound](quadrangle-partition.md) already rules out a raw disjunction on five or more variables, and a projection can be replaced by its raw input.

For a general formula $H$, extract its true singleton variables into a disjunction on a set $S$. Set all of $S$ to zero in $H$ and simplify to obtain $R$. Monotonicity gives $H=(\bigvee S)\lor R$. Each extracted variable occurred at least once, so $R$ uses at most $g+1-|S|$ leaves. If $S$ is empty the previous quadrangle result already covers $g≤9$.

If the hot flags of $R$ have a star formula within its leaf budget, replace $R$ by that formula. It remains true on the same flags and is false on every negative. A term that is false on every negative can be taken out of the shared disjunction and ORed into the final output. This preserves the tests and the number of gates: the joining OR below the shared output pays for the new joining OR above it. The only potentially shared signal left is the raw disjunction on $S$.

For a flag graph with $m$ edges, assigning them to a minimum vertex cover of size $t$ produces a star formula with $m+t$ leaves. On at most five distinct vertices a nonempty induced subgraph is a forest, since the incidence graph has girth six. A forest on $d≤5$ active vertices has at most $d-1$ edges and a cover of at most two vertices. Thus, if the residual has a repeated variable and at most six leaves, or an unused variable in its hot graph with that same budget, the star formula fits whenever the number of active vertices is smaller than the leaf count. The one-vertex and empty cases simplify directly.

It remains to examine a read-once residual. Split its top OR into AND terms. The residual has no true singleton, so each hot pair at a term uses one raw input from either child, each accepted by its child as a singleton. Replace each child by the OR of the endpoints used there. This keeps every hot flag and can only reduce negative values. Split the resulting term by connected components of its hot flags. In each component the two child sides are the two shores, up to exchange. The replacement uses each retained raw input once and accepts exactly the original hot flags. A component which is a star is a safe term and moves to the final output. Two nonstar components would require eight inputs. With at most six residual leaves there is therefore at most one remaining component.

The exceptional shared signal is the OR of $S$ with a product of a point disjunction and a line disjunction on one connected nonstar shape. Its hot flags and warm columns are explicit. For a four-vertex shape the three path edges are hot, and a negative warms the product precisely when it contains the missing corner. For larger shapes a negative warms the product when it contains at least one selected point and one selected line. Enumerating the shapes, their singleton placements and the exact local prices gives the following limits for a minimal normalized helper. All smaller forms were covered at their own size.

| Helper gates | Projectivity classes checked | Minimum certified raw leaves | Raw leaves available at 63 gates |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | 6 | 59 | 58 |
| 5 | 60 | 58 | 57 |
| 6 | 390 | 57 | 56 |

## Several small shared gates

For two independent raw pair disjunctions, all 59 configurations require at least 59 outer raw leaves. A 63-gate circuit can afford only 58 after paying for the two gates and at least four outer references. Equal pair disjunctions merge. If the second uses the first, the nontrivial case consists of a pair OR and a triple OR containing that pair. All 18 configurations require at least 61 raw leaves. At least three outer references leave at most 59.

A raw pair AND either accepts no flag, in which case replace it by zero, or is an actual flag and is false on every negative. In the latter case set its uses to zero, simplify at least one reader, and OR its value into the final output for at most the same cost. For $(a\lor b)\land c$, retain only members of the pair that form a flag with $c$. The product then either simplifies or is a safe term. These reductions cover the other gate choices and preserve the no-constant convention by simplifying affected gates.

For several distinct shared raw pair ORs on a common set of at most four inputs, there are at most six different pairs. With $h$ such gates, at least $2h$ outer references leave at most $64-3h$ raw leaves. The single and double bounds above settle the first two cases. The remaining checks are as follows.

| Distinct shared pairs | Projectivity classes | Minimum certified raw leaves | Available at 63 gates |

| 3 | 176 | 56 | 55 |
| 4 | 163 | 53 | 52 |
| 5 | 74 | 52 | 49 |
| 6 | 25 | 49 | 46 |

This also gives the semantic version stated at the beginning. A monotone Boolean function of at most two raw inputs is a constant, a projection, their AND or their OR. Replace each shared gate by the corresponding direct gate or raw value in gate order. Agreement on the tested rows suffices by induction. Merge equal pair ORs, simplify constants and copies, and eliminate shared raw ANDs by the safe-term argument. Removing one shared AND and reinstating it at the output adds one OR, paid for by at least one reader that simplified. An AND that becomes private can stay in a formula. No formerly private gate gains fanout from replacing one of the specified shared gates by a raw-input function. The resulting circuit has at most its original number of gates and only the distinct pair ORs counted above as shared outputs.

Run `python3 notes/generic-helper-price.py` with NumPy and SciPy installed, or use `--help` to select one check. The published calculation used NumPy 1.26.4 and SciPy 1.13.1.

## Searching for a circuit

The [constructive search](generic-star-search.cpp) starts from the 64-gate circuit using thirteen point stars. It bypasses any gate, including the output, by redirecting its uses to any one wire that leaves an acyclic intermediate circuit. It then permits any surviving gate to be AND or OR of any two non-descendant wires, including two copies of a wire. For each location, forcing its value separately to zero and one on all 520 tests identifies required and forbidden values for a replacement. Reinstating the original wire value reproduces every bypassed circuit's output on every row.

There were 5,192 acyclic bypasses. After excluding locations with a fixed error, 63,561 locations allowed 437,278,878 possible replacement gates. None gave a 63-gate witness. This search covers edits of the stated circuit; the structural lower bounds above apply independently to all circuits in their respective classes.
