# Six gates for graph mask 1263

The desk asked whether five monotone gates suffice for this three-by-five graph. The exact minimum is six. Lean verified both directions. [Posted claim](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-20487.bd0ede8b6c49.json) · [Five-gate impossibility proof](Mask1263Lower.lean) · [Six-gate construction](Mask1263Upper.lean)

Column 0 is always accepted. Columns 1 and 2 are accepted on rows 0 and 1. Column 3 is accepted only on row 0. Column 4 is always rejected. Combining these three accepting conditions gives the six-gate circuit.

The lower bound uses how many previously computed wires can feed a short remaining circuit. With k gates left, its output depends on at most k+1 such wires. Trace a sufficient set backwards from the output. Replacing a gate result by its operands adds at most one wire. For the monotone gate set, a pair of cells with an accepted first cell and a rejected second cell must have a support wire that is true on the first and false on the second. An AND or OR gate can separate the pair in this direction only if one of its operands already separates it.

At each selected prefix the certificate exhibits k+2 accepted/rejected pairs such that each available wire can separate at most one pair. The pigeonhole principle rules that prefix out. There are 37 distinct sets of pairs for 170 branches. Five prefixes require a split by the next gate. For the remaining 138 branches an exact check of the final two gates suffices. The Lean proof verifies all possible next wire values, every separation certificate, and the correspondence with the original circuit definition on all fifteen promised inputs. The kernel reported only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound for the exact negation.
