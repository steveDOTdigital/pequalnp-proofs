# Adjacent twins in the eight-input detector

L-meta-35289 asks whether one NOT shortens the posted edge detector. Its exact opposite is L-meta-35288. The [Lean control](EightVertexOneNot35289.lean) gives a 15-gate AND/OR circuit, checks backward references, and verifies the posted predicate on all 256 inputs. The [shared-disjunction replay](shared-biclique-35289.cjs) finds a minimum of 15 among ORs of biclique terms with shared raw-input ORs. Every single-variable low restriction has an AND/OR upper bound of at most twelve in the same model. Fixing vertex 0 or 3 low gives isomorphic restrictions, each with a minimum of twelve in that restricted circuit class.

## Finding a two-gate deletion

Vertices 0 and 3 are adjacent twins. With their values called u and v, let N be the OR of their two common neighbors and R the edge detector away from the twins. Lean checks the identity F = R OR (u AND v) OR ((u OR v) AND N). It also checks that each twin stays essential on both slices of the other.

Choose a smallest circuit among those using at most one NOT. If it is monotone, it already supplies the required replacement. Otherwise contract constants, copies, dead gates and repeated binary operands. The model's default-false reads can be simplified in the same way. The output is nonconstant and all live AND/OR gates have two operands. All reads of the single NOT except its first operand can be discarded.

Suppose neither twin's low pin saves two paid binary gates. Each twin then has at most one direct binary reader. An AND reader would become false on the low pin and simplify a further live binary reader, possibly after the NOT. Such a reader exists because the restricted target remains nonconstant. A twin also cannot feed just the NOT, since the rest of the circuit would make the output nonincreasing in that essential input. Thus its unique binary reader is OR. If the NOT directly reads the twin as well, its value becomes constant true on the low pin, simplifying a binary reader. That reader is different from the twin's OR, since an OR of the twin and its complement would already be constant. Hence each twin has a sole OR reader and no direct NOT read under the assumed failure of two-gate deletion. The twins cannot share their sole OR, since with the other twin high and all other variables low, the output equals the varied twin.

Take the earlier sole reader, u OR W. The wire W is independent of both twins because neither raw twin has been read yet. If R is false on the remaining input, choosing v to be the complement of N makes F equal to u. Consequently W cannot be true on this input, since it would mask u at its only reader. Thus W implies R. Redirect the later reads of u OR W to u and append an OR of the residual output with W. When W is false, the circuit is unchanged in value. When W is true, the required answer and the appended OR are both true. The operation preserves size and the allowed NOT count.

Repeat whenever neither low pin gives two paid deletions. Each step removes the earliest raw reader of a twin from the original circuit. All earlier gates are untouched. The newly appended joins have independent right operands, so the twins can only affect them through the old output cone. A raw twin cannot become an input to that appended chain by the removal of the old base output. Such a replacement would leave the other twin inessential in the complete output. Thus the earliest raw reader always belongs to the finite original list. The process terminates with a monotone replacement or with a circuit of no greater size in which the low pin of one twin saves at least two paid binary gates.

## Remaining lower bound

If a circuit with one NOT beats the 15-gate control, its paid binary budget is at most thirteen. The transformation and a twin pin leave at most eleven paid gates, even after treating all remaining negations as free. A signed lower bound of twelve for the seven-input twin restriction would therefore settle L-meta-35288. The exhaustion of shared raw disjunctions leaves that unrestricted signed lower bound open.
