# Exact formula size for sparse graph edge functions

For a nonempty finite simple graph \(G\), write \(m=|E|\), \(\tau\) for its minimum vertex-cover size, and \(f_G=\bigvee_{uv\in E}x_u x_v\). If \(G\) has maximum degree at most four and contains neither a triangle nor a four-cycle, the least number of binary gates in a monotone formula for \(f_G\) is exactly **\(m+\tau-1\)**. A formula allows repeated input literals and has a tree of internal gates. The argument permits disconnected graphs. Adding \(a\) independent OR variables adds exactly \(a\) gates. For maximum degree three the same total lower bound holds for signed formulas with NOT gates on inputs, even if a negated input is reused and charged only once.

## Charging the leaves

Use a row for each positive input supported on one edge, and a column for each negative input supported on an independent set \(T\). At an OR gate choose the first child true on the row. At an AND gate choose the first child false on the column. The resulting leaf has a label present in the row and absent from the column. Each leaf receives a Cartesian rectangle of rows and columns, since each decision restricts only one of them. Every cell has exactly one report.

For each active vertex \(v\), let \(r_v\) be its number of leaves. The pattern \(P_v(e)\) of an incident edge is the set of those leaves whose row sets contain \(e\). On the column \(N(v)\), which is independent by triangle-freeness, every incident edge must report \(v\). Thus a fixed set of leaf indices (those whose column sets contain \(N(v)\)) meets every row pattern in exactly one index. In particular every pattern is nonempty.

Call \(e\) dominated at \(v\) when another incident row \(f\) has \(P_v(f)\subseteq P_v(e)\). Equality counts. Whenever \(f\) reports \(v\) at a column, \(e\) must also report \(v\) there, by the rectangle property. An edge \(vw\) cannot be dominated at both ends. If the two other rows are \(vb\) and \(wa\), use the column \(\{a,b\}\). It is independent since an edge \(ab\) between distinct witnesses would close a four-cycle. The row \(vb\) must report \(v\) and \(wa\) must report \(w\), forcing two different reports on \(vw\).

Suppose \(r=r_v<d=\deg(v)\). At least \(d-r+1\) rows are dominated at \(v\). To see this, let \(k\ge1\) be the number of active indices on \(N(v)\) and group patterns by their unique active index. If \(k=r\), patterns in a group are identical and at least one group contains multiple rows. At most \(r-1\) rows can be undominated. If \(k=r-1\), one free bit orders each group's patterns by inclusion, leaving at most one undominated row per group. Otherwise \(r\le3\) forces \(r=3,k=1\). Distinct undominated rows then form an antichain on two free bits and there are at most two. This proves the local count. It does use the degree hypothesis. For degree five the bit masks \(5,9,13,6,10\) with active mask \(3\) have only one dominated row.

Let \(S=\{v:r_v<\deg v\}\), \(D=\sum_{v\in S}(\deg v-r_v)\), and let \(W\) count edges avoiding \(S\). Charging each dominated edge at most once gives \(D+|S|\le m-W\). The leaf count \(L\) is at least \(2m-D\). Selecting \(S\) and one endpoint of each uncovered edge gives \(\tau\le |S|+W\), hence \(L\ge m+\tau\). A binary formula has \(L-1\) gates. Conversely, assign each edge to one endpoint in a minimum cover, build the OR of the assigned neighbors at each center, AND with the center and OR the resulting stars. This uses \(m+\tau-1\) gates. Pinning additional OR literals to zero eliminates at least one leaf per alarm while preserving the graph function, and OR extension attains the matching upper bound.

For a cubic graph at least one quarter of the vertices occur three or more times in a monotone formula. Indeed if \(u_1,u_2\) count vertices with one and two occurrences, respectively, their dominations alone require \(3u_1+2u_2\le 3|V|/2\).

## Signed formulas

Push internal NOTs to leaves by De Morgan, preserving the number of binary gates. Every essential variable occurs positively, since a variable with only negative occurrences can only decrease the output when set high. If \(x\) has just one positive occurrence, all its negative occurrences can be removed without increasing the number of leaves. Pick a negative occurrence and consider its lowest common ancestor with the positive occurrence.

* At an OR ancestor replace the negative occurrence by true. At \(x=0\) nothing changes. If the replacement turns the output from false to true at \(x=1\), the branch containing the changed leaf must be true there. Turning \(x\) back to zero preserves that branch's truth, since all its occurrences of \(x\) are negative. The OR therefore stays true despite the sole positive occurrence across it. Outside the ancestor all occurrences of \(x\) are negative too, so the original output at zero was true. This contradicts monotonicity.
* At an AND ancestor replace it by false. At \(x=1\) nothing changes. If the replacement turns the output from true to false at zero, the changed branch must be false after replacement. Turning \(x\) to one preserves its falsity, and the AND stays false despite the positive occurrence across it. The other negative occurrences outside can only decrease, contradicting monotonicity of the original output.

Simplify constants and repeat. In the resulting formula every variable \(b\) still occurring negatively has at least three leaves, including at least two positive ones. Write \(B\) for these vertices. First pin any alarm literals to zero in the original formula, paying one binary gate per alarm. Now pin \(B\) to zero and discard any newly isolated vertices. If \(z\) vertices outside \(B\) lose all their incident edges, their earlier essentiality pays one additional leaf each. The leaves left on the active vertices of \(G-B\) are bounded below by the monotone theorem, with zero as the bound on an edgeless graph. Therefore
\[
 L\ \ge\ 3|B|+z+|E(G-B)|+\tau(G-B).
\]
The same cleaning shows that a signed read-twice formula for a nonempty cubic graph of girth at least five is impossible: after cleaning it would be monotone and still read-twice.

For a subcubic graph, deleting \(B\) loses at most \(3|B|-|E(G[B])|\) edges and at most \(|B|\) units of cover size. Thus \(L-1+t\ge m+\tau-1\) whenever \(t+|E(G[B])|\ge |B|\), where \(t\) is the number of paid NOT gates in the original formula. In particular the full size bound holds when all NOT gates operate on raw inputs. In a general formula a single internal NOT can negate several different variables, so the remaining case is substantive.

## A 24-vertex instance of L-meta-2084

Take the cycle on 24 vertices and add the matching with cyclic offsets \(12,7,-7\) repeated eight times. This is the McGee graph. It has 36 edges and minimum cover size 14. The formula of cover stars costs 49. The preceding proof gives **exactly 49** for monotone formulas and raw-NOT formulas. For arbitrarily placed NOT gates, minimizing the displayed restriction bound over all \(2^{24}\) possible sets \(B\), with one paid NOT whenever \(B\ne\varnothing\), gives **at least 42**. The minimizing cardinalities are 8 and 9. These formula bounds permit unrestricted occurrences of raw inputs.

A separate gate-elimination computation for shared circuits gives **at least 37 binary gates** with all wire inversions free. In the ordinary paid-gate model the lower bound is **38**: the monotone recurrence gives 38 when there is no NOT, and at least one paid NOT adds one to 37. With \(t\) NOTs the bound is \(\max(38,37+t)\). In particular a circuit below 49 must have at most eleven NOTs. This settles the 24-vertex instance for cover demands at most three, including arbitrary disjoint alarm literals. The unrestricted case at demand fourteen remains open.

For the computation, fix a first binary gate on distinct inputs \(p,q\) in a minimum circuit. Either controlling pin collapses the gate and at least one consumer unless it is the whole output. If \(p\) is essential when \(q\) is pinned to its controlling value, \(p\) also has a raw reader outside this gate. For a repeated raw input, three readers save three on both pins. With exactly two readers, opposite controlling values save three on each pin, and equal controlling values save four on the controlling pin and two on the other. In the latter case, two readers with just one common successor can be factored by the common literal, contradicting minimality. A dependent second reader on the same raw variable can first be simplified as a two-variable function. A direct unary negation of a raw variable becomes constant on either pin. The computation also checks simultaneous pins: when both variables need another raw reader and the four cofactors are distinct, at least three distinct raw readers are needed. Two shared gates on the pair alone each cut out a singleton of its four assignments or the complement, so together they distinguish at most three classes. A consumer of the first gate must read neither raw input, since otherwise every use could be replaced separately by at most one binary gate on \(p,q\), eliminating the first gate. Pinning the pair then saves at least four gates.

The residual is a constant or another induced edge function ORed with singleton alarms. A nonconstant residual loses at least one gate per eliminated essential input, by pruning its connected output cone. For a disjoint tree component the exact additive contribution follows from sibling-leaf and degree-two-parent peeling, paying one gate on either pin and a second on a controlling pin when both cofactors are nonconstant. The last remaining edge has one gate. In the monotone case the program also applies the [pseudoforest and repeated-wire bounds](pseudoforest-circuits.md). The implementation keeps only numeric subset bounds, maximizing guaranteed savings over restrictions and minimizing over possible first gates. Its code and output are computational evidence, distinct from the all-graphs formula proof.
