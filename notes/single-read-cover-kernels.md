# Least single-read circuits for small covers and degree-two incidence graphs

For every bipartite graph without a four-cycle whose edges can be covered by four vertices, the normal form requested in L-meta-3722 exists. The same conclusion holds for every forest and whenever each component has a shore of maximum degree at most two, with no bound on cover size. For these nonempty graphs the minimum binary monotone circuit size is exactly

$$ |E|+\tau(G)-1, $$

where $\tau$ is the minimum vertex-cover size. The proof combines a structural argument, existing lower bounds and a [reproducible finite calculation](single-read-cover-kernels.py). [Lean](SingleReadTwins.lean) separately verifies the leaf-duplication construction against the project's definitions. The unrestricted case of L-meta-3722 remains open.

## The witness and its four properties

Fix a minimum vertex cover. Assign each edge to one cover endpoint, choosing either when it has two. Every cover vertex owns at least one edge because deleting it would otherwise leave a cover. For each center OR its assigned neighbors, AND with the center and OR the stars. Counting gives the displayed size. Every constructed gate reaches the output. Each AND operand accepts a singleton, on which the edge function is false. Every OR before an AND has only nonimplicant operands, and every OR above the stars already implies the edge function. This is a lift fixed point. At a listed edge use its owner's star. The point and the line occur once each in its proof tree, so both directions of the project's linear-parts recurrence pass to the output. The empty graph has a zero-gate circuit whose output is past the input list. For forests the matching lower bound is the project's [forest theorem](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/blob/82b3b4894f43e862371c48a6d34284edc00792ca/lean/PEqualNP/MetaComplexity/Helpers/ForestBound.lean).

## Removing leaves and bounding the finite family

In a four-cycle-free bipartite graph, nonisolated vertices with identical neighbor sets must be on the same shore and have a single neighbor. Deleting one of two such leaves lowers the optimum by exactly one. Pin it to zero in a least circuit for the lower bound. For the upper bound, substitute the OR of the two leaves for the surviving leaf. Cover size stays the same because some minimum cover uses their common neighbor. Isolated vertices can be relabeled or dropped freely. An irrelevant input in a proposed smaller circuit can be pinned to zero and its gates simplified. A nonconstant residual needs no constant gates. Padding either shore by isolates therefore gives the desk's equal-shore formulation.

More generally the OR substitution preserves both orientation tests at every edge, for every circuit. Merge the twin bits by OR in the Boolean input and map the new endpoint back to the old one in an edge. At every old wire the two linear coefficients agree on the corresponding edge inputs. The new OR joins two inputs with the same side tag. It combines their coefficient pairs coefficientwise. The Lean file proves evaluation, size, binary gate legality, equality of the two output coefficients, each individual flag test, and preservation of an empty double-reread set. New endpoints are numbered at the end before a possible permutation back into point-then-line order.

Choose a cover of size $k$. Outside it there are no edges. After deletion of duplicate leaves there is at most one singleton neighborhood per cover vertex. Each other outside vertex owns a distinct pair of cover neighbors, since no two outside vertices can share that pair. There are at most $2k+\binom{k}{2}$ active vertices. To enumerate, try each partition of the cover across the shores, each set of internal cross edges and each admissible set of nonempty outside neighborhoods on the opposite shore. Reject four-cycles, duplicate leaf neighbors and graphs with a smaller maximum matching. König's theorem gives the required cover size. Canonical labels order the masks of neighbors after permuting the smaller shore and allow exchange of equally sized shores. There are respectively 1, 3, 13 and 91 distinct graphs at cover sizes one to four. Distinct components may be flipped independently when choosing shores. Enumerating all colorings as above covers them regardless.

There is also a direct proof through cover size three. A cyclic graph in this class contains a six-cycle by the matching bound. A three-vertex cover must be an entire alternating shore of the cycle. No outside vertex can meet two of these cover vertices since they already have a common cycle neighbor. All remaining nonisolated vertices are private leaves on that shore. The six-cycle costs eight gates and pinning all extra essential leaves gives the required additive bound.

## Lower bounds in the calculation

Pin some essential inputs in a live binary circuit and simplify, keeping a nonconstant output. If $s$ of the original distinct sources disappear, at least $s$ gates disappear. The undirected circuit multigraph with $g$ binary gates and $n$ sources has cycle rank $g-n+1$. The residual connected output graph is a minor. With unary NOT gates use the number of binary gates in this identity, and observe that the residual needs no additional NOT gates. One can pin all inputs rendered semantically irrelevant by the stated restriction as well. It follows that adjoining an independent OR alarm to a nonconstant function adds exactly one gate. An isolated star component on $t$ vertices adds exactly $t$ gates to a nonempty remainder, by simultaneous pinning and the ordinary construction.

For a graph with the twins and star components handled, let $L_0,L_1$ lower-bound the costs at the two pins of a vertex. Pinning it to one gives independent alarms on its neighbors OR the residual graph after deleting the vertex and its neighbors. If the residual is empty, $r$ alarms cost $r-1$. Use the project's [reader theorem](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/blob/82b3b4894f43e862371c48a6d34284edc00792ca/lean/PEqualNP/MetaComplexity/Helpers/ReadersExport.lean). Some vertex pays one of the three pairs $(3,3),(4,2),(2,4)$. Take the maximum of $L_0+a,L_1+b$, then the minimum over the allowed pairs and vertices. Both pins are nonconstant because some edge survives the zero pin and the one pin has at least one alarm. Three of the four two-variable restrictions cannot coincide here. By monotonicity and essentiality, that would mean the function uses the pair only through AND or only through OR. A pure AND pair must be an isolated edge, as witnessed by all other incident edges. A pure OR pair must be nonadjacent with equal neighborhoods, by testing the empty assignment and singletons outside the pair. Both configurations were removed. These bounds hold even when NOT is allowed. The subsequent improvements use monotonicity and enter only the monotone recurrence. A monotone least circuit with no pair funnel has a repeated raw input at its first gate. With two direct AND readers the zero pin saves four unless their sole common successor distributes and saves a gate already. Two OR readers give the dual conclusion. With mixed readers each controlling pin also removes a successor, giving three at each pin. Comparable successors directly reading the raw input simplify by absorption. These arguments justify using the same three payment pairs with independently improved monotone bounds.

**One shore must pay two gates.** Suppose one shore contains at least two nonisolated vertices with pairwise distinct neighborhoods. In any least monotone circuit some vertex on that shore can be pinned to zero saving at least two gates. Each vertex is essential, and its zero pin leaves a nonconstant function. Two direct readers would both disappear. A single AND reader becomes zero and must have a successor, costing two again. If all were singly read by OR, consider the earliest gate reading the shore. If it joins two of the shore variables, they occur only through their OR and must have equal neighborhoods. Otherwise its other operand uses only the opposite shore and is one when all those vertices are one. The gate then masks its raw input, although the function on this assignment is precisely the OR of the entire chosen shore. This contradiction proves the bound. A least circuit has no repeated operands, since these gates can be contracted.

## The eight-vertex calculation

Start from a six-cycle with one private leaf. Its optimum is nine. All nine-gate realizations up to scheduling and operand swaps are the nine circuits obtained by single OR insertion into the [six optimal cycle shapes](plane-ten-star-equality.md). Indeed, pinning the leaf to zero must save exactly one gate. Its only reader is therefore an OR. Reverse the contraction by inserting an OR with the new variable and redirecting a nonempty subset of the old reads of the other operand. The designated output also counts as a read. Full truth tables select nine surviving shapes.

Repeat this enumeration for an eighth vertex. It excludes ten gates on four graphs, including the three length-three paths between two hubs (the theta graph). For each of these graphs a zero pin at the new vertex recovers the nine-gate core. The script tries both choices of shore and all possible sets of neighbors. No scheduling or operand ordering assumption is needed in the insertion step.

**The theta graph costs twelve.** Suppose eleven gates suffice. Each of its six internal vertices has a zero pin equal to the nine-gate core, so has at most two direct readers. With two readers, an AND reader becomes zero and loses a further gate unless its successor is the other reader. That exceptional successor reads both $x$ and $x\land w$ and simplifies to an existing wire for either operation, contrary to minimality. If both readers are OR, undoing their contractions gives two OR insertions of the same new variable into one of the nine optimal cores. The program enumerates these and checks all compatible labelings on all 256 assignments. None computes the theta function. An insertion whose other input is also the new variable is redundant. The inverse includes a second insertion into an earlier inserted gate or one of its input paths.

All six internal variables would thus have a single reader. Consider the earliest gate touching one of them. If both operands are internal raw inputs they form a pair used exclusively through AND or OR, excluded by the pair test above. Otherwise its other operand uses only the two hubs. With both hubs zero that operand is zero, although every internal vertex is essential in the surviving matching of three edges. With both hubs one it is one, although all six internal vertices are essential in their OR. The AND and OR choices both fail. Twelve gates are attained by the four-center construction.

## An unbounded family with one shore of degree at most two

Suppose each nontrivial connected component has a shore in which every degree is at most two. The choice may differ by component. Then the same exact gate count and all four normal-form conclusions hold, with no bound on cover size. Here is an independent induction for this family.

Regard the other shore as the vertices of a simple graph $H$. Each two-neighbor input on the chosen shore subdivides an ordinary edge of $H$. Two such inputs cannot specify the same pair, by the four-cycle assumption. A one-neighbor input is a pendant attached directly to its vertex, which we call a half-edge in this representation. Isolates can be dropped. In an ordinary tree component on $t$ vertices without a half-edge the incidence matching number is $t-1$. Root the tree and match each nonroot vertex to its parent edge. If the component has a cycle or a half-edge the number is $t$. In the first case match cyclically around one cycle and attach the other vertices by tree edges. In the second case match a root to its half-edge and use a rooted spanning tree. These counts are additive across components. Write $U=|E|+\tau-1$ for the star upper bound of a nonempty incidence graph.

**A local form of the two-gate shore bound.** Select among least circuits one maximizing the number of gates whose functions imply $F$. It is lift-fixed. Indeed if an OR $y\lor W$ fails to imply $F$ but the operand $W$ implies it, replace the OR by $y$ at its reads and append an OR of $W$ at the output. Wherever $W=0$ there is no change, and wherever $W=1$ the correct answer is one. All surviving old wire functions only decrease. Every earlier gate that implied $F$ still does, and the new output also implies $F$. This contradicts maximality. Dead gates or repeated operands in any least circuit can be contracted or discarded.

Apply the proof of the shore bound to just one connected component. The earliest reader of its chosen shore, if all its variables are singly read by OR, must have the form $y\lor W$, where $W$ uses none of this component's chosen shore, unless two different raw neighborhoods are funneled. If $W$ implies $F$, the OR is a lift site, since $y$ alone is a negative input. Otherwise take a negative assignment with $W=1$. Clear the component's chosen shore and turn on all its opposite-shore vertices. The assignment is still negative, since it creates no edge in this component or elsewhere. The wire $W$ stays one by independence and monotonicity. Toggling $y$ should now make the output true, but its unique OR reader is already one. Thus some chosen vertex in this component pays two gates on its zero pin. In the subdivision application these neighborhoods are distinct.

**A pendant path of length two costs three additional gates.** Attach $y$ to an old vertex $u$ and a new leaf $v$ to $y$. For old nonconstant function $F$, the result is $F\lor(y\land(u\lor v))$. At each of the two pins of $v$, the residual costs at least $\operatorname{opt}(F)+1$, since $y$ is essential and setting it to zero recovers $F$. For the zero pin use the singleton $u$, which is negative in $F$, as the witness for $y$. In a least circuit for the new function, a direct reader of $v$ becomes constant at its controlling pin. That reader must have a successor because the residual function is nonconstant. Thus one of the two pins saves at least two gates, giving the claimed increase. Three gates suffice. A component consisting of a three-vertex path alone costs two, and adding it disjointly to a nonempty remainder costs three by the independent-star bound.

**Induct on the incidence edge count.** Remove any half-edge whose deletion leaves a cycle or another half-edge in its component. It saves one incidence and preserves the matching number, and the essential-input bound supplies the one gate. The only remaining half-edges lie singly on tree components. A leaf of the ordinary graph away from that half-edge (or any ordinary leaf when there is no half-edge) and its parent subdivision vertex form the pendant path just proved. Their deletion reduces the matching number by one and the incidence count by two. The isolated ordinary edge case is the three-vertex path above. A component with just a half-edge is an isolated two-input AND, handled by the independent-star bound. Forests alone also follow directly from the forest theorem.

The remaining ordinary components have minimum degree two and no half-edge. If one component has more edges than vertices, use the local shore bound on its subdivision vertices. Deleting the selected subdivision vertex removes two incidences and does not reduce the matching number. For a nonbridge the connected ordinary component retains a cycle. For a bridge, each side retains a cycle. A tree on either side would have at least two leaves, although only the bridge endpoint could have lost degree, and a singleton side is incompatible with the former minimum degree. Apply induction to the smaller incidence graph, including its other components.

It remains to handle disjoint unions of subdivided simple cycles. Each cycle has even length $2k\ge6$. Some raw input has at least two readers, since the first gate would otherwise form a two-variable funnel. No two variables in these cycles are twins or an isolated edge. Three readers save three gates on the zero pin. For exactly two readers, if either is AND, its zero pin eliminates a successor as well. If the successor were the other reader, it would combine $x$ with $x\land w$ and would already be redundant. If both readers are OR, the one pin costs four gates unless both have just the same single successor. Neither can feed the other by absorption. A common sole successor would compute $(x\lor a)\mathbin{\circ}(x\lor b)=x\lor(a\mathbin{\circ}b)$ for either operation, saving a gate. Thus one finds a zero pin saving three or a one pin saving four. The zero pin replaces one cycle by a path on $2k-1$ vertices and decreases $U$ by three. The one pin produces two independent alarms and a path on $2k-3$ vertices, so its exact cost by induction and the alarm bound is $U-4$. Both odd-order paths are subdivisions of ordinary paths, choosing the coloring of that component as needed. This completes the induction.

For a direct example, take any finite simple graph, subdivide every edge once, and add any number of new degree-one input vertices on the subdivision shore, each adjacent to an original vertex. All of these incidence functions have a least single-read circuit. Graphs with degree at least three on both shores of a component remain outside this structural theorem.

## Exhaustion at cover size four

The reader and ordinary pin bounds alone attain the upper bound on 46 of the 91 reduced graphs. The three other eight-vertex exclusions and single-vertex restrictions bring the count to 69. The twelve-gate theta result brings it to 77. The two-gate shore bound then gives all 91. In particular, subdividing each edge of $K_4$ with one edge omitted costs thirteen gates. Deleting any subdivision vertex leaves a graph costing eleven, and the five subdivision vertices have distinct neighborhoods. For the full subdivided $K_4$, every such deletion leaves that thirteen-gate graph, so fifteen gates are necessary. Together with the smaller cases this gives all four normal-form properties for any graph of cover number at most four, including any number of duplicated leaves or isolates.

At cover size five the same recurrence attains the star bound for 736 of 1,022 reduced graphs. This includes all 451 graphs meeting the degree-two component condition. The remaining lower bounds fall short by one gate in 272 graphs and by two in 14. These are gaps between certified bounds, not examples of smaller circuits. Pass `--five` to include this larger census.
