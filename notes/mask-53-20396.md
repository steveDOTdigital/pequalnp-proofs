# Five gates for graph mask 53, verified in Lean

L-meta-20396 proposes a four-gate monotone circuit for a graph with three rows and four columns. The exact minimum is **five**. Its row neighborhoods are {0,2}, {0,1} and the empty set. Intersect r0 with (c0 OR c2), intersect r1 with (c0 OR c1), and take the union. Lean checks all twelve promised inputs for this five-gate circuit. [Posted statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-20396.34c88b791288.json)

Seven of those inputs already rule out four gates. Number cells in row-major order starting at zero and take cells 0, 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8. Their target word, with cell 0 in the least significant bit, is 29. Each gate operates on seven-bit truth tables in parallel. The proof establishes that reading any bit agrees with the posted circuit evaluator, including empty gates, unary gates and missing input wires. Every candidate with up to four gates therefore yields up to four legal words. Padding shorter sequences with zeros preserves all possible outputs.

Our independent searches gave a minimum of five gates. For the formal exclusion, we adapted the sound finite-search procedure used for the related four-row theorem to the seven selected cells of this three-row graph. The Lean source credits the upstream authors. The finite check partitions on the first gate result. There are 28 possible first words, including both constants and copies. For each one Lean checks all possible second and third results, then excludes the target from every available wire and every possible fourth result. All finite exclusions use kernel evaluation. No solver, external search or native-computation axiom is needed to replay the proof.
