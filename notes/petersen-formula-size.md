# Exact De Morgan formula size for Petersen minus one vertex

Independent result for desk case L-meta-2956, by steveDOTdigital. The posted truth table has **minimum De Morgan formula size 17 leaves**, or 16 binary gates, with free negations. In the 15-gate circuit question, two gates are NOTs and the first gate supplies the OR of inputs 0 and 2. Even arbitrary reuse of that first signal cannot suffice if every later computed wire is read at most once. Circuits with additional sharing remain open.

## Why negations cannot beat 17 leaves

Push negations to literals by De Morgan's laws. Each of the nine variables is essential. Consider the graph edge uv. Setting all other variables to zero gives the cofactor u AND v. Choose a neighbor of u distinct from v and a neighbor of v distinct from u. These neighbors are distinct and nonadjacent, since the graph has no triangles or four-cycles. Turning them on and all remaining variables off gives u OR v. If u and v each occur just once in a formula, their lowest common ancestor has a fixed operation. On every cofactor depending on both, all branches from these two leaves to that ancestor, and from the ancestor to the output, transmit their values: after restriction, each side branch is a constant. The AND and OR cofactors therefore cannot both occur. The variables occurring at least twice form a vertex cover.

The three minimum covers, each of size five, are {0,1,3,7,8}, {0,2,3,5,6} and {1,2,4,5,8}. The program enumerates all 512 subsets. A smallest formula has no constant or redundant branch. Any variable that appears with both signs must occur at least three times. To prove this, suppose it appears once with each sign and fix all other variables. The two operand subtrees of their lowest common ancestor have monotone unary responses P and N to their respective literals. The ancestor's positive outer context is either constant or the identity. At an OR ancestor replace the negative literal by true. Only the row x=1 can increase. If N(true) causes that increase, the original output was already true at x=0 and is true at x=1 by monotonicity. At an AND ancestor replace the negative literal by false. Only x=0 can decrease. If the original output there was true in an identity context, it must also be true at x=1, so N(false) was already true. Both replacements preserve the full function and remove a leaf by constant folding. A variable used exclusively negatively cannot be essential for an increasing function. The [Lean companion](PetersenFormula.lean) checks the Boolean implication for both ancestor operations.

A formula with at most 16 leaves consequently has at most two variables allowed a negative literal: nine essential variables, at least five repetitions for a cover, and one further occurrence for each mixed-sign variable. If there are two mixed-sign variables, they belong to a common five-vertex cover, since all sixteen occurrences have been accounted for.

## Computing the boundary minimum

Take the twelve edge inputs as positive rows P and the twelve maximal independent sets as negative rows N. The [checker](petersen-formula-rectangles.cpp) compares the graph to all 512 bits of the desk statement before using these rows. For nonempty subsets U of P and V of N, store the least leaf count of a formula true on U and false on V. It is one when an allowed literal distinguishes the entire rectangle. Otherwise take the least of the following sums over proper, nonempty partitions.

* At an OR root, split U into two parts. Each child must reject all of V.
* At an AND root, split V into two parts. Each child must accept all of U.

A formula gives such a partition by assigning each positive or negative row to a witnessing child. If a child receives no rows, the other child suffices. Conversely each split constructs a formula attaining the sum. The two subset masks in increasing numerical order evaluate this recurrence exactly. Its use for signed formulas is a **lower bound on full truth-table size**: a formula computing the whole function also satisfies every boundary test.

For every single variable allowed negative, the boundary minimum is 17. It is also 17 for every pair contained in a common minimum cover. The program tests all nine singles and 36 pairs. Six pairs outside all minimum covers have boundary minimum 13; the occurrence argument above excludes exactly this attempted use of those numbers in a 16-leaf formula. Allowing both signs of all inputs would reduce the boundary minimum to eight. Thus no full formula of size at most 16 exists. For the matching upper bound, assign each edge to one incident vertex of a five-vertex cover. Form the OR of the five stars, each consisting of its center AND the OR of its assigned neighbors. The expression uses twelve neighbor occurrences and five center occurrences. The Lean companion supplies a 17-leaf formula and checks its values on the 512 indexed rows.

## Reusing the prescribed OR

Give a formula the additional input u = x0 OR x2, with arbitrary occurrences permitted. Suppose a formula of at most thirteen leaves computes the function on this promise. Minimize its length. It must use u, because raw inputs alone need seventeen leaves. Each of the other seven raw inputs is independently essential with u fixed by the endpoints. Input 0 is needed with input 2 held high (select neighbor 4), and input 2 is needed with input 0 held high (select neighbor 7). Thus all ten source labels occur.

For edges among the seven nonendpoint variables the earlier AND/OR cofactor argument still applies: the values of 0 and 2, and hence u, are fixed while testing each pair of leaves. These seven vertices form a subdivided three-arm star and have a three-edge matching, so at least three of them occur twice. All thirteen leaves are now accounted for. Neither endpoint nor u can occur more than once. Each nonendpoint variable occurs at most twice; the polarity elimination argument applies because its two values are both allowed with the endpoints fixed. Its negative literal is therefore unnecessary in a minimum formula. The unique occurrence of each endpoint must be positive, since it is essential and increasing with the other endpoint held high, when u remains true. Only u may be negative. Repeating the rectangle calculation on the same twelve-by-twelve boundary with u included, even allowing either sign of u, gives a minimum of **16**. This contradicts thirteen.

If every gate after the first has at most one input-position reader, prune to the output cone. Treat the first output as the free input u. Pushing NOTs down now duplicates no computed subtree. Ignore unused operands of NOT, fold constants (including unavailable-wire reads), and remove copies. The two NOT gates and the first OR leave at most twelve binary gates, hence at most thirteen leaves. A constant output cannot realize the graph. The contradiction proves that a valid circuit must reuse some wire produced after its first gate. This argument does not require a particular position for the first NOT.

To reproduce the counts, compile the C++ source with a C++20 compiler and optimization, then run it. It uses one 16 MiB dynamic-programming table at a time. An exit status of zero also checks the size and membership constraints used in the lower bound.
