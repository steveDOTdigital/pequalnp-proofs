# The 40-input incidence function in case 9834

[Case 9834](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-9834.afec136e603f.json) asks for a fan-in-two AND/OR/NOT circuit of size at most 112. The graph has 21 point inputs, 19 line inputs and 95 incidences. Every line contains five points, and distinct lines have exactly one common point. The following results leave the unrestricted 112-gate question open.

## The exact size for monotone formulas

The minimum is **113 gates**. For each of the 19 lines, join its five incident points with four OR gates and AND the result with the line input. Eighteen OR gates join the 19 stars. Lean checks the resulting gate list against the posted Boolean expression on every input. [Lean verification](OrderFourPunctured9834.lean)

For the lower bound, use one positive row per incident point and line. The negative test for flag `(p,l)` contains the other points of `l` and the other lines through `p`. It is independent because two lines cannot share two points. The rectangle-rank argument gives at least 95 leaves plus the minimum vertex-cover size. Every vertex covers at most five incidences, so a cover needs at least 19 vertices. This gives 114 leaves and 113 gates. The same Lean file verifies the incidence, rank and cover calculation. [General rank argument](flag-cross-rank.md)

### Equality forces the 19 line stars

In a formula with 114 leaves, exactly 19 vertices report their own label on some flag's diagonal rectangle entry. They cover every edge. Since their degrees sum to at most 95, each edge has exactly one reporting endpoint. Connectedness forces those endpoints to be one whole shore, and only the line shore has 19 vertices. Thus each line occurs once and each point occurs once for each of its neighbors.

Use all maximal independent assignments as columns, extending each cross to a maximal independent set. Both endpoints of the cross remain absent since each already has a selected neighbor. The unique leaf for a line spans all five of its flag rows. On every column missing that line, maximality gives an active neighboring point. The corresponding flag must report the line, so the line leaf also spans every column missing it. On a column containing the line, its flag rows must report their individual points.

Fix a point and one of its lines. The negative assignment containing that line and all points outside it is maximal. Among this point's incident flag rows, exactly its row on the chosen line must report the point in this column. This forces a distinct singleton-row leaf for each of its incident lines. Those leaves exhaust the point occurrences.

In the formula partition an OR splits rows and an AND splits columns. A row split cannot divide a line's five rows before its line leaf is separated. A nontrivial column split would have to separate the presence and absence columns for every line still in that group. For two distinct lines, the all-points, all-lines and individual-line columns realize all four membership patterns. Hence until each line stands alone, only row splits can occur. Within each line the column split gives its one AND, followed by the OR tree selecting its five points. Every leaf participates because the leaf lower bound is tight. In particular a 112-gate monotone circuit whose only sharing is one binary gate on raw inputs used exactly twice is impossible. Unfolding would repeat that gate in a minimum formula, while distinct stars share at most one raw point.

## Restricting one shared intermediate signal

Consider a trimmed monotone circuit with just one shared gate output `H`. Let its private input formula cost `h` gates and let it have `r ≥ 2` distinct readers. Setting `H` high removes at least `h+r` gates. Propagating the constant leaves a formula. All 95 positive flags remain accepted. Every independent assignment on which `H` was already high stays negative.

Suppose `H` accepts singleton inputs at a set of `k ≥ 2` vertices on one shore. Its input formula contains each of those variables, so `h ≥ k−1`. In a circuit of size 112, the residual formula has at most `112−k` leaves. It still needs all 40 variables. For example, for a set of selected points, the all-points negative forces each line variable. To force a point, choose an incident line missing at least one selected point. Such a line exists because at most one line contains two specified points and the minimum point degree is three. A flag on the chosen line must be distinguished from the independent pair comprising that line and the selected point outside it. Interchanging the shores gives the line-set argument.

For a retained set of flags, the rank argument gives a lower bound equal to the number of retained flags plus their minimum vertex-cover size. The preceding essentiality proof supplies the leaf needed at each vertex of retained degree one, and also supplies one additional leaf per isolated original vertex. A matching gives the required cover lower bound.

One choice retains every flag whose ordinary cross contains a selected singleton. A second choice can change the negative test. Work separately at each center on the selected shore. If some other selected vertex has no common neighbor with the center, add it to each cross and retain the whole star. Otherwise each selected vertex distinct from the center shares exactly one opposite neighbor with it. If every neighbor is shared in this way, the ordinary crosses already suffice. In the remaining case omit one such shared neighbor and keep the other flags at the center. Adding the selected vertex through the omitted neighbor to the retained cross preserves independence and keeps the helper high. When only one neighbor lacks a witness, keeping the ordinary flags is another choice. These choices can be made independently at the different centers.

The [enumerator](order-four-punctured-helpers.cpp) checks each subset on each shore and computes matching bounds on the retained flags. For nine selected points the lower bound is at least **104 leaves**, and a putative residual has at most 103. For ten selected lines the bound is at least **105 leaves**, against a budget of 102. Therefore a circuit with a sole shared signal and at most 112 gates can have at most **eight singleton points and nine singleton lines** accepted by that signal. Of the nine-line subsets, 144 survive the tests at the 103-leaf boundary. At that boundary the helper must cost exactly eight gates and have exactly two readers. Its eight gates can serve nine different singleton inputs only by combining the nine raw lines with ORs.

The formula optimum and the circuit construction are kernel-checked. The equality classification and single-helper bounds use the written arguments above and, for the subset counts, the C++ enumeration.
