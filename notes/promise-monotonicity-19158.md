# Removing NOT on one-per-side graph inputs

[L-meta-19158](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-19158) asks whether every size-s AND/OR/NOT circuit for an n by n promise graph, n at least 3, has an AND/OR replacement with at most s+2n−5 gates. [L-meta-19159](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-19159) posts its exact negation. The assignment title mentions small cases; its Lean quantifier covers every natural n at least three.

We establish the bound for **all 512 three-by-three matrices** and **all 65,536 four-by-four matrices**. This proves it for every n when there are at most four distinct row patterns and four distinct column patterns. The bound also holds whenever either side has at most three distinct patterns, regardless of the number on the other side. The bound is attained for every n. The unrestricted statement is open.

## Compression when both sides have duplicate patterns

Take k representative row groups and k representative column groups, for 3≤k≤n, with all rows identical within each row group and all columns identical within each column group. The graph is constant on each rectangle of this partition. On inputs using only one chosen representative of each group, the original circuit gives a circuit for the k by k quotient at the same size: replace unused raw coordinates by false, renumber the other raw and internal wires, and preserve the output selector. An out-of-range wire supplies false without an extra gate, including for an original invalid reference.

Conversely, compute one OR of the raw indicators in each group. Across both sides this costs exactly 2(n−k) binary OR gates. Rewire a monotone circuit on the quotient to read those group indicators. In particular, a bound of s+2k−5 on the quotient gives s+2n−5 on the original graph.

When n≥4 and each side has at most four distinct patterns, split equal-pattern groups if necessary to obtain exactly four groups on each side and apply the four-by-four result. More generally, a counterexample of smallest order would have n≥5 and either all rows distinct or all columns distinct. With duplicates on both sides one could merge one pair on each side and use the smaller-order bound.

## Rectangular quotients with three column patterns

The same conversion works for arbitrary n if either side has at most three distinct patterns. By transposition take that side to be the columns. Two column types permit at most four row types, already covered. With exactly three column types, the number p of distinct row types is at most eight. For p≥5 keep one representative of each row and column type. Compression to a p by 3 rectangle preserves the unrestricted size. Expansion costs (n−p)+(n−3) OR gates. It therefore suffices to bound the monotone cost of the rectangle by s+p−2.

A nonmonotone representation must read at least p−1 row bits and two column bits, since all types are different. Its binary output cone needs at least p binary gates, and at least one NOT is paid, so s≥p+1. For most rectangles a monotone upper bound of 2p−1 now suffices. Compute each column's row set by OR, gate it with the matching column bit, and OR the results. If an all-ones row is present, remove it from the column sums and OR its raw bit into the final output. This costs at most W+2 gates, where W is the sum of the row weights except that an all-ones row contributes one. Empty column terms can be omitted. The three two-bit patterns have adjusted weight two, four patterns have adjusted weight one and the empty pattern has weight zero. In particular W+2≤2p−1 for p≥6.

For p=5 there are six exceptions to that numerical inequality. Each has all three weight-two rows and two of the four adjusted-weight-one rows. Up to permuting columns these are the row-mask sets {1,2,3,5,6} and {1,3,5,6,7}. The construction costs at most ten. A nonmonotone circuit with s≤6 would need five binary gates, one NOT, and all six of its distinct raw inputs in a tree. The replay enumerates all formulas of at most six gates and excludes both masks. Hence s≥7, giving ten within s+p−2. This covers all 93 choices of distinct row patterns with p from five through eight. If the original circuit is monotone, it itself gives the required replacement.

Consequently, a smallest-order counterexample would have n≥5, all patterns distinct on at least one side, and at least four distinct patterns on the other.

## Sharpness at every order

Fix a row i and a column j. Require that neither endpoint is the specified one. OR their two raw indicators and negate the result: two gates suffice. In a monotone circuit every one of the n−1 other row inputs must occur in the output cone. Otherwise changing that ignored row to row i, at a fixed column other than j, can only increase the raw bits that the cone sees, yet must change true to false. The same holds for the n−1 other columns. Connecting 2n−2 essential raw leaves needs at least 2n−3 binary gates. Two OR trees for the allowed row and column indicators, followed by their conjunction, attain this. Thus the overhead 2n−5 cannot be reduced even by one. This agrees with the posted [monotone lower bound](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-19155).

In fact the conversion holds for every circuit of size at most two. A nonmonotone circuit of this size has at most one binary operation. Push negations to its raw arguments. Each complemented row or column indicator is the OR of the other n−1 inputs on that side and costs n−2 gates. Two such complements and one operation cost at most 2n−3, within the bound for s=2. A single NOT costs at most n−2 monotone gates, within the bound for s=1. Constants and copies fold.

## Three-by-three verification

On the promise, a wire is exactly a nine-bit truth table. The [formula and orbit replay](promise-monotonicity-19158.cjs) generates minimum formula costs by combining smaller-cost tables with AND and OR, optionally also NOT. An invalid output gives zero at no cost; an empty AND gives one at cost one. This construction supplies a monotone upper bound of at most seven for all 512 tables after one improvement: for each three-label inequality table, pair each matched row and column by OR and test whether at least two of the three signals are true. This takes three plus four gates.

Consequently an unrestricted circuit of size six or larger already satisfies the required bound. For sizes through five the replay enumerates circuits, not just formulas, by adjoining one previously unavailable truth table to an unordered set of existing intermediates. Each new table is an AND, OR or NOT on available wires, with arbitrary reuse. Every least circuit for a nonconstant target can be generated this way. Folding constants and copies cannot increase size. Including the raw and constant targets, 434 tables occur by size five. At each first occurrence the monotone upper bound is at most that size plus one. Constant and raw outputs satisfy the bound directly.

## Four-by-four verification

Relabel rows and columns independently and allow transposition. The 65,536 masks form 192 orbits, and both circuit measures are unchanged within each orbit. For each representative compute the minimum unrestricted formula size F and construct a monotone circuit with bound M. Start with minimum monotone formula size, then allow a paid monotone helper as a reusable leaf in a formula. The representatives of the helper operations used are an OR on two same-side inputs, an AND on one input from each side, and an OR on one input from each side. Minimize over the matrix orbit. This gives upper bounds without assuming that other circuit forms are impossible.

Five explicit improvements are checked on all sixteen cells by the [bounded-synthesis replay](promise-monotonicity-19158.py). Mask 31710 is four-label inequality: four paired indicators followed by a seven-gate test that at least two are true. Mask 31711 is three-label inequality extended by a universal row and column, costing nine gates. Masks 13278 and 15838 are three-label inequality with duplicated row or column classes. Building three paired indicators over eight raw bits costs five gates total, and the threshold costs four more. For mask 14302 use row and column groups {0}, {1}, {2,3}, giving this nine-gate inequality circuit, then OR in the single cell (2,2) for two additional gates.

Every representative has M≤F+3, so no formula violates its bound. A candidate counterexample must use at least one NOT and share an intermediate. Let r be its number of distinct raw inputs, b its binary gates, and k its NOTs. A live cone has 2b+k input edges and r+b+k vertices. If b=r−1, the graph is a tree and the circuit is a formula. Hence a candidate has b≥r and size at least r+1. Its unread rows must all have the same signature; the analogous condition holds on the columns. These give a lower bound on r from the largest duplicate class on either side.

The program tests every remaining support, size s from r+1 through M−4, and NOT count from one through s−r. On each input row the SAT constraints select two earlier wires and an AND, OR or unary NOT and enforce the exact truth table. Every satisfying witness would invalidate this upper/lower comparison and is reported for further work. All 206 bounded searches completed without a candidate. As a check near the threshold, mask 14302 *does* have an eight-gate unrestricted witness. That is why its eleven-gate monotone construction matters. The remaining comparison for that mask is only through size seven.

Run `node promise-monotonicity-19158.cjs` for the formula and orbit calculations. Run `python promise-monotonicity-19158.py` with Python-SAT installed for the full four-by-four verification. These are external finite calculations. They establish small orders and the compression consequence, while graphs of arbitrary order with more distinct patterns remain unresolved.
