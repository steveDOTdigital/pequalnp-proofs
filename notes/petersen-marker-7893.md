# A marked wire on the Petersen graph

L-meta-7893 asks whether adding a marked wire to input zero can make a 16-to-18-gate AND/OR circuit pass the listed Petersen tests. All gates **through the production of the marked wire** are forbidden from reading raw input zero. Later gates may read zero. Thus the marked value H is independent of input zero, and the substitution is feed-forward. The following partial result excludes every such circuit with a flat ordinary output cone. A legal **19-gate** example attains this bound; the [replay](petersen-marker-7893.py) checks it on all 1,024 inputs. The full assignment allows nested ANDs in the ordinary output.

The 15 positive masks are the Petersen edges and the 15 negative masks are exactly its maximal independent sets. A monotone function that passes these tests computes the edge function everywhere. Of the negatives, the ten without vertex zero are the prescribed cold tests of H. The neighbors of zero are 1, 4 and 5. All nine other singletons are contained in cold tests. Among independent inputs without zero only the two triples 200 and 772 are contained in no cold test. All these finite facts are checked directly from the desk masks.

Call the unmodified circuit C and the corrected output C*. Since C* dominates C, C rejects every independent set. It already accepts the three edges through zero, where the substitution does nothing. An edge missed by C must be accepted by H on that edge. Inserting a single OR of zero and H immediately after H, and directing all later uses of raw zero to the OR, gives a usual acyclic monotone circuit computing the full graph. Earlier gates retain their operands because they do not read zero. If the original has at most 18 gates, this construction has at most 19. Our [independent ordinary-circuit lower bound](petersen-unary-input.md) forces exactly 19, hence the original must have exactly 18. The new circuit is minimum, so all its gates are live, binary and irredundant. A marked input wire cannot qualify: each nonzero input is true on a cold test. The inserted gate is the unique reader of raw zero, operates by OR, and its other operand must fire on a missed edge. Our independent 19-gate *lower bound* for ordinary circuits uses an exhaustive calculation outside Lean.

## Excluding a live marker that rejects both exceptional triples

Suppose H is used in the ordinary output cone of C and rejects both exceptional triples. Then H is false on every independent input: clamp zero, extend to an independent cold test unless the clamped row is one of the two exceptional triples, and use monotonicity. In the ordinary circuit, cut every use of H to zero and OR the retained H with the residual output. The result rejects every independent set. It accepts each edge on which H is true. On all other edges, the original substitution changes nothing and cutting also leaves the ordinary output unchanged. The new circuit costs at most as many gates as C: at least one immediate reader simplifies, paying for the final OR. If H itself is the output, the substitution can never change that output. Both cases contradict the ordinary 19-gate lower bound.

Consequently a live marker in any hypothetical witness must accept 200 or 772 as well as some edge. All proper subsets of those independent triples lie inside cold tests. The marker therefore depends on each of the three triple variables. At least one additional variable is essential because the marker accepts an edge and no subset of the triple of size two. In particular its cone contains at least three binary gates. This criterion does not require a flat ordinary output.

## Flat ordinary output with arbitrary preprocessing and arbitrary marker

Assume that the **ordinary live output cone** has no AND feeding a later AND. Allow arbitrary sharing, and arbitrary gates outside this cone for the marked wire. After unfolding the final ORs symbolically, each term is a product of two raw disjunctions. There is no raw output disjunct because singletons are independent. Both sides of a nonempty product are disjoint, again by singleton rejection. Every cross pair must be an edge, since C rejects all independent sets. With no four-cycle in the graph a nonempty product is an ordinary star, including a single edge. Constant, unary or repeated operands can be simplified. No structural restriction is imposed on the marked wire's own cone.

Let R be the set of edges missed by the ordinary output and k its cardinality. If R is empty then C computes the graph and the ordinary lower bound applies. On any e in R the augmented input must contain an ordinary accepted edge. The only added vertex is zero, and e itself is missed. Therefore e contains one of 1, 4, 5. There are exactly six possible missed edges, two centered at each of these neighbors. Every missed edge must turn on the marker. A live ordinary star product or a subsequent OR of products vanishes on every missed edge. A raw preprocessing OR that hits a missed edge would also hit a singleton in a cold test. Thus a useful marker must come from outside the ordinary cone.

Choose a center for each ordinary product and let r count distinct chosen centers. Put m = 15−k. The ordinary output requires at least m+r−1 gates. To see this, group neighbor disjunctions by center. At a center whose terms collectively use d distinct neighbors and t products, the neighbor cones require at least d−t OR gates, by counting leaves in binary cones with at most t roots. Distinct centers cannot share one of these effective ORs because their neighborhoods meet in at most one vertex. Redundant singleton copies can be bypassed. The sum of the d values is at least m, and there is one AND per product and at least one fewer output OR than products. This gives at least m plus the number of products minus one, as claimed.

Restrict the auxiliary marker calculation to endpoints of R, with zero clamped low. Give it every possible ordinary-cone source for free. The raw literals suffice for individual inputs. For each chosen center add disjunctions of every subset of its neighbors on **ordinary accepted** edges, intersected with the retained endpoints. This covers intermediate raw preprocessing wires as well as their roots. Every ordinary product and every output OR of products is zero on R and the clamped negative tests and can be replaced by zero there. Pinning other raw inputs low cannot make a negative turn on. On R the projection is exact. Thus the relaxed marker has at most B = size−(m+r−1) further binary gates and at most B+1 external source wires in its live cone. At least one gate is needed because no single nonzero raw disjunction can meet R and reject the cold tests.

For each missing edge and each cold negative, at least one chosen source must be true on the edge and false on the negative. Otherwise monotonicity makes the two required marker outputs incompatible. The replay enumerates the 63 nonempty subsets of the six possible edges and all center sets covering the remaining edges. For each it builds the generous free-source list above. It finds the exact minimum number satisfying the necessary constraints by branching on every possible source in a remaining constraint. **No configuration survives at 18 gates.** At 19, the only survivors have two missing edges at one neighbor of zero, five ordinary centers and two gates left for the marker. They are related by symmetries fixing zero.

For the representative at vertex 5, take the marker to be `x5 AND (x7 OR x8)` and put its two gates first. Use ordinary stars with centers 0, 2, 3, 6 and 9 and respective neighbor sets {1,4,5}, {1,3,7}, {4,8}, {1,8,9}, {4,7}. Combine the five products by OR. Without augmentation the two edges at masks 160 and 288 are missed. Either edge turns on the marker, which enables the ordinary zero-star through vertex 5. Every cold negative turns off the marker. The replay checks legal gate indices, the prefix restriction and the complete truth table directly.

The general case must have a nested AND in C's ordinary output. The marked wire can also be absent from that ordinary cone if it supplies the inserted OR. The flat classification and the sharp example do not decide the unrestricted 18-gate claim.
