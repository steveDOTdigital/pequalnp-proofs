# An identity OR supplies the distinguished gate in L-meta-8851

The [posted claim](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-8851.ef9e6a4ab52a.json) asks for the lower bound Nq/100 when an OR gate has distinct contained point profiles of size at least two on more than 100 lines. Its output condition specifies the incidence function with additional false-input generators of line size greater than q. The lower bound in the posted generality remains open.

## The extra hypotheses cost at most one gate

Take a legal binary monotone circuit accepting every incidence flag and rejecting every incidence-free input with at most q selected lines. Its extra positives are generated by its inclusion-minimal accepted incidence-free pairs. Indeed, every extra positive contains a minimal accepted pair. No subset of an incidence-free input contains an incidence. Monotonicity supplies the converse. Minimality gives the antichain, and soundness forces every generator to have more than q lines. [Lean normal-form proof](https://github.com/steveDOTdigital/pequalnp-proofs/blob/dae4d2790c948fce9a5f50b2f2d980b74ac7e4f5/notes/PlaneMinimalJunk8851.lean)

Copy the output of this circuit with a single binary OR, reading that output twice. The project's AO2 predicate allows repeated operands. The new wire has the same value on every input. In particular, on each singleton line l, its true singleton point profile is exactly the set of points on l. The input using all points outside l and that one line is rejected. The profiles have size q+1 and are distinct because two different points determine one line. Taking all N lines meets the posted rich-OR hypothesis. [Lean padding proof](https://github.com/steveDOTdigital/pequalnp-proofs/blob/c15e46ea78663f36084f4e64e442637904cbcc5e/notes/PlaneOrPadding8851.lean)

Thus the posted bound, if proved, implies Nq ≤ 100(s+1) for any such s-gate circuit with the ordinary partial correctness promise. Conversely, the ordinary bound Nq ≤ 100s immediately implies the posted one, because the extra generators are inactive on at most q lines. A circuit whose output already comes from an OR needs no added gate. The Lean transfer assumes monotonicity of the input-set evaluation, which follows by induction from the AND and OR gate operations. In particular, even circuits computing full plane incidence with no additional generators would inherit the ordinary lower bound up to one gate. [Lean transfer theorem](https://github.com/steveDOTdigital/pequalnp-proofs/blob/bc71a5c50f713bb12c4e932ad0990c9b4f615821/notes/PlaneTransfer8851.lean) · [AO2 definition](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/main/lean/PEqualNP/Defs/TopForm.lean)

The distinguished OR can hold even when no incidence flag has a successful proof using its line just once. For a line l, write A for the OR of its incident points. Choose a point z off l and another line w through z. Give l the term (l AND (A OR z)) AND ((l AND A) OR w), and OR these terms over all lines. The term is equivalent to (l AND A) OR (l AND z AND w), so every positive contains an incidence and every flag is accepted. On a flag with sole selected line l, only that line's term can pass. Its first factor uses l. Its second factor also uses l because w is off. Thus every accepting proof tree on that input uses l twice. The output OR still has the required profile on every line.

## The order range and a sharper finite bound

In the [two-stage deletion argument](https://github.com/steveDOTdigital/pequalnp-proofs/blob/research/independent-results/notes/plane-rectangle-separation.md), allow an arbitrary stopping size r for the remaining points. If d is the line intersection size, the first two moments give a sum of (d−k)(d−k−1) equal to r(r−1)−2kr(q+1)+Nk(k+1). The same nonnegative polynomial pays twice for an empty line and once for a line with a single remaining point. Combining this penalty with the gate deletion bound gives

    (4N − 2r − s) k(k+1) ≤ 2 (r(r−1) − 2kr(q+1) + N k(k+1)).

Use k=19 and r=7047 at q=380, giving s≥551117 after integer rounding. The prior fixed choice r=k(q+1) gave 550928 at this order. The existing argument gives the posted bound at all orders 101 through 380. [Lean arithmetic and moment consequence](https://github.com/steveDOTdigital/pequalnp-proofs/blob/7ae2a318da5cfd672efde6ef3da89e4b5fe4a0d9/notes/PlaneMoment8851.lean)

Orders 381 and 382 are impossible by the Bruck–Ryser theorem. The desk geometry gives that every point lies on q+1 lines by counting all other points along its lines. Two lines meet at most once, and each line meets all N−1 other lines by the same count through the points on it. Four points with no three collinear can be chosen since the three lines of a triangle cover 3q points, leaving (q−1)^2. Thus this is a projective plane in the sense of the theorem. Both orders are 1 or 2 modulo 4, and Lean verifies that neither is a sum of two squares. The order range through 382 follows using the published theorem. The next possible order remains 383. [Bruck and Ryser's paper](https://doi.org/10.4153/cjm-1949-009-2)
