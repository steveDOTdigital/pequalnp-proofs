# Triangle tests for the restricted order-three incidence search

Independent work by steveDOTdigital on L-meta-1979. The positive tests are the 52 individual incidences of the order-three plane. A permitted negative is a nonincident pair of sets (A,B), with at most two selected points on each line and at most two selected lines through each point. Such a point set is an arc, and the line condition is its dual. The goal is a monotone circuit of at most 63 binary gates. The 64-gate star circuit supplies a control.

## An exact set of 520 tests

For a triangle T of three noncollinear points let E(T) be the set of all lines missing it. Test the negative row (T,E(T)); also test its dual for every nonconcurrent triple of lines. These 468 negatives and the 52 positives suffice.

Three noncollinear points meet 9 lines, so four lines miss them. No three of those four can be concurrent: all three points would have to lie on the fourth line through the alleged common point. The four missing lines therefore form a dual arc. Four points with no three collinear meet 10 lines, so three lines miss them. Those three are likewise nonconcurrent. An arc has at most four points. Indeed, five points with no three collinear would give ten distinct secants. There would be 15 intersections of pairs of these secants away from the five points: each four-point subset gives three pairs of disjoint point pairs. At any one external point there are at most two secants, because the pairs of selected points must be disjoint. There are only eight external points. The same reasoning applies on the dual side.

If A is a triangle, B is contained in E(A). If A has four points, let T be the three lines missing A. Then B is contained in T and A lies among the points missing T. Exchange sides if B has at least three members. If both sides have at most two, grow A to a triangle while continuing to avoid B. The lines in B exclude at most eight points. When A has two points, also exclude their joining line, of size four. When A has zero or one point, exclude A itself. In either case a permissible new point exists among the thirteen. These steps cover every allowed negative with a test row. Conversely all test rows are permitted. They are maximal: the exterior of a triangle is a quadrangle and the exterior of that quadrangle is exactly its original triangle, by the counts just proved.

There are 234 triangles on each shore: of the 286 triples, 52 are collinear. The [enumerator](plane-arc-tests.py) independently constructs all 12,234 permitted negatives and verifies that their 468 maximal elements are exactly the stated tests. Since the circuit is monotone, falsity on these elements implies falsity below them.

## Why two unfinished ordinary stars cannot save a gate

Fix any two lines X and Y. Their union consists of a common point o, three private points on X and three on Y. Allow every OR of a private point on X and a private point on Y as a free source, in addition to all nine raw inputs (the seven points and two lines). This relaxation includes every point-OR that can survive from an ordinary star on another line once points outside the union have been fixed to zero. The restricted problem has eight required incidences. Even on the relaxed negative tests it takes **exactly nine further gates**.

Suppose an eight-gate circuit exists. Simplify constants and copies, discard unused gates, and consider its live output cone. With fan-in at most two a connected cone of eight gates uses at most nine distinct sources. Both line bits and o must be read, since they are the only sources capable of distinguishing a required positive at o from the corresponding negative singleton. At most six distinct sources remain for the six private raw points and the nine possible cross ORs.

If private point a on X is omitted as a raw source, at least three selected helper ORs must contain a. Otherwise take one private point of Y from each selected helper containing a, at most two points. These points together with line X make a permitted negative. On every selected source its bits dominate those of the required positive (a,X). Monotonicity contradicts the required outputs. The argument also holds for a private point on Y. With k omitted raw points and h selected helpers this gives 3k ≤ 2h, since each helper contains two points. The six-source budget gives h ≤ k. Hence k=h=0.

Exactly the nine raw inputs now feed at most eight gates, so the live circuit is a read-once binary formula. Fix all but a private point a on X, the common point o and the two lines to zero and simplify. The graph of required true pairs is the induced path a-X-o-Y. Such a path cannot be the positive-pair graph of a monotone read-once formula false on singletons. A root OR gives a disjoint union on the disjoint operand variables. A root AND gives a complete bipartite graph between the singleton-positive inputs of its two operands, together with possible isolated vertices. A four-vertex path is connected, has no isolated vertices and is not complete bipartite. A constant or a single variable cannot realize it either. The usual two stars joined by an OR achieve nine gates.

This also excludes any prefix consisting of eleven ordinary line stars in a 63-gate witness. The prefix costs 44 gates. In the remaining live circuit each completed line must be observed through its raw bit or its star output: toggling that line with a single incident point changes the required answer. After fixing those eleven line bits to zero, at least eleven distinct live source wires of the suffix become constant. Simplifying a live two-input circuit at k source wires that become constant deletes at least k gates if the output stays nonconstant. In the original live DAG, choose one reader for each fixed source and each constant gate it reaches. Follow these paths until their first nonconstant gates. A constant gate can join at most two such paths; each path component contributes one more deleted gate at its nonconstant frontier, where the second operand survives and the gate becomes a copy. Distinct components can use the same frontier only if both operands are constant, which would put it inside the constant region instead. This charges at least one deletion per fixed source, including sources made irrelevant by an earlier controlling value. After fixing points outside X and Y to zero, the surviving point-only helpers are raw, constant, or the free cross ORs already allowed. At most eight gates would remain, a contradiction. The dual argument excludes eleven ordinary point stars.

## Search with three unfinished lines

For the nonconcurrent lines (0,1,3), the local instance has nine raw points, three line bits, 12 positives and 56 maximal projected negatives. For the concurrent lines (0,1,5) the counts are ten points, three lines, 12 positives and 81 negatives. A thirteen-gate residual reads at most fourteen distinct sources. Each selected line is mandatory. The enumerator lists all sets of raw points and available helper ORs within the remaining source budget that distinguish each positive row from every negative. With `--pairing -1` it supplies **every** pair and triple available from the ten completed lines, including pair choices that might be incompatible in a single real prefix. Thus an exclusion in this model also applies to actual prefixes.

In the concurrent case no private or common raw point can be omitted. In the triangle case there are only twelve source sets omitting a raw point, and all twelve are equivalent under the 24 tested permutations preserving the local inputs, helpers, and rows. The [exact synthesis program](search-plane-arc-residual.py) returned certificate-checked UNSAT for the representative `--pairing -1 --omitted 1 --helpers 1029 --gates 13`. These sets have fourteen sources, so thirteen is the smallest gate count compatible with reading them all. The nine-gate, two-line positive control returned SAT. Searches with every raw point and no free helper reached their 150-second limits for both three-line shapes. The unrestricted 63-gate existence question remains open.
