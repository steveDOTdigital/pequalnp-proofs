# How much sharing would a 26-gate Boolean matrix product need?

[L-meta-17823](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-17823) asks for all nine entries of a 3 by 2 times 2 by 3 Boolean product using at most 26 fan-in-two AND, OR and NOT gates. The [27-gate construction](MatrixProduct17823.lean) passed Lean 4.36.0 using the project evaluator and fan-in definition. A separate test checked all nine outputs on all 4,096 inputs. We obtain a necessary condition on any saving. Draw a bipartite graph with the three rows on one side and three columns on the other. Mark a cell when its output depends syntactically on more than three gates, counting its entire cone. **In any circuit with at most 26 gates, the marked cells must contain a cycle.** The exact 12-gate lower bound for the 2 by 2 product strengthens this: at least six output cones must each contain four or more gates. This leaves the posted claim open.

## A single cell

Write its four bits as a,b,p,q. Its value is (a AND p) OR (b AND q). All four variables are essential. A connected cone with four essential variables needs at least three binary gates. At equality there is no unary gate, extra variable, constant, repeated leaf or shared intermediate. The cone is a read-once tree.

Its root must be OR. At an AND root the variables split into two nonempty disjoint branches. The minimal true sets of a conjunction on disjoint supports are all pairwise unions of minimal true sets in the two branches. To yield the two disjoint pairs {a,p} and {b,q}, neither branch could have just one minimal true set: such a set would lie in both pairs. Both branches would therefore need at least two minimal true sets, giving at least four unions. At an OR root, each true pair must lie wholly in one branch, which forces the two AND products and their OR. Direct enumeration of all 960 ordered four-leaf trees with AND/OR internal gates gave only the eight orderings of this one tree. Different cells have distinct product gates and distinct output gates. They may share raw inputs.

## A forest of requested cells costs three gates per cell

For a set F of row-column pairs, test a circuit only on inputs that are zero outside the two row bits and two column bits of a *single* pair from F. All sixteen local assignments for every pair are allowed. The circuit must still produce the correct output for every cell in F on this whole test domain. A circuit meeting this weaker requirement needs at least three gates per edge whenever F is a forest. Constants and unrestricted sharing are allowed.

Take a leaf vertex, its unique incident edge e, and the leaf's private inputs u,v. On e's four-variable cube, varying u,v produces four distinct functions of the other endpoint's bits p,q: zero, p, q and p OR q. There must be at least two different gates reading u or v. With no reader, the output would be independent of u and v. With one reader, both essential inputs would have to enter that same gate, leaving one bit to distinguish those four functions. The output cannot be a raw wire because it depends on all four inputs.

The output gate for e is distinct from both readers. A direct raw input to a binary AND or OR would make one setting of it force a constant output, whereas both settings of u and of v leave a nonconstant function on e's cube. A unary gate with such an input could depend on only one variable. This argument also covers empty or repeated pin lists and missing wires, which supply constants or copies under the project evaluator.

Set u=v=0. Every gate reading either raw bit now reduces to a constant or a copy, saving at least two gates. The output for e is zero on the remaining test domain. Replacing its gate by zero everywhere, including downstream uses, saves a third distinct gate. The resulting circuit realizes the forest with e removed. Induction from the empty forest gives the lower bound. Three gates per requested cell attain it.

## Why the 2 by 2 product needs exactly 12 gates

Consider the full product on r rows and c columns, with r,c at least two and inner dimension two. Every raw bit remains essential to the vector of outputs after fixing any other raw bit to either value. For a left-matrix bit, choose an output in its row. If the fixed bit belongs to the right matrix, choose another column; otherwise any column works. Enable the selected lane of that column and disable its other lane. This output now copies the chosen raw bit, irrespective of the fixed value. Transpose the argument for a right-matrix bit.

Count distinct gates that read raw bits. Use effective operand positions: for NOT only the first position counts. Let d(x) be the number of different such gates reading raw bit x. Every raw bit is essential, and none of the nine target functions (or four in the 2 by 2 case) is a raw wire. Thus d(x) is positive. If a gate reads two different raw bits x and y, it must be AND or OR. Fixing y to its controlling value forces that gate to a constant. Therefore d(x) cannot be one, since x remains essential under that restriction. The same holds for y. Give each reader of x a charge of 1/d(x). A gate reading just one distinct raw bit receives at most one unit. A gate reading two receives at most a half from each. There must be at least as many raw-reading gates as there are raw bits.

No target output gate can itself read a raw bit in an effective position. For an AND or OR, one fixed value of that input would make the target constant, but every output remains nonconstant under both values of each individual bit. A NOT reading a raw bit could depend on only that bit. The rc outputs are pairwise distinct, so their gates are distinct and additional. The lower bound is **2(r+c)+rc**. It gives **21** for the assigned product and **12** for the 2 by 2 product. The latter is exact, as three gates per output attain it.

## Consequences for the full product

Suppose S is the set of cells with a three-gate cone. Their 3|S| gates are pairwise disjoint. Let F be the other cells. On every single-cell test from F, all gates of S evaluate to zero: every product in S uses either another row or another column. Replacing them by zero preserves correctness on that domain. If F were a forest, the remaining circuit would need at least 3|F| gates. Together these give at least 27, proving the cycle condition. Of the 512 sets of cells, 328 are forests and 184 contain cycles. Exactly 81 forests have five edges, the maximum.

If exactly four cells have cones larger than three gates, they must be the four cells of a 2 by 2 rectangle. Pin the unused row and column to zero. Every gate in the other five three-gate cones becomes zero, now on the entire remaining eight-input cube. Removing their 15 gates would give an at-most-11-gate circuit for the four entries of a 2 by 2 Boolean product. Conversely any such 11-gate circuit would extend to a 26-gate circuit by adding three gates for each of the other five cells.

Consequently exactly four larger cones cannot suffice. Five cannot suffice either. If five marked cells include a cycle it is a 2 by 2 rectangle plus one other edge. Test on the union of the full eight-input cube of that rectangle and the four-input cube of the extra edge. All three gates of every unmarked cell vanish throughout this domain. The extra edge has an endpoint outside the rectangle that occurs nowhere else among the marked cells. The leaf argument removes its two raw readers and its output gate. On the rectangle the four surviving outputs then need at least 12 gates. The total is at least 12 gates for the four unmarked cells, three for the extra edge and 12 for the rectangle. **Any circuit using at most 26 gates must therefore have at least six larger output cones, including a cycle.**

The forest argument alone gives a lower bound of **19** for the nine-output problem. Choose any five-edge spanning tree. Each of the four other target outputs is identically zero on its single-cell tests. All nine target functions are distinct and nonliteral on the full input cube, so the four other output gates can be replaced by zero. The five tree outputs require at least 15 surviving gates.

The checked examples and these lower bounds give neither a 26-gate witness nor an unrestricted exclusion at 26. No upstream filing or additional proved-lemma count follows from this assignment so far.
