# Checking a single-tape run by its visits to tape intervals

For the restricted case of L-lib-123 in which the original decider is a deterministic **single-tape, single-head** machine, a computation of T ≥ 2 steps admits an existential commitment and a universal check of cost O(T^(2/3) polylog T) on a multitape verifier, apart from reading the original input. The tape alphabet and the machine are fixed. The verifier may ignore a third existential block. The project definition of FP3 quantifies over general finite multistack machines. This argument gives a restricted protocol and four Lean-checked lemmas about its accounting and local replay. [Lean lemmas](../lean/PEqualNP/Outside/LocalVisitVerification.lean)

## What the prover commits to

Divide the tape into consecutive intervals of B = ceil(T^(1/3)) cells. There are B possible offsets for the partition. A single left or right step crosses a boundary for exactly one offset, and a stationary step crosses none. Summing over offsets gives at most T crossings. Choose an offset with at most floor(T/B) crossings.

Commit to the resulting chronological list of visits. Each record gives a duration and the state and direction needed to pass from that interval to its neighbor. The first position and initial state are public. The visit list takes O((1 + T/B) log T) bits; all relevant time and tape indices fit in O(log T) bits. Use a fixed horizon, making halting states stationary, and require the public final state to accept. A crossing on the very last step passes its resulting state directly to the terminal check.

Within each interval count only the steps spent there. Place a checkpoint every H = ceil(T^(2/3)) local steps, omitting the last one if it ends the run in that interval. Each checkpoint stores the B tape symbols and the head position and control state needed to resume inside a visit. At an exact visit boundary the tape is the private data carried to the next visit; reentry side and state come from the public visit list. Write checkpoints in order of interval index and local step count. If the visit totals are tᵢ, at most Σ floor(tᵢ/H) ≤ T/H checkpoints are needed over the entire tape. Their storage is O((T/H)(B + log T)). In particular there is no table of every interval at every global time cut.

## What one universal challenge checks

The challenge selects an interval and a local chunk number. Global syntax checks validate the offset, the crossing count, the durations summing to T, the neighboring interval transitions and the public initial and final conditions. Checkpoint records must be uniquely keyed and properly ordered. One scan of the public ledger suffices to extract the visits of the challenged interval and calculate its cumulative local times. For a valid chunk, reject missing required checkpoints. Invalid challenge indices can be ignored after the global checks. There are enough challenge strings to name every actual chunk.

Load the starting checkpoint, or initialize the B cells from the input and blanks if this is the first chunk. Replay at most H local steps. During a visit, require every recorded exit to occur at the specified step and side, with the specified new state. Reject any unrecorded exit. Between two visits to the same interval, intervening single-head moves lie on one side of it. The head therefore returns to the boundary cell at which it last left. No scan across B cells is needed at each reentry. The intervening control state can change; the new entry state is taken from the ledger and is independently checked by its predecessor visit. At the end of a chunk check the claimed next checkpoint, including its tape contents. The last chunk simply finishes its remaining visits.

An honest run supplies every record. Conversely, if all challenges pass, chunks of each interval agree at their shared endpoints and reproduce that interval's entire local tape history. Follow the public visits in chronological order. At each entry the local tape is the one left on the previous visit to this interval, and the entering state is exactly the prior interval's verified exit state. Determinism gives the actual trajectory and the claimed accepting outcome. The Lean file proves composition of checked chunks, projection of a chronological replay onto one interval, and the two counting inequalities. The single-tape model and its multitape verifier have not been implemented in Lean.

## Cost and scope

On a multitape verifier the challenged tape can be represented by two stacks around its head. A replay step takes constant work plus O(log T) for counters. A constant number of sequential scans of the ledger and checkpoint records, initializing or comparing a B-cell checkpoint, and replaying at most H steps take O((1 + T/B + (T/H)(B + log T) + H + input length) polylog T) time including bookkeeping. Scanning padding costs linear time in the padding. For example, for T = O(2^(6m)) on m-bit inputs, this fits O(2^(5m)) with room for logarithmic factors. A fixed multiplicative constant in T can be absorbed into the budget or the machine description. These parameters can equally be chosen as powers of two known from m.

In the project's verifier format all three guess blocks have the prescribed length, and its deterministic relation must run in linear time even on arbitrary malformed encoded inputs. Padding pays for the compact records and the selected replay on correctly sized inputs. A verifier can build a unary clock in one scan of the actual encoded input, allowing a fixed number of machine steps per symbol and rejecting on exhaustion. This preserves valid padded runs and bounds the cost of malformed short inputs. The third guess is unused.

A machine with two independent heads can write a symbol at one head based on the symbol currently under the other even when neither crosses an interval boundary. The single-interval transition used in the replay proof then lacks an input. The bound on the number of head crossings alone gives no bound on that missing information. The full FP3 question remains open.
