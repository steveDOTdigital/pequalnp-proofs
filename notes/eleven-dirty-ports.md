# The eleven-vertex search with three dirty AND gates

Independent work for L-meta-1342, shard 1 of 2, by steveDOTdigital. The target has 16 minimal positive inputs and 24 maximal negative inputs. A dirty AND is true somewhere the target is false. By monotonicity it then fires on at least one of the 24 maximal negatives. The exact minimum is **seven binary AND gates even if every OR is free**. A direct argument rules out five; a proof-checked SAT calculation rules out six; a 22-gate star circuit achieves seven. The dual argument also requires at least seven binary OR gates even if every AND is free. These results leave the 21-gate existence question open.

## Five ANDs would force incompatible raw inputs

Propagate constants and unary copies. With free ORs, each AND operand is a disjunction of raw variables and earlier AND outputs. Call its set of raw variables a port. At each AND, factor out raw variables common to both operands by distributivity and carry their disjunction forward to its readers. This costs no ANDs and makes the two ports disjoint. Every normalized AND then vanishes on singleton assignments. The output disjunction contains no raw inputs because the target is false on singletons.

With k ANDs a circuit has at most 2^k maximal false rows. Choosing one operand at each AND produces an OR-only upper bound. For any false row choose a false operand at each false AND; either operand works at a true AND. This choice preserves all gate values there. An OR's zero set has one greatest member, so choices preserving distinct maximal false rows must be disjoint.

At least five ANDs are needed for 24 rows. Suppose five suffice. Fixing vertex v to one leaves exactly as many maximal false rows as originally contained v. Any gate with v in a port can be deleted under this restriction: the operand becomes true and the other is already a disjunction. Since the two ports are disjoint, each such port saves a distinct gate. In vertex order 0 through 10 the counts of surviving maximal false rows are 15, 11, 13, 10, 8, 8, 7, 7, 5, 5, 5. The first four vertices therefore occur in at most one raw port each, and the others in at most two. Every vertex must occur because it is essential.

A vertex v with just one port must be alone in it. Otherwise let u be a companion in this port. Pick a neighbor w of v nonadjacent to u, allowing w=u. This is possible since v has at least two neighbors and two distinct vertices have at most one common neighbor. The row {u,w} is independent, but adding v turns on an edge and changes no port. This is impossible. In particular the four low-degree vertices 0,1,2,3 each have an exclusive singleton port.

If v among 4 through 10 has two ports, take a companion a from one and b from the other. Necessarily a and b are distinct and adjacent: otherwise v has a neighbor w adjacent to neither, since its degree is at least three and each of a,b shares at most one neighbor with it. Adding v to the independent set {a,b,w} again changes the target but no port. Thus all companions across the two ports form edges.

Peel off any vertex left alone in some port, deleting that vertex from all ports and discarding empty ports. Each peeled vertex consumes at least one distinct port. Starting from eleven vertices and at most ten ports, a nonempty core remains with fewer ports than vertices. It has at most seven vertices; none is one of 0 through 3. Every remaining vertex belongs to exactly two ports, each of size at least two. Its two sets of companions are disjoint and completely joined in the graph. They cannot both have size two or more, which would give a four-cycle. Thus every vertex is in a port of size two.

No port can have size at least four. Each of its members v would have a partner outside it in the size-two port at v. Partners of distinct members must coincide, since otherwise they share at least two neighbors in the large port. One common partner cannot belong to four distinct ports. For a triple port, the three partners are distinct: if two coincide, that partner is adjacent to the whole triple and a different third partner would have two neighbors in common with it. Coincidence of all three violates the two-port bound.

There must be exactly two triple ports. Their number is positive and even, since the sum of port sizes is twice the number of vertices and exceeds twice the number of ports. The triples are disjoint and there are at most seven vertices. In a six-vertex core, the other three ports pair these two triples by a matching. In a seven-vertex core a seventh vertex z would subdivide one of those pairs, joining one member of each triple through two size-two ports. It cannot partner two members of the same triple. For the two remaining direct pairs write v1-u1 and v2-u2, with v3 and u3 joined through z. The companion rule makes both z and v3 adjacent to u1 and u2, a four-cycle. Thus only six vertices are possible.

The companion rule now supplies a six-cycle: between the triples all six nonmatching cross edges must exist. Each of the five remaining ports is either an alternating triple of that cycle or a pair of opposite vertices. No port contains consecutive vertices of the cycle. Every six-cycle of the graph on labels 4 through 10 must use edge 4-8. Indeed, after deleting 4-8, peeling vertices 4,10,6,7 in order leaves only the triangle on 5,8,9. Thus no port contains both 4 and 8. Peeled ports contain no member of this final core, and adding peeled vertices back to a surviving port cannot change its intersection with the core.

Finally, vertex 1 occurs in just the singleton port {1}. At each of the positive pairs {1,4} and {1,8} take the first true AND. Until this point every active operand must be active from its raw port. The two ports of this gate are disjoint and their cross product must contain the two active vertices. Hence the one partner of port {1} must contain both 4 and 8. This is the desired contradiction. The [independent enumerator](eleven-five-and-ports.py) checks all 1,182 candidate abstract port graphs and obtains exactly one labeled collection of surviving ports.

## Ruling out six ANDs

The [exact synthesis encoder](eleven-and-budget.py) uses six ANDs, with each operand an arbitrary OR of raw variables and earlier AND outputs. It permits arbitrary fanout, empty selections, and an arbitrary final OR of paid gates. Common operands can be factored out of an AND for free, as above. The order of the two operands is immaterial; the encoding places the least selected wire on the left. Selecting just the left side and an empty right side is allowed. An unused empty gate permits padding. Every essential variable must be selected, and the restriction bound above limits its number of occurrences. Testing the 16 minimal positives and 24 maximal negatives suffices by monotonicity.

The encoder requires every edge to cross the two raw ports of some gate. This follows by taking the first true AND on that edge. It also requires all 276 pairs of different maximal false rows to be separated, as justified below. With these constraints CaDiCaL returned UNSAT for six ANDs with internal LRAT proof checking enabled. With seven ANDs the pinned star model returned SAT and replayed correctly on all 2,048 rows. Hence every 21-gate shard witness has at least seven ANDs. After marking any three dirty ANDs, at least four ANDs must remain among the eighteen other gates.

## Seven ORs and a bound for three dirty gates

For the OR bound, dualize the target and exchange AND and OR. The dual function asks whether the selected vertices form a vertex cover. Its sixteen maximal false rows are complements of the graph edges. If it had at most six ANDs and unrestricted ORs, normalize ports as above. Fixing any vertex true leaves at least twelve of those maximal false rows. It can therefore occur in at most two raw ports. For each edge {v,w}, adding v to its complementary maximal false row turns the function true. Some raw port containing v must have been false there; this port is contained in {v,w}. A vertex with no singleton port consequently needs a separate pair port for each neighbor. All vertices of degree three or more must have singleton ports. The degree-two vertices are mutually nonadjacent, so each one without a singleton adds one more mandatory port to the eleven normally required. There are at most twelve port slots. Either all vertices have singleton ports with at most one extra slot, or one degree-two vertex is missing a singleton and has its two pair ports instead.

For any edge e, at most three port slots can be subsets of e, including empty ports. In the first case there are at most two singleton ports plus one extra; in the second, at most two singleton ports and one edge-pair port. On the complement of e, therefore, at least k−3 of the k ANDs have both raw operands true. Each allows either choice in the OR-upper-bound argument. These sixteen maximal rows need at least 16 times 2^(k−3) choices, exceeding 2^k. This proves the dual lower bound.

On the original graph the same counting sharpens the shard search. For any three marked dirty ANDs let their sets of maximal negative witnesses be A, B and C. Any correct circuit with k binary ANDs satisfies

    24 + |A| + |B| + |C| + |A ∩ B| + |A ∩ C| + |B ∩ C| + |A ∩ B ∩ C| ≤ 2^k.

This binary-gate inequality counts both choices at each marked gate true on a row, then sums over its 24 rows. In particular three marked ANDs in a six-AND binary circuit cannot all fire on the same six maximal negatives. A second check used in the [AND-budget search](eleven-and-budget.py) follows from unions: distinct maximal negative rows have a positive union. At the first gate whose union-input value exceeds the OR of its two separate row values, the two operands of an AND must have opposite patterns (1,0) and (0,1). This provides a necessary separation for all 276 pairs of maximal negatives.

## Three marked gates and 18 other gates

Cut the chronological gate list at three AND gates that fire on negative inputs. Four blocks of gates remain, totaling at most eighteen. Store each marked AND's operand list separately. Evaluate one block, append the AND of its designated wires, then continue into the next block. This keeps shared wires and arbitrary dependencies between the three marks. Empty and unary operand lists and out-of-range reads retain the desk's semantics.

The [Lean assembler](ElevenThreeMarks.lean) proves that joining any four such blocks produces a monotone circuit with exactly the stated size and the stated value on every input. The synthesis child requires a negative witness for each mark. It also requires at least four further AND gates. Its eleven adjacency masks were checked in Lean against every ordered pair in the original desk list. The [direct candidate search](eleven-dirty-search.py) checks the 16 minimal positives and 24 maximal negatives. A 25-gate control with three dirty gates passed all 2,048 assignments. The 21-gate search with binary operands and three dirty witnesses reached its 150-second limit. The initial six-AND encoding reached its 180-second limit. Adding the two proved necessary constraints yielded the UNSAT result above. Run the AND-budget script from the Lean directory with arguments `6 --seconds 600` or `7 --star`. No shard exhaustion is claimed.
