# Singleton-clean circuits through vertex cover five

For every finite four-cycle-free bipartite graph with minimum vertex cover at most five, there exists a smallest binary monotone circuit for its edge function in which every AND is false on every singleton input. The circuit can share arbitrary wires. There is no bound on the number of vertices. This proves a subclass of L-meta-3456. Its unrestricted statement remains open.

At cover five there are 1,022 reduced incidence types up to isomorphism and exchange of the shores. On 794 the calculation proves the exact optimum $U=|E|+4$. On 216 more, numerical bounds exclude every possible singleton hit in an extremal optimum strictly below $U$. The last 12 are pendant-leaf extensions of one core. A saturated fanout count rules out all 96 remaining choices of a singleton in these extensions. Optimality of the star size itself remains undecided here on 228 graph types.

## A restriction forced by a singleton hit

Discard isolates and normalize a least circuit to its live output cone with two distinct inputs per gate. The nonempty graph function depends on every remaining raw variable and rejects every singleton. The empty graph has the zero-gate false output available in the project model. Let $n$ count essential inputs, $s$ count gates, and call a wire hot on $x$ if it accepts the singleton $\{x\}$. Choose a least circuit minimizing the total number of pairs consisting of an AND and a singleton on which it fires. Break ties by minimizing the sum of the numbers of OR ancestors of the ANDs.

Suppose an AND is hot on $x$, and let $h$ be the earliest such AND. Open its two operands through private OR trees, stopping at shared wires, raw inputs and ANDs. If every terminal hot on $x$ on one side implies the opposite operand on all assignments, factor that side into the OR of the implying terminals and the remaining terminals. Pull the former group outside the AND. The private tree needs no additional gates. The only possible new AND is bounded by the old one and loses $x$. The output at the old wire agrees on all assignments, hence all later singleton responses agree. Minimality forbids the move. The [private-OR argument](singleton-frontier.md) gives the gate count including either empty group.

Each side therefore has a hot terminal failing to imply the opposite operand. A raw hot terminal is $x$ and implies both operands. Earlier ANDs are cold on $x$. The two terminals $u,v$ must be distinct shared ORs, incomparable by full-function implication, neither directly reading the other. Each implies its respective operand of $h$. The first shared wire on the sole-reader path from $x$ is also hot and distinct from both. Indeed it dominates both, and forcing that cut wire high already suffices to activate either descendant when every other raw input is zero. Monotonicity gives the implication on every input. This supplies at least three distinct hot shared wires.

Call a gate affected if at least one operand is hot on $x$. All hot gates are affected. Each hot wire has a later affected reader since the output is cold. At least **six gates** are affected. Otherwise, choose two distinct readers each for $u,v$ and one for $h$. The five chosen arcs enter at most three distinct gates, since none enters $u$ or $v$. At least two targets have two hot operands and are hot themselves. Choose such a target other than $h$. It is later than both $u$ and $v$, because it reads both or reads $h$. Its own outgoing arc is a sixth arc entering the same pool of at most three gates. All three targets must now have two hot inputs. This accounts for all five possible gates, all hot, leaving the last hot gate without its required affected reader.

Pin $x$ high. Every hot wire becomes constant true by forward propagation. Every affected gate folds to a constant or to its other operand. Write $L_1$ for a lower bound on the restricted function and $k$ for the number of essential raw inputs it retains. Then
$$s\ \ge\ L_1+\max(6,n-k+3).$$
For the second term, the total excess fanout, summed as outgoing occurrences minus one at each nonoutput wire, is $s-n+1$. The high cofactor can retain at most the sum of the excesses of the cold wires. Delete the hot wires, their incident edges and everything without a directed path to the output. In the remaining cone each nonoutput wire still has a path to the output, so its excess is nonnegative and at most its original excess. Constant folding, pruning and contraction of unary aliases cannot increase the cycle rank. Further semantically irrelevant raw inputs may be pinned as well. The residual binary circuit has excess equal to its size minus $k$ plus one. The three shared hot wires each lose at least one unit.

## Charging pure marks and pendant centers

Let $D$ contain the vertices of degree at least two. Follow the unique path of readers from each raw vertex until its first shared wire or the output. A vertex is marked if its first shared wire is hot on that vertex. The [prefix purity and cover proof](pseudoforest-circuits.md) shows that each mark of a vertex in $D$ accepts exactly its own singleton, and the marked vertices cover $G[D]$. For an unmarked $y$ in $D$, the first cold AND on its private path has one operand hot only on $y$ and its other operand hot on every neighbor of $y$.

Consider budget $B=U-1$, where $U=|E|+\tau(G)-1$ is the size of the clean star construction. A vertex $p$ is eligible here if it has degree at least three, a private leaf $z$, and $B-L(G-p)\le3$, with $L$ a verified lower bound. The existing [pendant-center proof](pseudoforest-circuits.md) applies to this local configuration in any four-cycle-free graph. The only use of the cycle there was to supply the numerical three-gate budget and two other neighbors. We give the details for the present extremal choice.

A pendant center cannot have a sole OR reader $p\lor W$. Its independent sibling $W$ must imply the graph function, because any independent assignment omitting $p$ on which $W$ fires can also include $z$, and then adding $p$ should change the output. Bypass the old OR by $p$ and add $W$ back at the output. This preserves the function and size bound. Existing ANDs can only lose singleton hits. At least one AND had the removed OR as an ancestor, since an all-OR path to the output would accept $p$ alone. The final OR adds no ancestor to any AND, contradicting the secondary minimum in case the hit count stays equal.

If $p$ or $z$ has multiple readers, charge that raw wire. Otherwise the reader of $p$ is $A=p\land H$, where $H$ accepts all neighbors of $p$, and the reader of $z$ must be $J=z\lor S$ to pass the singleton $z$ to $H$. Pin $p=0,z=1$. The remaining function is exactly $G-p$. If $H\ne J$, the pin removes $J$, its next reader on a path to $H$, then $A$ and a reader of $A$. These four gates are distinct. If $H=J$ and $S$ is private to $J$, the pin removes $J,S,A$ and a reader of $A$. The budget forbids both cases. Thus $S$ is a shared wire. It accepts precisely the singleton inputs in $N(p)\setminus\{z\}$. For an extraneous singleton $y$, if $S$ already fired, the negative input $\{p,y\}$ could not distinguish adding $z$ through its only reader. The sibling is independent of $p,z$. It has at least two singleton hits, so is internal. Charge $S$.

Distinct eligible centers give distinct charges. Raw charges belong to disjoint center/leaf pairs, and equality of two sibling charges would give two common neighbors. These wires also differ from the pure marks of all other degree-at-least-two vertices. Thus for eligible centers $P$, at least $|P|+\tau(G[D\setminus P])$ sharing units are required. If $x$ is dirty, use just the eligible centers whose closed neighborhood avoids $x$, called $Q$. The $|Q|$ charges and the $\tau(G[D\setminus(Q\cup\{x\})])$ necessary marks there are all cold on $x$. Add the three hot sharing units proved above. All these counts apply in the same chosen circuit.

## How the finite bounds are obtained

The [reproduction program](five-cover-singleton-pins.py) reuses the [cover catalogue and earlier exact bases](single-read-cover-kernels.md). To enumerate, fix a minimum cover, distribute it between the shores, enumerate its internal incidence relation and all distinct nonempty neighborhoods outside it, and exclude four-cycles and repeated sibling leaves. Keep graphs with matching number five, then identify isomorphic graphs by trying all permutations within invariant degree-profile classes. Edges give the adjacency relation itself. Numbering the vertices never supplies a lower bound.

The calculation uses the [forest direct sum](cyclic-core-peeling.md), exact costs through cover four, and the [pseudoforest and minimum-degree bounds](pseudoforest-circuits.md). The degree-two shore result is proved in the [earlier catalogue](single-read-cover-kernels.md). For a restriction, setting independent vertices high produces one independent OR literal for each unpinned neighbor, together with the edge graph outside the fixed vertices and these neighbors. Each such literal costs one additional gate over a nonempty edge function. With no remaining edge, $r>0$ literals cost $r-1$. Constant restrictions are omitted. The program applies these rules recursively on strictly smaller graphs.

For clarity, the first gate is an AND or an OR of two raw inputs. A raw AND on a nonedge can be forced low without affecting any minimal positive row. An input of the first gate must have another reader if it remains essential after the other input is pinned to the controlling value. Every pin loses its direct readers, and a controlling pin also removes a successor of the first gate. A raw reader of a wire cannot be a reader of another gate directly involving the same raw wire in a minimum circuit, by absorption. With two readers, the standard [same-type and mixed-type payments](cyclic-core-peeling.md) apply. The existing [two-gate shore lemma](single-read-cover-kernels.md) applies independently on each component with distinct neighborhoods on the chosen shore.

More generally, when all variables missing from a nonconstant cofactor are fixed, a raw source of fanout $d$ loses $d-1$ excess units as well as its own source. After deleting the fixed raw sources, all other wires still reach the output, and further contractions cannot increase cycle rank. For a single controlling pin of the first gate, its edge from the opposite input can also be deleted when that opposite input remains essential, since it must retain another path to the output. These facts justify the extra payments in the single- and double-pin tests. The minimum across possible first gates is a lower bound. The computation never infers a smaller circuit from a lower bound falling short of $U$.

## The twelve remaining types

All twelve have five points and eight nonleaf lines. Their point masks are $7,9,10,12,17,18,20,24$, with the points numbered from bit zero. The differences are private leaf placements on points. The six-gate and cold-mark tests leave exactly the eight nonleaf lines as possible dirty vertices in each type. A circuit beating its star construction would have at most eight excess fanout units.

Suppose line $x$ is dirty. After deleting $x$ and the private leaves, the remaining incidence edges have matching number five and the five points are their **unique** cover of size five. The enumeration checks this directly for each deleted line. All five points must therefore have pure marks, cold on $x$. Together with the three hot shared wires this exhausts all eight units. Every shared wire has exactly two readers, the other seven core lines are unmarked, and the first shared wire of $x$ is pure by its degree.

Choose a point $p$ with four core line neighbors, for example bit three. Each unmarked neighboring line $y$ contributes a distinct exit from a wire hot on $p$ to a gate cold on $p$. This is the first cold AND on $y$'s path. Its singleton-pure operand identifies $y$, while its other operand accepts at least two different singletons. If $x$ is also a neighbor, use the first wire true on $\{p,x\}$ but false on both singletons. This is an AND with the two inputs hot on opposite singletons. It cannot be one of the gates counted for $y$, since the singleton-pure operand for $y$ is cold on both $p$ and $x$. Thus at least four exits are necessary.

Only the mark of $p$ and possibly $u,v$ contribute excess among wires hot on $p$. If there are $a$ hot gates, the $a+1$ hot wires (including raw $p$) have at most $a+2+\mathbf1_{u(p)}+\mathbf1_{v(p)}$ outgoing occurrences. At least $a$ occurrences feed hot gates. If $u,v$ are both hot on $p$, the two operands of $h$ are both hot, using one extra internal occurrence. Hence at most **three** outgoing occurrences can exit to cold gates in every case. This contradicts the necessary four.

Removing isolated inputs is free. Duplicating a leaf costs exactly one gate, by zero-pinning the new essential input in one direction and substituting the OR of the twins at the old leaf in the other. That substitution preserves singleton cleanliness since a singleton at either twin looks exactly like the old leaf singleton. The forest direct-sum formula also extends this conclusion to the disjoint union of any graph in this class and an arbitrary forest. Use the two clean optima and one output OR when both parts are nonempty. The smaller-cover cases were proved previously, so the result includes all graphs in the stated range, with arbitrary isolated inputs and leaf multiplicities.
