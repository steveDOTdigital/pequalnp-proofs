# Circuit bounds for the 128-by-128 graph

The [posted claim L-meta-20585](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-20585) asks for at most 900 AND/OR/NOT gates on one-row, one-column inputs. We constructed a **1,489-gate** monotone circuit and checked every one of the 16,384 inputs. We used the rectangle arrangement in the [published 1,500-gate proof](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/blob/3b47ace034724a2b2d0294334d51c3e6aae1c63a/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_19358.lean) and independently recomputed its shared OR gates. For that fixed arrangement we prove a **1,182-gate lower bound**. The 900-gate claim remains open.

## Formula lower bounds independent of that arrangement

For a complete bipartite subgraph with a rows and b columns, write its efficiency as ab/(a+b). The largest efficiency in the target graph is exactly 36/7. Columns 125, 74, 109, 36, 68, 124, 116, 10 and 29 have twelve common neighbors. In the complement graph every rectangle has efficiency at most nine.

The [biclique enumeration](rectangle-efficiency-20585.cjs) checks the upper bounds by integer comparison. For each smaller dimension a above the proposed efficiency q, test the smallest b≥a with ab>q(a+b), in both orientations where necessary. Once b≤a+1, any rectangle whose smaller dimension exceeds a contains a tested shape. For a fixed shape enumerate increasing sequences of rows. Retain a possible next row only if its intersection with the columns of the whole prefix still contains b columns. Stop an extension when too few candidate rows remain. The two searches visited 137,377 and 21,689,790 prefixes, respectively.

Push negations in a fan-out-free formula to its leaves by De Morgan's laws. This preserves the count of binary gates. Suppose each path now contains at most one AND. After flattening ORs, each nontrivial term is a product of two ORs of literals. A positive product yields at most two rectangles. For factors with positive row/column sets (R,C) and (S,D), their cross-products are R×D and S×C. A common row or column would force a whole row or column of ones, impossible here. The sum of the side sizes of these two rectangles is at most the number of raw-leaf occurrences in the product.

Complemented literals give the same leaf-count bound. If one factor contains a negated row i, the other factor cannot contain any column literal: a positive column would force at least 127 entries in that column and a negative column would force a 127-by-127 rectangle. It cannot contain a positive row different from i or any negative row, since that would force an entire row. Thus a nonempty product has only positive row i in the other factor. Restricting the first factor to row i leaves a disjunction of positive columns, or an empty product: a surviving negative column would force at least 127 entries in the row. The column argument is symmetric. These exclusions hold for both graphs, since every row and column of each has at least two zeros and at least two ones. A raw literal directly under the output OR would likewise force a full row or column in the target.

The graph contains 6,485 ones. Covering them with these products therefore needs at least ceil(6485·7/36) raw-leaf occurrences and at least **1,260 binary gates**. Applying the same argument to the 9,899 ones of the complement shows that a formula for the original graph with at most one OR per path after negation is pushed to the leaves needs at least **1,099 binary gates**. These bounds concern formulas. Shared intermediate results change the count.

A separate exact count concerns edge-disjoint rectangle partitions. The determinant of the adjacency matrix is 675 modulo 1,009, so its rank over that field is 128. Each rectangle matrix has rank at most one. An edge-disjoint partition sums to the adjacency matrix, hence needs at least 128 rectangles. The row stars attain 128.

## Shared gates in the published rectangle arrangement

The published circuit uses 64 products of pairs of OR outputs, followed by 63 final OR gates. Hold fixed the raw-input set required for each of those 128 OR outputs. Allow any OR-only network with unrestricted sharing to realize them. The [template replay](rectangle-template-20585.py) reads the immutable public source and derives those sets. A minimum network may be taken to have distinct nontrivial supports: two ORs with the same set of raw indicators compute the same signal and can share the earlier result.

For a target set T, its private gates can receive a raw singleton or a shared OR. The support of any such shared OR is contained in the intersection with another target. Let h(T) be the smallest number of other-target intersections and singletons covering T. A connected private output cone must have at least h(T)−1 gates. The computed counts for h=5,6,7,8,9 are 7,65,49,6,1. Their sum weighted by h−1 forces 697 private gates.

Counting one gate visit in each target cone it serves, the network needs at least 1,919 visits, the sum of |T|−1 across the 128 outputs. A shared support S can supply at most the number of targets containing S. Enumerate all subsets of size at least two of all pairwise target intersections. For capacities 2,3,4,5,6,7,9,14 the numbers of distinct eligible supports are 3729,449,70,21,5,1,1,1. To upper-bound the visit capacity of any trial gate budget, assign one visit per private gate and give each remaining gate the largest unused shared capacity. A budget of 1,054 OR gates falls short. Adding the 127 final gates gives at least 1,182 overall.

For the constructive upper bound we varied the pool of paid shared masks, charging each candidate its actual OR cost. For each desired mask the replay finds a minimum cover by smaller paid masks and single raw bits, constructs the binary ORs and then joins the 64 products. The emitted 1,489-gate network matches the posted matrix bit for bit. These are local computational checks. The unrestricted posted theorem has no proof from this construction.
