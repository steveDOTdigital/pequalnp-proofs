# The 111-gate threshold with the order-four line alarms

Case L-meta-4920 asks whether a binary monotone circuit with 110 paid gates can compute the order-four incidence function given fourteen free line alarms. **Every binary formula needs exactly 111 paid gates.** The same optimum holds when arbitrarily many gates preprocessing the seven special lines may be shared and every remaining paid gate has at most one reader. The latter permits arbitrary mixtures of AND and OR in the shared preprocessing. The general case with shared signals involving other inputs remains open.

Number points and lines from 0 through 20. The neighbors of point p are the translates of 0, 5, 7, 17 and 20 modulo 21. The seven heavy points are 3i and the seven special lines form the residue class 2 modulo 3. The free alarms Aᵢ and Bᵢ are ORs of the lines with offsets {5,17} and {5,17,20} from 3i. Heavy points meet three special lines and exterior points meet one. The heavy points and special lines induce a Fano plane.

For the upper bound, use one point-centered star per point. An exterior point needs four ORs and one AND. A heavy point needs two ORs, using Bᵢ and its two ordinary lines, followed by one AND. The final OR costs twenty gates, for **14·5 + 7·3 + 20 = 111**.

## At least 105 raw occurrences

Delete the 21 heavy–special flags, retaining 84 edges. The diagonal flags (p,p) give a perfect matching, so their vertex cover number is 21. For this argument a formula may use any number of supplementary inputs, each the OR of any subset of at least two special lines. Let R count occurrences of the original 42 raw inputs.

For an ordinary-line flag f = (p,l), take as a negative input all other points of l and all other lines through p. It is independent because the plane has no four-cycle. For a retained special-line flag, p is exterior. Select the other exterior point on l, all ordinary lines through p and every special line except l. This is independent. The selected exterior point has l as its only special neighbor, and an ordinary line through both exterior points would give a four-cycle.

Use the formula's rectangle partition on retained positive flags versus these negatives. On diagonal f, only the two physical endpoints can be reported. For an ordinary-line flag no alarm is true on its positive row. For a special-line flag every supplementary alarm is true on its negative column. If two distinct retained flags meet, the only possible report on the first flag versus the second flag's negative is their common raw vertex. In the ordinary-column case, when the other row has a special line, that line itself is switched on in the column.

At each raw vertex the local zero-one report matrix has ones off the diagonal. Its rational rank charges one leaf for every diagonal missing that vertex, plus one if some diagonal reports it. A vertex missing on every diagonal has degree at least two and its matrix J − I is invertible. Each leaf with this raw label contributes at most one to the rank, regardless of rectangles labelled by alarms elsewhere. Summing over the raw labels gives **R ≥ 84 + 21 = 105**. The [general flag-rank argument](flag-cross-rank.md) proves the matrix and cover inequalities.

Now let t be the number of alarm leaves of a formula using the fourteen stated alarms. Expanding them gives at most R + 3t raw leaves. The full plane requires 126. Combining R ≥ 105 with R + 3t ≥ 126 gives R + t ≥ 112, or at least 111 paid gates. At equality R = 105, t = 7 and each alarm is a triple. The expanded formula is a minimum raw formula. Its canonical stars must be point-centered because it contains ORs of three lines. Each Bᵢ can appear only in the star of its heavy point. Thus the seven triple alarms occur once each, up to the choices of binary association and ordering.

## Arbitrarily shared preprocessing on the seven special lines

Take a circuit of at most 110 paid gates whose shared paid signals are built entirely from the special lines and their given alarms. Put these signals and their paid ancestors into a prefix of k gates. The suffix is a formula with at most 111 − k leaves. Replace each prefix signal at its suffix reads by the OR of the special singletons it accepts. This can only lower its value and preserves its value on every flag, which has at most one active special line. Monotonicity then preserves every positive assignment, and all negatives stay rejected. Simplify empty supports to zero and singleton supports to raw inputs.

Write sⱼ for the support sizes of the remaining t nonraw leaves, and L = R + t. The rank bound still gives R ≥ 105, so t ≤ 6 − k. If every sⱼ ≤ 3 then the formula bound above applies directly. Otherwise a support of at least four occurs. Expanding to raw inputs needs **at least 127** leaves. Equality at 126 would give canonical raw stars, and no star contains four special lines as neighbors. Hence

    Σ(sⱼ − 1) ≥ 127 − L ≥ 16 + k.

For k ≥ 3 this exceeds 6t because sⱼ ≤ 7. With k = 1, a gate acting on the singleton supports can take only a union or an intersection. The Fano triples meet pairwise, so its maximum support is five. The inequalities force t = 5, R = 105 and at least four reads of a size-five support. With k = 2 the possibilities are t = 4, R = 105, or t = 3, R = 106 with all three supports of size seven. In the last case, fewer than 106 raw leaves would give an expansion shorter than 127.

When R = 105, all local rank inequalities are equalities and the reporting cover has size 21. Its only choices are all points or all lines. To check this, match p to l = p and direct an arc p → l for every retained flag. This graph on 21 indices is strongly connected, as checked in the accompanying calculation. A size-21 cover takes exactly one vertex per matched pair. Choosing the line endpoint at p forces the line endpoint at every outneighbor. Thus each label on the chosen shore occurs exactly once, and each label across it occurs exactly its retained degree.

**Point shore.** Each of the 21 point variables occurs exactly once. Set all lines high. The restricted formula computes the OR of all points, so the lowest common ancestor of any two point leaves is an OR. Indeed both children containing essential points are nonconstant, and an AND of two nonconstant monotone functions on disjoint point sets cannot act as their OR in a read-once formula. More directly, each child is zero when its points are all zero, or its own variables would be inessential. One singleton from either child must make the output true.

Every AND on point p's path therefore has a line-only sibling. For a given line assignment, the difference between the output with point p alone active and with every point inactive can propagate only if every such AND sibling is true. Conversely, if those siblings are true, the point's true value propagates all the way to the root. The all-points-off output is always false. Thus the star predicate “some line through p is active” equals the conjunction of these line-only sibling predicates. Each is true on every singleton neighbor of p. On the input consisting of all lines outside p's neighborhood, one sibling must be false. By monotonicity that sibling computes precisely p's star predicate. It cannot guard any other point: another point has singleton neighbors outside this pencil. These line-only subtrees are disjoint for different points.

To distinguish all five singleton neighbors from the all-outside negative, a leaf OR of special lines can help only if its entire support is within the pencil. Each exterior star thus needs at least five line leaves. Each heavy star needs its two separate ordinary lines and at least one leaf for the three special lines. The disjoint subtrees and 21 point leaves give at least 14·5 + 7·3 + 21 = 112 suffix leaves, exceeding its budget.

**Line shore.** Each raw line occurs once, each heavy point twice and each exterior point five times. Set one special line l high and all five points on it low. This removes its own occurrence and 3·2 + 2·5 point occurrences, seventeen raw leaves in total. The surviving 16 points still have five lines each and a matching saturates them. The 80-edge four-cycle-free graph requires 96 raw leaves. Only 88 raw leaves survive the pin before expanding the nonraw inputs. Each support contributes zero leaves when it contains l and at most sⱼ otherwise. Summing the required eight extra leaves over the seven choices of l gives Σ sⱼ(7 − sⱼ) ≥ 56. With four or fewer supports the sum is at most 48. With the five supports in the one-gate case, at least four have size five and the sum is at most 52. Both possibilities fail.

**Three universal supports.** Pin the OR of all special lines high at its three reads. This preserves all positive flags and every original negative cross that already contains a special line. There are 91 flags with such a cross, and they contain a perfect matching. Singleton negative tests needed by the rank bound extend to independent inputs containing a special line: for a singleton point use a nonincident special line, and for a singleton line add any special line. Monotonicity therefore gives at least 91 + 21 = 112 raw leaves after the pin, while only 106 were available. This exhausts the cases.

## A further class with unrestricted line preprocessing

Suppose the unfolded circuit reads every point once, but arbitrary line-only paid gates may be shared, including gates on the fourteen ordinary lines. The point-path argument above supplies 21 distinct private point ANDs and a line subcircuit computing each pencil exactly. It also supplies at least twenty distinct point-bearing ORs connecting the 21 essential point leaves: a shared point-bearing gate would duplicate a point in the unfolding.

For each point p, evaluate the line subcircuit for its pencil against the negative containing all lines outside the pencil. For every true singleton neighbor follow only signals false on this negative. At an AND, choose a fixed false operand; it accepts every singleton accepted at the AND. At an OR both operands are false on the negative, and their singleton supports cover the output's support. All retained signals accept only singleton lines in p's pencil. A paid signal accepting two distinct singleton lines can be retained for at most one point because two lines meet in only one point.

Replace each retained signal with empty or singleton support by a constant or a raw singleton. Contract the chosen unary AND edges. The resulting dependency graph covers the five neighbors by sources, each a singleton or a native alarm contained in the pencil. It needs at least five distinct sources for an exterior point and three for a heavy point. A connected binary graph reaching k distinct sources needs at least k − 1 binary merges, even with repeated use of intermediate wires. Every counted merge is a paid OR accepting at least two singleton lines. These gates are private to their pencil and separate from the point-bearing gates. The cost is at least **14·4 + 7·2 + 21 + 20 = 111**. This also proves the corresponding 70-gate lower bound for the simultaneous computation of the 21 line pencils with these free inputs.
