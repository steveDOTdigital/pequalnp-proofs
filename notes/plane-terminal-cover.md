# Terminal covers for the 62-gate projective-plane search

L-meta-2567 seeks a monotone circuit with at most 62 paid gates after one free AND or OR. Its three allowed input pairs are (0,1), (0,13) and (0,15). Point p and line l are incident when p+l modulo 13 belongs to {0,1,3,9}.

## A shared circuit with several outputs

Choose a paid prefix C, a set of t different wires and a final operation. An OR of those wires must cover every positive row, with each wire false on every negative. An AND has the dual requirement: every wire accepts every positive, and the wires together reject every negative. Joining t wires costs exactly t−1 gates in the [explicit construction](PlaneTerminalCover.lean). The search budget is therefore `C.size + t ≤ 63`. The construction preserves arbitrary reuse inside C and works for every input of the declared length. It accepts t=1 as a general lemma; the search uses t≥2.

The search allows a private row for each selected wire. On that row it alone supplies the true bit for an OR, or it alone supplies the false bit for an AND. This condition preserves the original existence problem. Take a minimum paid circuit for any successful free input. The target depends on more inputs than a raw or free wire, so the output is a gate. Discard unused gates and propagate constants and copies. The two operands of the final gate must be distinct and incomparable as Boolean functions. If one dominated the other, the output would equal one operand and that gate could be deleted. Thus these two operands already provide a valid frontier with private rows. Larger frontiers can omit redundant wires until every wire has a private row.

There are 52 minimal positive and 704 maximal negative inputs. The [search program](plane-terminal-cover.py) independently enumerates all 8,192 subsets of the point side and confirms that the desk's negatives are precisely the maximal ones. Evaluating the selected prefix gate on raw inputs preserves monotonicity. Therefore the 756 rows suffice to check the whole function. A private row for an OR can be shrunk to a minimal true row; for an AND it can be extended to a maximal false row.

For a fixed prefix the program considers every existing wire and every additional AND or OR of two existing wires as a possible terminal. Give an existing wire weight one and a new gate weight two. The final charged size is the prefix size plus the weights of the chosen terminals minus one. An additional gate shared by several terminals is paid once in the prefix. The bounded set-cover calculation keeps a cheapest representative for each boundary signature, drops dominated candidates and branches on an uncovered row. Rows with pairwise disjoint candidate families give an additive lower bound. Any successful selection is converted back to explicit gate indices and replayed on all 756 rows.

The control uses 38 paid gates to build point disjunctions, 13 AND terminals and 12 final OR gates, for 63 paid gates. The same prefix cannot reach 62 with one new gate per terminal. The program checked both final operations over 144 generated prefixes, then over 84,742 distinct one-gate extensions counted within 24 base layouts. None reached 62. Each extension was available to every terminal. These finite exclusions apply to the generated prefixes and terminal layer. The unrestricted search child remains open.

## A pencil of unfinished lines cannot supply the saving

In any finite projective plane of order q≥2, let d=q+1 and n=q²+q+1. Suppose a monotone binary incidence circuit contains ordinary stars on every line except k distinct lines through a common point, where 1≤k≤d. An ordinary star forms the OR of a line's d point inputs with d−1 gates and ANDs it with its line input. Other gates may use arbitrary intermediate wires. **The full circuit needs at least n(d+1)−1 gates.** All n ordinary stars attain this count. For order three, nine completed stars outside one pencil already force 64 gates, including the gate supplied free on this assignment.

**Simultaneous pinning.** In a connected live output cone of binary AND/OR gates, fixing k distinct source wires to constants removes at least k gates if the result stays nonconstant. Count both operand edges of a repeated read. The underlying connected multigraph has 2g edges and g+s vertices for g gates and s distinct sources, so its cycle rank is g−s+1. The simplified live cone is a connected minor. A gate passing through its only variable operand can be contracted onto that wire; constant and unused parts can be deleted. Duplicate wires can retain parallel operand edges. Contraction and deletion cannot increase cycle rank. Writing g′ and s′ for the result gives g−g′≥s−s′≥k. This applies to a suffix with correlated input signals: take them as distinct source wires in the graph and pin only signals whose values the raw restriction actually fixes.

**The residual pencil.** Its k lines share a point c and each has d−1 private points. Allow any number of free point ORs of size at least two with at most one point from each pencil line. Every raw input must still be read. To see this for a point, turn on one line containing it, turn off the other points of that line, and turn on every point outside the line. Toggling the chosen point changes the output while every helper stays true. Line bits are essential and helpers depend only on points.

We prove the pencil costs at least k(d+1)−1 gates, by induction. Without used helpers the incidence graph is a tree with kd edges and a matching of size k, obtained by choosing a private point on each line. The [forest lower bound](../lean/PEqualNP/MetaComplexity/Helpers/ForestBound.lean) gives kd+k−1. With k=1 no such helper exists. Otherwise, pick a used helper and a private point in its support. A genuine helper cannot contain c because c lies on every pencil line. Set the line through the chosen point to zero and all its d−1 private points to one. The restricted target is the pencil on the other k−1 lines. At least d+1 live sources are fixed: the line, its private points, and the chosen helper. The other helpers become constant or still obey the same hypothesis. Simultaneous pinning and induction give at least d+1+(k−1)(d+1)−1 = k(d+1)−1 gates.

**Return to the plane.** The n−k fixed stars occupy d(n−k) distinct gates: two lines share at most one point and cannot share a genuine point OR inside their ordinary stars. Place these stars first. For each completed line, the suffix must read its raw line bit or its completed star, since the target depends on that bit. Setting all completed line bits to zero fixes at least n−k live suffix sources. Then set points outside the unfinished union to one. A surviving nonconstant point-OR helper comes from an old line and meets each unfinished line in at most one point. The pencil bound applies. Adding d(n−k), n−k and k(d+1)−1 gives n(d+1)−1. The dual statement holds for point stars. This extends the [three-unfinished-line result](projective-plane-line-prefixes.md) to a pencil with any number of unfinished lines. It leaves other prefix geometries and the unrestricted projective-plane search open.
