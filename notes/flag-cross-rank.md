# Exact formula size for projective-plane incidence

For a projective plane of order q with N = q² + q + 1 points, every binary monotone formula for incidence needs **N(q + 2) − 1 gates**. Stars centered on the points or on the lines attain the bound. Every flag in either construction has a proof reading its point and its line once each. Thus the read-two replacement in L-meta-2582 exists for every formula, at every plane order. The lower bound already holds when the only negative tests are one explicit input per flag.

More generally, in any four-cycle-free bipartite incidence graph, a formula accepting m specified edges and rejecting independent sets needs **m + τ leaves**, where τ is the minimum vertex cover of the specified edges. This is exact even when values on the other edges are unrestricted. The argument places no bound on degree. The same lower bound holds for a partition into leaf rectangles, whether or not the partition comes from a formula.

## One negative test per flag

For a flag e = (p,ℓ), switch on every point of ℓ except p, and every line through p except ℓ. This input is independent. An incidence between a selected point and line would complete a four-cycle through e. In a projective plane this input contains q points and q lines, and it is maximal independent. A point outside ℓ lies on one of the selected lines, and every other line meets ℓ. The two omitted endpoints also have active neighbors.

For any positive flag row and negative column, a true formula reporting a false value on the column selects one differing raw variable. At an OR follow the first true child on the row, and at an AND follow the first false child on the column. The pairs routed to each leaf form a rectangle, since each routing choice depends on only one input. Each selected label is one of the flag's endpoints and is absent from the negative assignment.

Index the rows and columns by the retained flags. On the diagonal, let a flag *report* the endpoint selected by the partition and *miss* the other. Fix a vertex v and restrict to the flags incident to v. At row a and column b with a different from b, the other endpoint of a is active in the negative test for b. Thus the selected label must be v. The zero-one matrix that records a report of v has **every off-diagonal entry one**. Its diagonal is zero exactly on the flags that miss v.

A leaf labelled v contributes the outer product of its row indicator and column indicator. The rectangles are disjoint, so their ordinary rational sum is precisely this local matrix. Its rational rank is a lower bound for the number of occurrences of v.

Suppose s of the diagonal positions miss v. If some position reports v, keep all s missed rows and columns plus one reporting representative. The representative row is all ones. Subtract each missed row from it to obtain that position's unit vector. These s differences and the representative row are independent, giving rank at least s + 1. If all d positions miss v and d ≥ 2, the matrix is J − I. A vector in its kernel has every coordinate equal to the sum of its coordinates, so that sum equals d times itself and must vanish. This gives rank d. For d = 1 with a zero diagonal, one additional singleton negative containing the other endpoint forces at least one occurrence of v. In a full plane each local degree is q + 1, and every singleton also extends to one of the flag columns above.

Summing the local bounds counts one missed endpoint per edge, plus one extra occurrence for each distinct vertex that reports on some diagonal. Those reporting vertices cover all the retained edges. This gives m + τ leaves. Conversely assign each retained edge to one endpoint in a minimum cover. The AND of each center and the OR of its assigned neighbors is a star. Joining the stars by OR uses m + τ leaves and m + τ − 1 gates.

For the plane, every point and line has degree d = q + 1. For a point, the other N − 1 points split into sets of q on its incident lines. There are Nd flags, so a cover needs at least N vertices and either entire shore works. This gives the displayed bound.

## Equality on a full plane

In a minimum partition the reporting vertices form a cover of exactly N vertices. Their incident edge counts sum to Nd, so each edge has exactly one such endpoint. Connectedness forces the cover to be an entire shore. Each center has exactly one leaf, and each opposite vertex has exactly d. The center leaf spans all its flag rows, since that center reports on every local diagonal.

In every cross column missing a center, some neighbor is present by maximality. That neighbor's flag must report the center. Its sole leaf therefore reports it on every one of its flag rows. On columns containing the center, the opposite endpoint reports instead. Fix a noncenter line ℓ and a point p on it. A cross based on another line through p, with its foot different from p, meets ℓ in the single point p. The only row at ℓ reporting the line in this column is its flag with p. Thus a singleton row mask is needed for each of its d flags, exhausting its d leaves. The same reasoning applies with the two shores swapped.

These rectangles force the minimum formula shape. A nontrivial row split can only separate whole centers, since one leaf spans every flag at a center. A column split before two centers have been separated would have to preserve both complementary sets of columns for each center. Distinct centers' membership indicators on crosses are neither identical nor complementary. For two points, take their common line with a third foot for a column containing both, and a different line through just one for a column distinguishing them. Thus all multi-center splits are ORs. With one center the first split must be its AND, followed only by ORs selecting an individual neighbor. This holds even on the sparse flag-versus-cross promise.

Consequently unfolding a smaller shared circuit cannot yield a minimum formula. Any genuinely shared internal subtree would occur twice, while distinct stars of the minimum formulas share at most one raw neighbor and have different centers. In particular, if H is the only shared signal, costs h gates and is used r times, a circuit smaller than the star construction must have size at least N(q + 2) − (r − 1)h. An arbitrary number of shared signals constant on the positive flag class or the negative cross class can be removed at no increase by [ordered certificate reconstruction](quadrangle-partition.md#eliminating-class-constant-shared-gates). The formula bound and hence the desk's read-two conclusion hold for that class of circuits as well.

## Restrictions on one shared signal

Let H be the sole shared internal signal in a trimmed binary circuit, with h gates in its private formula and r distinct readers. Remove duplicate operands first. If the circuit is smaller than the star construction, pinning H low or high deletes at least h + r gates. The surviving output is a formula. These pins are used to obtain lower bounds, so the residual need only pass the specified rows and columns.

At a low pin, all independent inputs are still rejected. If t flags are lost, the hereditary formula bound applies to the remaining Nd − t flags. A cover of those flags has at least N − ⌊t/d⌋ vertices since each vertex meets at most d flags. Thus **h + r + 1 ≤ t + ⌊t/d⌋** whenever the residual has at least one flag. The same inequality holds with an empty residual by the total gate budget. This tests actually pivotal flags.

At a high pin all flags remain accepted. Suppose H accepts k distinct singleton points with k ≥ q + 2. Every cross containing one of these points stays negative. Retain the flags whose own crosses contain one, and let D count the other crosses. Every retained line has degree at least q in this subgraph. Each retained point has degree at least two: its lines to the other chosen points cannot all be one line, since a line contains only q + 1 points. The rank proof therefore applies without any singleton tests. The residual has at least Nd − D + N − ⌊D/d⌋ − 1 gates. Since h ≥ k − 1, a below-star circuit requires **k + r ≤ D + ⌊D/d⌋**. This works for any function H with those singleton responses, and likewise for singleton lines.

There is a bound independent of the positions of the k points. Count their number X on each negative cross. The sum of X is S₁ = kq(q + 1). The sum of X² is S₂ = S₁ + k(k − 1)(q − 1), because two distinct points appear together on the crosses based at the other q − 1 points of their common line. Cauchy–Schwarz gives D ≤ Nd − ⌈S₁²/S₂⌉. With r ≥ 2 this excludes a below-star sole helper accepting at least 5 of 7 singleton points at order 2, 8 of 13 at order 3, 13 of 21 at order 4, or 20 of 31 at order 5. As q grows, if k/N tends to a positive fraction α, the upper bound for D/N tends to (1 − α)/α. Hence α cannot exceed (√5 − 1)/2.

## Verification

The [binary star construction in Lean](FlagStarCircuit.lean) emits the project's actual gate list. It verifies earlier-wire reads, two inputs per gate, exact size, truth on every assignment, and a single joint count vector bounded by one on every prime implicant. The theorem `withinBudget` proves the desk's conclusion for every circuit of at least N(q + 2) − 1 gates. The theorem `formulaCase` combines the lower bound and the construction to prove the desk's conclusion for every circuit compiled from a binary formula, regardless of its size.
