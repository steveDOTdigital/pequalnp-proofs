# Removing ANDs that fire on a singleton

For L-meta-3456, a binary monotone circuit computing the edge function can be made singleton-clean at no increase in size if it has at most one shared internal wire. The result also holds with arbitrarily many shared wires when their functions are totally ordered by implication on the graph's maximal independent sets. A shared wire here is an internal wire read more than once in the live output cone. Fanout of raw inputs is unrestricted. Both results hold for general simple graphs.

In any counterexample, a suitably chosen least circuit must contain two distinct shared OR wires that fire on the same singleton. Each is true without the other on a different maximal independent set. Both independent sets are sensitive to the same offending AND. This statement gives tests for excluding candidate circuits. The general case with such wires remains open.

## Factor a private OR tree

Fold constants and unary copies, remove dead gates and contract repeated operands. For a nonconstant graph function the resulting live circuit uses ordinary binary AND and OR. The empty graph has a zero-gate solution. These simplifications cover the nullary and unary gates allowed by the desk's `Monotone2` definition.

Choose the earliest AND $h=A\land B$ firing on some singleton $x$. Open each operand's OR tree backwards through **private** OR gates only. Stop at a raw input, any AND, or any shared wire. These terminal lists may contain repeated wire IDs. Keeping occurrences preserves the original size count. The two private trees have no shared internal node.

In $A$, let $U$ be the disjunction of terminal occurrences whose values imply $B$ on the test rows, and let $T$ be the disjunction of the rest. Suppose every terminal of $A$ firing at $x$ belongs to $U$. Replace $h$ by $U\lor(T\land B)$. The new internal AND is bounded by $h$ on all assignments and is false at $x$. The value presented to the former readers of $h$ can only increase. It stays the same on all test rows. If $T$ is empty, simply use $A$ and delete the AND.

If the two nonempty groups have $p$ and $q$ occurrences, the original tree and AND cost $p+q$ gates. The replacement uses $p-1$ and $q-1$ internal ORs, one AND and one final OR. No shared ancestor is copied. Each other pre-existing wire that remains has the same reads or fewer, allowing for the former output reads of $h$ which its replacement inherits. If the replacement aliases an existing wire that becomes shared, it represents the former value of $h$ on the test rows and $h$ was shared. Thus the set of shared wire functions on the test rows cannot grow.

With all assignments as tests the output is unchanged everywhere and the total number of singleton hits over all ANDs strictly falls. This also proves normalization under a weaker hypothesis on full truth tables: for each singleton, the shared functions firing there need only be pairwise comparable. Full functions and their singleton responses survive on shared outputs, so the hypothesis persists. In particular this gives the assertion for at most one shared internal wire, even for a function other than a graph function.

## Maximal independent sets and the two witnesses

For a graph, all maximal independent sets suffice as test rows. The output cannot decrease anywhere, and it cannot turn on at a negative assignment if it stays off at all maximal negative assignments. Changes at other singletons can affect later ANDs, so use a forward order on the AND gates. First minimize the number of ANDs, then the lexicographic vector counting how many singletons fire at each AND in that order. When the number of ANDs stays the same, the old AND order can be preserved. The redistributed private ORs only need their terminal signals before $h$. Earlier ANDs are unchanged, and $h$ loses at least $x$. This strictly decreases a finite measure. A total order by implication of the shared functions on maximal independent sets remains true after each rewrite and suffices for normalization.

For the sharper obstruction one can test just the maximal independent sets where **forcing $h$ high** makes the original output true. At all other maximal independent sets even forcing the highest possible value cannot turn on the output. This version still decreases the forward measure. It needs no invariant on sharing to analyze a purported counterexample. Choose among least circuits one with the smallest number of ANDs and the lexicographically smallest singleton counts over their topological orders.

If neither side of $h$ can be factored at $x$, choose a terminal $u$ of $A$ firing at $x$ but failing to imply $B$ on the sensitive tests. Similarly choose a terminal $v$ of $B$ failing to imply $A$. Neither can be an earlier AND by the choice of $h$. A raw terminal firing at $x$ is exactly $x$, which implies both operands by monotonicity. Hence $u,v$ are shared OR wires. There is a sensitive maximal independent set $I$ with $u=1,B=0$ and another $J$ with $v=1,A=0$. They distinguish $u,v$ in both directions. Both omit $x$ and both contain a neighbor of $x$, by maximality. There is also a graph edge from $I\setminus J$ to $J\setminus I$: choose a vertex of $J\setminus I$, then a neighbor in $I$ by maximality. That neighbor cannot lie in $J$.

Every gate of a least circuit also survives attempts to bypass it by either operand. Each AND must therefore have a sensitive maximal independent set for each of the operand patterns 10 and 01. Dually, each OR needs a sensitive minimal positive edge for each pattern, with sensitivity tested by forcing that OR low. For each of the two shared ORs just found, at least one of its essential edge witnesses omits $x$, since one of its operands already fires at $x$. These tests distinguish local sharing that matters to the graph output from redundant sharing in a scheduled circuit.

## Pinning a vertex or replacing a wire

Pin $x$ to zero, fold constants and prune the new output cone. Let $F_0$ be its function and $K$ the neighbors of $x$. Restore the target as $F_0\lor(x\land W)$. The alarm $W$ need only agree with “some member of $K$ is on” where $F_0$ is false. A monotone alarm passes exactly when it is true on each singleton in $K$ and false on every maximal independent set of the graph with $x$ and $K$ deleted. This follows by extending independent inputs that avoid $K$ to maximal such inputs.

An OR of existing wires can be chosen by set cover. Discard wires firing on any of the forbidden rows. The remaining wires cover the neighbor singletons where they fire. If $m$ wires suffice, restoration takes $m+1$ added gates for a nonconstant residual and a nonisolated $x$. Raw neighbor inputs always give $m\le\deg(x)$. If the pin removes at least $m+1$ gates this clears every AND hit at $x$ within budget. On all other singletons the old signals keep their zero-pin values and the new star AND is false. In particular, in a least circuit chosen instead to minimize **total** singleton hits, a dirty $x$ with nonconstant residual has at most $\deg(x)$ direct readers. Each such reader disappears at the zero pin. This last choice of extremal circuit is distinct from the forward-order choice above.

There is an exact boundary test for replacing any wire by a signal built independently of it. On a minimal positive edge where forcing the wire low would reject, the replacement must be one. On a maximal negative where forcing it high would accept, the replacement must be zero. These conditions are necessary and sufficient for a monotone replacement: the fixed downstream circuit is monotone in its one changed input, and the two boundary families determine its entire output. To decrease the total dirty-AND count when inserting an OR, also prevent increases at singleton rows. A replacement AND can be required to be false on every singleton. The available independent wires include later gates outside the changed wire's fanout, since these can be scheduled earlier.

The three-input signal $h=(x\lor a)\land(x\lor b)$ shows why preserving a local signal can cost an extra gate. Suppose $x,a,b,x\lor a,x\lor b$ are available. The required signal accepts $x$ alone and $ab$ together but rejects $a$ and $b$ alone. No available wire, one OR, or singleton-clean AND can satisfy those four rows. Two gates suffice as $x\lor(a\land b)$. The [Lean example](SingletonFrontier.lean) places this fork inside an eight-gate circuit for the matching $xe\lor ab$. Both OR operands have another reader, both matched edges need $h$, and the two sensitive independent sets are $\{a,e\}$ and $\{b,e\}$. A pin repair stays at eight gates and clears every AND. A separate same-size OR replacement uses an existing $a\land b$ wire originally scheduled later.

## Finite checks and Lean scope

The [independent program](singleton-frontier.py) evaluates whole truth tables and checks gate counts, truth values and the relevant descent measure after rewrites.

- On three raw inputs it enumerated all 44,718 scheduled binary monotone programs with one to four gates, distinct earlier operands and last-gate output. The 8,316 whose live cones had a singleton-true AND were all cleaned by full-truth factoring.
- On four inputs it fixed the two shared ORs and $h$ as the first three gates and enumerated suffixes of one through four gates. It retained live circuits computing nonempty four-cycle-free bipartite edge functions in which both ORs had a further live reader and forcing $h$ low changed the output. The 11,088 qualifying schedules gave 2,910 distinct ordered live circuits after compaction. Factoring on sensitive negatives cleaned 2,876 directly. Alternating size-reducing operand bypasses with factoring cleaned all 34 remaining circuits. A pin was affordable in 22 of those 34 and a direct one-gate replacement worked in 14; these groups overlap.

Run `python3 notes/singleton-frontier.py` at the repository root. The [Lean file](SingletonFrontier.lean) proves the factoring identity, strict removal of the chosen singleton from the new AND, the local gate budget, the two distinguishing rows, the chain implication, the maximal-negative alarm test, the sensitive boundary test and monotonicity of the project's actual circuit suffix evaluation. It verifies binary monotone legality, earlier-wire reads and the stated input/output properties of the eight-gate matching example and its repair. Run `lake env lean ../notes/SingletonFrontier.lean` from the Lean directory with upstream definitions at `82b3b489`. Iteration over arbitrary circuit DAGs above is a written proof. The exact least-size result for graphs of small cover or with a degree-two shore is [proved separately](single-read-cover-kernels.md) and also gives singleton-clean star witnesses there.
