# Which shared signals can occur in a least circuit for the order-three plane?

[L-meta-2391](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-2391) asks whether *every* least binary AND/OR circuit for the incidence graph has an empty set of junk gates. A junk gate is an AND which is true on some independent input. The conclusion is universal over all least circuits. The 64-gate formula consisting of thirteen stars gives the upper bound used below. The [opposite statement](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-2392) would require an actual least circuit with a junk gate.

**Partial result.** If there is no shared internal signal, every least circuit is a formula of ordinary stars and has no junk gate. If there is exactly one shared internal signal H, none of the following can occur in a least circuit.

* H is a disjunction of raw inputs, of any width, or a conjunction of a point disjunction and a line disjunction, of any widths.
* H rejects every singleton input and at most nine vertices occur in the flags that turn it on.
* H accepts a singleton and has at most six contributing vertices. These are its accepted singletons together with the endpoints of the flags it accepts that contain no accepted singleton.

A flag turns on precisely an incident point and line. Raw inputs may have arbitrarily many readers. These results leave larger private producers and circuits with several shared intermediate signals open. The [Python replay](least-plane-junk-2391.py) uses the exact incidences from the posted statement. The combinatorial arguments are written proofs.

## Counting the leaves after a cut

In a least circuit every gate is used and no binary gate reads the same wire twice. Pinning a wire low and propagating constants may remove gates. If H is false on all flags, pinning it low preserves all flags and decreases the output on independent inputs. If H implies the graph function, pin its reads low and OR the original H into the output. This eliminates at least two distinct direct readers and costs at most one new gate. If the residual output is constant false, use H alone. Thus neither type of shared signal can occur in a least circuit. This argument also works with other shared signals present.

With a single shared H its input cone is a private formula of t gates. The suffix is a formula with A raw leaves and r occurrences of H, with r ≥ 2. Its total circuit size is s=t+A+r−1. An input outside the contributing vertices can be pinned low in H alone. This changes no positive flag and creates no independent positive. A strict simplification contradicts leastness. Each of the d contributing variables is essential after trimming. Hence t ≥ d−1. At equality the producer reads each once. At t=d just one variable occurs twice.

We use the [flag comparison and extra-row rank argument](plane-pair-sharing-3773.md#recovering-the-cover-cost-on-deleted-flags). A row is a positive flag. A canonical negative column for (p,l) sets all other points on l and all other lines through p. At a retained diagonal the comparison reports an endpoint. Each incident retained diagonal reporting the opposite endpoint costs one leaf of this vertex's label. There is one additional leaf if the vertex is itself reported on some diagonal or has a usable extra row. The set of reported endpoints covers the retained edges. When all rows in a set S are cold at the cut wire, the cross columns of the full plane give at least |S|+ν(S) physical leaves. Here ν is the maximum matching size. The same bound holds for a formula accepting S and rejecting every independent set of the full plane. Both full shores are independent columns. The [formula equality proof](plane-shared-formulas.md#equality-for-formulas) shows that a formula of at most 64 gates for the full graph consists of all thirteen stars of one shore.

## All widths of a sole shared disjunction or rectangle

For a pure-shore disjunction of d ≥ 2 inputs, mask each member by a distinct-from-it mate in the same set. At most d flags are lost. The enlarged-cover argument gives A ≥ 65−d, hence s ≥ 65. A single raw bit is already available.

For a mixed disjunction, a raw leaf of every label v is required in the suffix. On any flag containing v, the entire opposite shore forces v, since it already turns on the mixed helper. Delete each flag on which the helper is true but its cross column is false. If D is this deleted set and U its endpoints then A ≥ 52−|D|+|U|+ν(E outside U). The [original proof of this inequality](plane-pair-sharing-3773.md#a-single-shared-disjunction-of-any-width) also applies to a subset of positive flags, keeping the original full-plane cross columns and using the corresponding retained edge set throughout.

The replay checks all 67,092,481 choices of nonempty point and line sets. At s ≤ 64 the first bound leaves only widths (1,1), (1,2), (1,3), (1,4), (2,2), (2,3), up to exchanging shores. If one side is a singleton and the other has b ≥ 2 members, expand the singleton at every read, leaving the other shore virtual. Then A+r ≥ 65−b. At s ≤ 64 equality must hold. Exactly b flags must be lost, and the enlarged cover has size 13 and contains a damaged vertex on the singleton's shore. The two shores are the only minimum covers because the incidence graph is connected and regular. All retained diagonals therefore report the singleton's shore. The rank proof charges one leaf at each of its thirteen vertices and one opposite-shore leaf for each retained flag. The singleton has at least one original physical occurrence and r expanded ones. These extra copies contradict equality.

For the remaining cases the direct bound forces r=2 and t=d−1. Expand one shore to physical leaves at both occurrences and leave the other virtual, trying each choice of mates there. For (1,1) expand both shores. Write W for the reported cover enlarged by the damaged vertices. The rank test at v gives one charge if v lies in W, plus one charge for each retained edge at v reported at its opposite endpoint. If one endpoint is outside W it must take the edge charge. Otherwise try both assignments. Enumerate all covers of size at most the expanded leaf budget minus the number of retained flags. Subtract the two added leaves at each expanded label and require at least one original physical leaf of every label. All mate tests and both shore tests must use the same original counts. No resulting label vector has sum at most 64−d. The replay checks complete simultaneous incidence-permutation orbits and their sizes.

For a point-by-line rectangle, use the [rectangle test with extra positive rows](plane-pair-sharing-3773.md#one-shared-point-by-line-rectangle) at the stricter target of 65 gates. The three original row sets and extra-row tests leave 468 choices of two four-element shores, out of the 67,092,481 supports. Allow any subset of the active rows whose crosses activate the helper and whose endpoint forcing tests pass. Keep that subset and every inactive row with a masked cross or with no endpoint on a selected active row. These conditions are precisely those needed to force physical labels on every retained diagonal and adjacent off-diagonal comparison. Applying the same extra-row test to these sets reaches 65 for all 468 choices.

## A sole shared signal rejecting singletons

Let B be its active flags and T their d endpoints. With K=E minus B the leaf bound gives

    s ≥ |K| + ν(K) + d + (t−d+1) + (r−2).

Begin with every induced edge on T in B. Each edge removed from B increases the right side by at least one. For d ≤ 9 the replay leaves only the following supports with the first three terms at most 64.

| Vertices | Induced edges | Supports |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | 8 | 1,404 |
| 8 | 9 | 468 |
| 9 | 8 | 156 |
| 9 | 9 | 16,848 |
| 9 | 10 | 9,360 |

The bound is 64 throughout except for 936 nine-vertex, ten-edge supports where it is 63.

A read-once cold producer is an OR of products on disjoint variable sets, as far as its flag hits are concerned. An OR splits into disjoint supports. At an AND root a hit must cross the two children, and its endpoints must make the respective children true singly. Since every variable participates, both children are disjunctions. Split the two possible point-line orientations and then the connected components of each product's incidence graph. This preserves all flag hits, decreases values on independent inputs and uses at most one less gate than the number of leaves. Pull safe products out of the producer OR and into the final output, one OR removed for each OR appended. Zero remaining unsafe products or one remaining rectangle is impossible by the preceding bounds. Two remaining unsafe connected products each need at least four vertices. At d ≤ 9 they each have at most five, leaving no room for a separate nonempty safe product. Their incidence graphs are forests, so |B| ≤ d−2. This is impossible at d=8 or 9. Four edge-disjoint perfect matchings give ν(K) ≥ 13−⌊|B|/4⌋, already enough in the displayed inequality. The smaller supports were excluded directly.

If some variable is repeated, only the 936 cases with a spare gate survive. Here all ten induced flags must turn on H, the producer has ten leaves on nine inputs, and there are exactly two reads. In every case the induced graph is connected. There is just one cut vertex, whose removal leaves a singleton y and a connected seven-vertex component. The replay checks every cut.

Call the repeated variable x. At an OR root both children are cold. If their supports are disjoint, the induced graph would disconnect. If both contain x, there can be no edge between their exclusive vertices. Each child contains an exclusive vertex since a nonconstant monotone formula on x alone accepts its singleton. Thus x is the cut vertex. The child on x,y is exactly the safe edge xy and can be pulled to the output, leaving a read-once cold producer on the other component together with x. Its connected flag graph on those eight vertices has nine edges. An OR root cannot disconnect that graph. Its AND root must split the two shores, so the remaining producer is a rectangle.

At an AND root with disjoint supports, connectivity and acceptance of every edge force the two supports to be the two shores and each child to be their disjunction. If both children contain x, every edge avoiding x crosses their exclusive supports. On each nontrivial connected component after deleting x this partition is by shores and every vertex triggers its own child singly. If deleting x leaves a connected graph, both children are therefore disjunctions, possibly also containing x. An edge from x forces its acceptance on the proper side and coldness prevents its acceptance on both sides. This gives a rectangle.

If deleting x leaves the singleton y, apply that same argument on the seven-vertex component. For clarity take x on the point shore. A neighbor of x in the large component forces the point child to accept x singly. The line child rejects x singly. To accept xy the variable y must lie in the line child. After its singleton line hits in the large component are separated, the only remaining function of x,y that matters there is either y or x AND y: it rejects x alone and accepts xy. The first case is a rectangle. In the second case H equals the eight-vertex core rectangle OR the safe edge xy, by absorption. Replace the original nine producer gates by the seven-gate core rectangle, the edge AND, and an OR at the output. The shared rectangle still has both readers and the size does not increase. This too is excluded.

## A sole shared signal accepting singletons

Let A be the nonempty set of accepted singleton labels, B the active flags disjoint from A, and T the endpoints of B. Now d=|A|+|T|. Replace H by OR(A) at its readers and append the edge function of B at the output. On flags outside B the replacement agrees; on independent sets it can only decrease the output. An orientation of the edges of B toward a minimum vertex cover gives a star formula. The new gates cost at most |A|−1+|B|+ν(B), including the output OR when B is nonempty. In particular, if B is a star forest then |B|+ν(B)=|T|. This transformation costs at most t. For |A| ≥ 2 the new circuit would have a sole shared disjunction, already impossible. If B is empty and |A|=1, redirect straight to that raw input and save the producer.

When A={x} and B is a nonempty star forest, the transformed circuit is a formula. If it is least it must be exactly the 64-gate formula of ordinary stars. Its appended branch must therefore be a union of complete stars, since it is separated from the residual formula by the output OR. Take one such star with center c. If x is on c's shore, choose a neighbor l of c missing x, possible since distinct centers have only one common neighbor. If x is opposite c, then x misses c because x is not an endpoint of B; choose any l in the star. The residual branch cannot fire on {c,l,x}: none of the stars in that branch has an active incident pair there. Yet the original suffix fires on the flag {c,l} when H is true. Turning on x in the residual branch supplies every replaced H and can only increase the other raw inputs. This contradiction excludes the singleton case as well.

For d ≤ 6, B uses at most five vertices and is a forest. A forest this small which is not a star forest must be a connected nonstar tree on four or five vertices. Its shores have sizes (2,2), (2,3) or (3,2). All its induced edges are in B since an additional edge would make a cycle of length at most five. The complement E minus B has a perfect matching in every case, checked by the replay.

If A={x}, replace H by x in the suffix alone. The resulting formula accepts E minus B and rejects all independent inputs, so it has at least 52−|B|+13 leaves by the rank argument. Its leaf count is s−t+1. But t ≥ |T|=|B|+1, forcing s ≥ 65. If |A|=2 then B must be a four-vertex path. When both hot labels are on one shore, use their disjunction as a virtual pair on E minus B. At most two more flags are deleted and the enlarged cover still costs at least thirteen. The physical suffix therefore needs at least 49−2+13=60 raw leaves. Adding t ≥ 5 and r−1 ≥ 1 gives at least 66 gates. If the two hot labels are on opposite shores, use the mixed-disjunction deleted-flag test on the same 49 positive rows. It gives at least 59 physical leaves for all 56,628 labeled cases and hence at least 65 gates. This completes the six-vertex exclusion.

The posted statement concerns unrestricted least circuits. None of the subclass arguments decides it or its opposite.
