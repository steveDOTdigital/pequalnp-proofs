# The eight-way multiplexer at 23 gates

[L-meta-9939](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-9939) asks whether the multiplexer on three address bits and eight data bits has an AND/OR/NOT circuit with at most 23 gates. The usual circuit uses 24. The [verifier](multiplexer-9939.cjs) evaluated it on all 2,048 inputs, using the first address bit as the least significant bit exactly as in the posted expression. The unrestricted 23-gate question remains open.

**The exact minimum is 24 gates if each data bit occurs once in the circuit unfolded from its output.** The address subcircuit may use arbitrary AND, OR and NOT gates with unrestricted sharing. NOT may also be applied anywhere on a data path. Thus any 23-gate circuit for the posted function must reuse a data input or share an intermediate signal that depends on data. The 24-gate control attains this restricted minimum. The [extension to repeated data](#repeated-data-and-shared-preprocessing) also covers shared data-only logic and repeated leaves whose opposite signs are separated.

## Counting the address circuits

Represent each function of the address by its eight-bit truth table. Starting with the three address variables, add one new table at a time by AND or OR of two available tables, or NOT of one table. The enumeration deduplicates equal sets of available tables. Constants and copies can be removed by propagation or folding. Gates unused by the data tree may likewise be omitted.

For each pair of different addresses i and j, some available signal must be true at i and false at j. Otherwise when only data bit i is true, monotonicity in the available address signals would force the circuit's output at i to be at most its output at j. Of 202,322 different libraries generated through six address gates, 5,340 satisfy this condition. Address-coordinate permutations preserve the cost. Complementing all address bits and all signals simultaneously also preserves it: dualize AND and OR throughout, complement the data assignments and relabel which data bit is selected. The three source predicates map to source predicates.

## Computing the exact tree cost

Apart from gates with an address operand, a tree joining eight distinct data leaves has exactly seven binary gates. To find the least possible number of gates with an address operand, solve each subtree for three disjoint collections of addresses. On the first collection U, the subtree must return the data bit named by the address; these are precisely its leaves. The other two collections specify rows where it must be constantly one or constantly zero. All remaining rows are unconstrained.

An AND with an address predicate can discharge required zeros where that predicate is false. It must stay true at every required projection and one. An OR can discharge ones by the dual rule. For an OR joining two subtrees, split U into their two nonempty leaf sets. Each child must be zero on all rows where its sibling supplies the requested bit. On a required zero both children must be zero. On a required one at least one must be constantly one, since the children's data sets are disjoint and assignments falsifying both could otherwise be combined. Try every allocation of these ones. The AND recurrence interchanges zero and one. A single data leaf needs no further gate when it has no constant requirements. These operations exhaust the possible roots, and every transition corresponds to a circuit.

## All read-once data paths

The [full verifier](multiplexer-tree-9939.cjs) extends the lower bound to any number of address gates and allows NOT anywhere in the data tree. Remove unused gates and constant or repeated address predicates before counting. Write h for the number of address gates that remain. There are always seven joins of two data subtrees. In addition, each of the eight leaves needs at least one AND or OR with an address operand before its first join with a different data leaf. At an address selecting a leaf from that sibling, the first leaf's operand must take the neutral value of the join. A bare bit or a sequence of NOTs cannot be constant on that row. The eight paths before their first joins are disjoint. Consequently h at least nine already costs at least 24 gates.

With no data-dependent NOT and seven address gates, a 23-gate attempt can use at most nine address operands on the data tree. None of the 59,689 address libraries passing the antichain test works. There are 5,286 representatives under the symmetries above. With eight address gates, only eight such operands are available, and none of the 58,828 address-library representatives works. The enumeration reached every useful library of up to seven gates before extending to eight. When two rows are still comparable after seven gates, adding one AND or OR of existing signals cannot make them incomparable. In that case only a NOT need be tried as the last address gate.

To check many libraries without repeating the same tree search, the verifier compiles the bounded recurrence above into tests for the existence of an address predicate with prescribed zeros and ones. For an AND operand, enumerate the subset of required zeros left for the child; the predicate must pass those zeros, all projections and all required ones, and discharge the other zeros. The OR rule is dual. At a join try every split of the remaining gate budget. Batching these Boolean tests in a machine word gives an independent result for each library. Supplying all eight singleton address predicates is a positive control: eight data operands then suffice.

If any NOT acts on data, at least two do. Each data leaf has a unique path to the output, and the parity of the NOTs on that path must be even: at its own address the output is its positive, nonconstant bit. Push all data-path NOTs to the leaves by De Morgan's laws for a lower bound. They cancel on data leaves and can complement address operands. Grant all those address complements for free. The resulting positive-data tree must fit within 14−h address operands after paying for seven joins and at least two original NOTs. This is impossible immediately for h at least seven. For h from zero through six, test **all** address libraries, including those that fail the antichain condition before the free complements. Their respective symmetry-class counts are 1, 2, 10, 54, 324, 2,149 and 15,298. Only three classes survive: one with h=0 and two with h=2.

For those three, the verifier uses the original address library and charges for actual data NOT gates. The subtree state also records whether each selected projection must be the data bit or its complement. The polarity is uniform within a subtree because the path above its root is shared. A root NOT swaps constant zero and one requirements and flips the polarity. Two consecutive NOTs cancel, so the child of a root NOT may be required to have a different root operation. The exact minima for address operands plus data NOTs in these three classes are 22, 18 and 19, respectively. Adding their seven joins and address gates gives 29, 27 and 28 gates. None reaches 23.

Run `node --max-old-space-size=640 notes/multiplexer-tree-9939.cjs` for the full read-once check. The unrestricted posted statement and its negation remain undecided by this calculation.

## Repeated data and shared preprocessing

The optimum stays at **24** for the following larger class. Address-only and data-only signals may be shared arbitrarily. Any signal with both address and data ancestors has at most one path to the output. In the unfolded circuit, each data variable may occur any number of times. For a variable occurring with both signs, require one binary gate whose two input subtrees contain respectively all its positive and all its negative occurrences. The sign of a leaf is the parity of the NOTs on its path to the output. In particular the condition holds whenever each variable occurs at most twice, and it also holds for arbitrarily many all-positive occurrences.

### Removing separated negative occurrences

Unfold the circuit and push NOTs towards the leaves to choose substitutions. This produces an AND/OR formula monotone in the values of its signed leaves. The substitutions will be applied to the original formula, so this conceptual rewrite costs no gates.

Fix a data bit d with separated signs. Tie its positive leaves to an independent bit u and its negative literals to an independent bit v. Fix all other input values. On the address selecting d, the formula has values zero at (u,v)=(0,1) and one at (1,0). Monotonicity then forces the value to be u on all four pairs. At any other address, the values at (0,1) and (1,0) coincide. At the separating gate its children are functions of u alone and v alone respectively. The surrounding formula, with other inputs fixed, is a constant or the identity as a function of that gate. For an OR separating gate the value at (1,1) is the OR of the two diagonal values: each child attains its larger value on one of the two diagonals. For an AND separating gate, the corresponding identity holds at (0,0) with AND. These identities also hold for the constant surrounding context.

Set all negative literals to one for an OR separating gate and to zero for an AND separating gate. On the selecting address the value stays u; on every other address the diagonal identities show the value is unchanged. The choice of constant is independent of the address and all the other inputs. Repeat for every variable with both signs, keeping the original tree positions during this step. A variable with negative occurrences only could not return its own positive bit at its selecting address.

### Keeping one positive occurrence per data bit

At address i, set all the positive copies of the selected bit to the same value b. All occurrences of all other data bits may now take independent values. Monotonicity bounds the result between setting all these other bits to zero and setting them all to one. The multiplexer returns b at both endpoints, so the result is b for every independent choice.

In any such fixed-address subtree that returns zero with all selected copies low and one with all of them high, one occurrence suffices. At an OR root both children must give zero with the selected copies low. At least one child must give one for *every* assignment of the other independent leaves with the selected copies high. If neither did, assignments making both children zero could be combined. Retain that child recursively, and fix the selected copies in its sibling to zero. At an AND root use the dual rule, fixing the discarded copies to one. This gives one surviving occurrence of bit i and fixed constants for its other occurrences. It works for all assignments of the other leaves. Make these choices for each i at its own address. The choices can coexist: at each address the construction already allowed arbitrary values on all leaves belonging to the other bits.

### Paying for the specialized circuit

Apply the chosen raw-input constants to the original unfolded circuit and fold constant gates. The address-only circuitry can be reused in its original form. Original gates with both kinds of ancestor have only one path to the output, so specializing them incurs at most their original cost. Any purely data-dependent subtree now has at most one remaining essential bit. Otherwise two different addresses would each have to recover a different independent bit from the same one-bit data signal: for fixed values outside this subtree its unique path above it is a unary Boolean function. A unary function recovering a nonconstant bit must be the identity or negation. The subtree would have to equal a literal of each of two different variables, which is impossible. Equivalently, after ordinary constant folding, a read-once formula with disjoint essential children cannot hide an inessential variable. The surviving pure data subtrees are therefore constants or literals.

Positive literals cost nothing. To pay for negative literals, take the subgraph of the original pure-data circuitry feeding its pure NOT gates. If it has k connected components, n distinct input variables, B binary gates and U unary gates, counting edges gives n ≤ B+k. Each component contains at least one NOT, so k ≤ U. Any needed negative literal belongs to one of these n inputs: changing a positive raw literal to negative in its pure-data subtree requires an original NOT on its path. One NOT per needed input therefore costs at most B+U, within the original pure-data gate count. Constants produced by simplification can be propagated; the final output is nonconstant.

The specialized circuit consequently costs no more than the original and reads each data bit once. The exact read-once minimum above proves the lower bound, and the standard circuit attains it. A 23-gate solution must either share a signal involving both kinds of input or have a data bit with at least three occurrences whose positive and negative leaves lack the stated separation. This still leaves unrestricted circuits to investigate.

### Shared gates that mask a data signal

The same lower bound permits two further kinds of mixed-signal sharing under the sign condition above. The choice of surviving occurrences is unchanged.

First, a shared AND or OR gate may combine an address-only operand with a data-only operand that uses just **one distinct data bit**. Across all unfolded copies of this gate, at most one copy can retain an occurrence of that bit. In every other copy the data operand becomes a constant; the gate folds to a constant or its existing address operand. One copy costs at most the one original gate. This works simultaneously for all shared gates of this form. The preceding count still pays for any necessary pure-data negations.

Second, if all pure-data gates use AND or OR, the shared gate's pure-data operand may involve **any number of data bits**. Suppose r copies of such a gate survive with a data-dependent operand. Each operand must be a single positive literal, by the unary-path argument above, and those r literals are distinct. Compared with the one original mixed gate this costs at most r−1 extra gates when r is positive. To pay for these, take the union of the pure-data ancestor subgraphs of the relevant operands, including the bare input vertex when it is itself an operand. In one connected component with n source variables and B binary gates, the underlying edge count gives n−1 ≤ B. Each paid extra corresponds to a distinct retained source in this component, and at least one retained source per component belongs to a first copy already paid for. Thus all extra mixed gates in that component cost at most n−1. Those B original data gates are available to pay for them because every surviving pure-data subtree has folded to a bare positive literal or constant. The special mixed gates cannot be nested inside each other's data-only operands, so the retained sources used in this count are disjoint. All other mixed gates must have at most one path to the output for this conclusion.

In particular, when every data-dependent gate is monotone, sharing arbitrarily many masks of the form “address predicate AND/OR pure-data function” still cannot achieve 23 unless some more deeply mixed signal is also reused.

### Direct search for a 23-gate circuit

The [rewrite verifier](multiplexer-rewrites-9939.cjs) also searches rewiring operations that can introduce mixed sharing or change data polarity. Start with the 24-gate binary selector tree, with address negations built first and selectors in bottom-up order. Choose the sum-of-products or product-of-sums implementation independently at all seven selector nodes, giving 128 controls. All pass the 2,048-row truth table.

Delete any one gate, aliasing it to any earlier wire or to the unavailable-wire value false. At any other gate, allow any AND, OR or NOT over earlier available wires. This includes constants and single-wire projections under the posted evaluator. For each repair location, clamp its output first to zero and then to one to determine exactly which input rows require each value from the replacement. If both clamps fail on one row no repair at that location is possible. Otherwise test every legal replacement against the required rows. A deliberately damaged control is repaired by the same method and verified on all inputs.

There were 72,192 deletions with a chosen alias and 1,660,416 deletion/repair locations. Of those, 282,912 admitted consistent row requirements. No available replacement gate met them. This search does not exclude other rewiring operations or arbitrary 23-gate circuits. Run `node notes/multiplexer-rewrites-9939.cjs` from the proof fork to reproduce it.
