# Exact monotone circuit size on pseudoforests without four-cycles

Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph with at least one edge and no cycle of length four. Triangles are allowed. If each connected component contains at most one cycle, the minimum number of binary AND and OR gates computing “the selected vertices contain an edge” is
\[
                         |E(G)|+\tau(G)-1 ,
\]
where \(\tau\) is the minimum vertex-cover size. Isolated inputs cost nothing. This settles the pseudoforest subclass of L-meta-1739. On the bipartite instances of L-meta-3456 the optimal circuit constructed below has every AND false at every singleton. These conclusions allow unrestricted wire sharing within and between components. A second consequence, valid for arbitrary four-cycle-free graphs of minimum degree at least two, is the lower bound \(|V|+\tau-1\).

## The upper bound and the graph reduction

Assign every edge to an endpoint in a minimum cover. Each chosen center owns at least one edge, since otherwise the other centers already cover everything. Form the OR of the owned neighbors at each center, AND it with the center, and OR all the stars. The costs are respectively \(m-\tau,\tau,\tau-1\), where \(m=|E|\). The AND operands use disjoint vertices.

Argue by the number of edges. Use the [leaf-peeling lemma](cyclic-core-peeling.md) when a leaf has a parent of degree at most two or a sibling leaf. Briefly, extend the target by arbitrary disjoint OR literals. Each such literal costs one extra gate above a nonempty edge function, by pinning it to zero and bypassing its reader. Pinning one sibling leaf to zero pays for its lost edge and preserves cover size. At a degree-two parent, either pin of the leaf decreases the proposed cost by at most two (the high pin produces an isolated OR literal). At least one pin saves two gates if both cofactors are nonconstant, because a controlling pin at a first reader also simplifies a consumer. A degree-one parent with another surviving edge satisfies the same argument. The isolated-edge endpoint has size one. See the linked argument for the constant cases. Pinning and simplifying AND/OR gates preserves monotonicity.

Omit isolated vertices. A surviving tree component would have a peelable leaf. In a component with a unique cycle, a nonleaf outside the cycle farthest from the cycle has either two leaf children or degree two with a leaf child. Thus every remaining component is a cycle with at most one pendant leaf at each cycle vertex. Write \(P\) for the cycle vertices with leaves, \(k=|P|\), and \(B\) for the other cycle vertices. Each component has the same number of edges as active vertices, hence \(m=n\). A minimum cover can contain all of \(P\) by exchanging a selected private leaf for its center. Consequently
\[
                    \tau(G)=k+\tau(G[B]).
\]

## Where singleton signals must split

Fold constants, unary gates and repeated operands, choose the live output cone, and pin isolated inputs to zero. This yields a circuit with \(n\) essential inputs and \(s\) strictly binary gates. Every wire except the output has positive fanout. Summing fanout minus one over those wires gives \(2s-(n+s-1)=s-n+1\). In particular at most that many wires can have multiple readers.

For a vertex \(v\), follow the only reader from its input while there is exactly one reader, stopping at the output or at the first wire with several readers. The path up to and including that last wire is its private prefix. Every path from the raw input to the output passes through every wire on the prefix. Write “hot on \(u\)” for being true on the singleton assignment \(\{u\}\).

**Purity.** A prefix wire for a vertex \(v\) of degree at least two cannot be hot on any different \(u\). Choose a neighbor \(w\) of \(v\) that is not adjacent to \(u\). Such a neighbor exists because two distinct common neighbors would give a four-cycle. The choice \(w=u\) is valid. The assignment \(\{u,w\}\) is negative and adding \(v\) makes it positive. If the wire is hot on \(u\), monotonicity makes it true on both assignments. Every influence from \(v\) passes through the wire, so the output cannot distinguish them.

Mark \(v\) if the first wire with several readers on its prefix is hot on \(v\). This charges a distinct shared wire to every marked vertex of degree at least two, by purity. If \(v\) is unmarked, there is a first wire \(A\) on the prefix that is cold on \(v\), since the output is cold on every singleton. It is an AND, with the previous hot wire as one operand. Its other operand \(H\) is independent of \(v\). An earlier path from \(v\) to \(H\) would require branching or a repeated operand. For each neighbor \(u\), the output changes between \(\{u\}\) and \(\{u,v\}\), and every path from \(v\) to the output passes through \(A\). Hence \(A\) must turn on and \(H\) is hot on \(u\).

If \(u\) is itself unmarked, the only wires hot on \(u\) belong to its prefix strictly before its first cold AND. Indeed every wire off that initial chain either ignores \(u\) or receives all influence from \(u\) through that AND, which is zero at both the empty and singleton assignments. If \(u\) has degree at least two, purity makes each of those hot wires accept only \(u\) among singletons. The preceding \(H\) accepts two different neighbors of \(v\) when \(v\) has degree at least two. Thus two adjacent vertices of degree at least two cannot both be unmarked. This directly gives at least \(\tau(G[B])\) distinct bare-vertex charges. Applied to the entire vertex set of a graph of minimum degree two or greater, it also proves the stated \(|V|+\tau-1\) lower bound without any pseudoforest hypothesis.

## One more charge for each pendant center

Among least circuits, minimize the sum over AND gates of their numbers of distinct OR ancestors. In this circuit a pendant center \(p\) cannot have just one reader of the form \(p\lor H\). To see this, \(H\) is independent of \(p\) by acyclicity and the sole-reader hypothesis. If it were true on an independent set omitting \(p\), add the private leaf \(z\). The set remains independent and \(H\) remains true. Now add \(p\). The edge \(pz\) turns the output on, although the only reader of \(p\) was already saturated. Thus \(H\) implies the whole target on all inputs, also when \(p\) is present, by deleting \(p\) first. Bypass the old OR by \(p\) and OR \(H\) back at the final output. This computes the same function with the same number of gates before trimming. There must be an AND descendant of the removed OR, since any all-OR path from it to the output would accept the singleton \(p\). No new OR becomes an ancestor of any AND. This strictly reduces the chosen sum or, after simplification, circuit size. Both contradict the choice.

Suppose a least circuit has \(s\le m+\tau-2\). For \(p\in P\), the graph \(G-p\) is nonempty, loses three edges, and loses exactly one cover vertex (use a minimum cover containing \(p\), and conversely add \(p\) to any cover of the residual). Induction gives size \(m+\tau-5\) for that residual. Pin \(p=0\) and its now irrelevant private leaf \(z=1\). At most three distinct gates may be removed by any valid simplification.

If \(p\) has several readers, charge the raw wire \(p\). Otherwise, if \(z\) has several readers, charge the raw wire \(z\). In the remaining case the sole reader of \(p\) is \(A=p\land H\). As above \(H\) is hot on all three neighbors of \(p\). In particular it accepts the singleton \(z\). The sole reader of \(z\) cannot be an AND: on \(\{z\}\) its other, independent operand is zero, and all wires depending on \(z\) downstream would remain at their empty-input values. Thus its reader has the form \(L=z\lor S\), and \(H\) is either \(L\) or downstream of \(L\).

If \(H\ne L\), choose a first successor \(J\) on a path from \(L\) to \(H\), and a reader \(K\) of \(A\). A reader exists since \(p=0\) leaves a nonconstant output. Under the stated pin, \(L\) becomes one, \(A\) becomes zero, and each of \(J,K\) can be simplified. These are four distinct gates in topological order \(L,J,A,K\), contradicting the budget. Hence \(H=L\).

The sibling \(S\) accepts the two distinct cycle neighbors of \(p\). It accepts no other singleton. It ignores \(p,z\) by the two sole-reader assumptions and acyclicity. For a different vertex \(u\) outside those neighbors, \(\{p,u\}\) is negative but adding \(z\) makes it positive. If \(S\) were hot on \(u\), the only reader \(L\) of \(z\) would already be saturated. Thus \(S\) has exactly the two cycle-neighbor singleton hits and is an internal wire. If its only reader were \(L\), pinning \(p=0,z=1\) would remove \(A,L,K\) and make \(S\) dead. This also saves four. Therefore charge the shared wire \(S\).

Different centers have disjoint raw options. A shared internal wire charged to two distinct centers would make the same two distinct vertices common neighbors of both centers, giving a forbidden four-cycle. Each internal center charge has exactly two singleton hits, whereas every bare charge has one. A raw center or leaf also cannot be a bare charge, since it fires only on itself. We have at least \(k+\tau(G[B])=\tau\) distinct shared wires. The fanout count now gives \(s\ge n+\tau-1=m+\tau-1\), the required contradiction.

## Verification and scope

[Lean verifies](PendantOrLift.lean) the private-leaf exposure, semantic masking argument and safe sibling implication. On the project's actual indexed gate lists it verifies the output of the OR relocation on all inputs, equality of sizes, preservation of binary monotone legality, and the earlier-wire bounds for the renamed suffix. A [second Lean file](PseudoforestCharge.lean) proves singleton purity from masking, the vertex-cover implication from the two prefix properties and uniqueness of two-neighbor charges. The arbitrary-DAG path arguments, restriction budget and the complete pseudoforest induction above are written proofs. The signed AND/OR/NOT version is open here.

An [independent finite check](pendant-cycle-check.py) verifies the exact costs of all eight placements of private leaves on a pentagon up to symmetry. For the two cases where earlier elementary restrictions fell short, it checks infeasibility at 10 and 13 gates with CaDiCaL's proof checker enabled. This calculation uses cofactor bounds and the OR relocation, independently of the fanout charging proof. Run the two Lean files from the repository's Lean directory using `lake env lean ../notes/FILE.lean`, and the finite check from the repository root using `python3 -B notes/pendant-cycle-check.py 5 --seconds 35`. Neither desk's unrestricted statement is resolved by this result.
