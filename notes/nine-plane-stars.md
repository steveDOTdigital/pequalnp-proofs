# Nine ordinary stars cannot fit below 64 gates

For L-meta-650, a binary monotone circuit for incidence of the order-three projective plane with ordinary stars at nine points, or at nine lines, has at least 64 gates. With eight stars the same lower bound holds when four of the five unfinished lines are concurrent, or dually. The 13-star construction meets 64. The unrestricted 63-gate search remains open. The [earlier equality result](plane-ten-star-equality.md) characterizes the 64-gate circuits containing ten same-side stars.

An ordinary line star joins its four incident raw points by three binary ORs and ANDs the result with its raw line bit. Any intermediate OR may have arbitrary further readers. Two completed stars cannot share any of these gates because different lines meet in only one point. The nine stars therefore cost 36 gates, schedulable first. Suppose a 63-gate circuit contains them. Its remaining circuit has at most 27 gates.

## Mandatory sources in the remaining circuit

Each completed line contributes at least one distinct source, its raw line bit or its completed star. Turning on just that line and varying an incident point proves this. For every point \(p\) on an unfinished line \(l\), its raw bit must occur. Turn on just \(l\) and all points outside \(l\). Toggling \(p\) changes the output. Every nontrivial completed-line point OR involving \(p\) is already one because the two lines intersect only at \(p\); every completed star is zero. Each unfinished line bit is necessary as well.

Turning off the completed lines thus pins at least nine sources in the suffix. Turning off \(t\) mandatory raw points pins another \(t\). For a nonconstant residual these operations cost at least \(9+t\) gates. To see this, regard the used prefix wires as distinct formal sources before substituting any remaining correlations. In a connected binary output cone with \(g\) gates and \(r\) sources the underlying multigraph has cycle rank \(g-r+1\). Propagating constants, contracting copies and pruning a dead branch give a connected minor. For the surviving \(g',r'\), this gives \(g'-r'\le g-r\) and \(r'\le r-9-t\). The bound still holds when two surviving sources are subsequently identified, since their readers can use one input with no new gates.

## The three possibilities for four unfinished lines

* **Three concurrent, with a transversal.** Keep the triple point and its three transversal intersections. Seven private points are pinned to zero. No completed line contains two retained points: the transversal joins each pair of its own points and the three other unfinished lines join them to the triple point. Old point helpers are therefore copies or constants. The eight-variable core consists of three internally disjoint three-edge paths between two hubs. It needs twelve gates by the [theta bound](single-read-cover-kernels.md), also a consequence of the [bicyclic-core theorem](pseudoforest-circuits.md). The total is at least \(36+9+7+12=64\).
* **All four concurrent.** All 13 points and four unfinished lines are mandatory suffix sources, in addition to the nine completed channels. A 27-gate suffix could use at most two more sources. If it used two distinct point helpers, it would have at least 28 sources and no excess fanout, so it would be a read-once tree. Let \(c\) be the common point and \(y\) an unfinished line bit. With the other inputs zero their restriction is AND. With one other line and one private point of \(y\) high, the restriction is OR. Both variables occur only as raw leaves in this test: no completed line contains \(c\), the completed line bits stay zero, and the point helpers are fixed within each test. The lowest common ancestor in a read-once tree cannot give both operations. There is at most one used point helper. Its completed line meets each unfinished line only once. Choose a private representative per unfinished line avoiding the helper, and turn the other eight private points off. The remaining four-arm subdivided star has eight edges and a four-edge matching, hence needs eleven gates by the forest bound. The total is at least \(36+9+8+11=64\).
* **No three concurrent.** Keep the six pairwise intersections and turn off the four private points. Think of the four lines as the vertices of \(K_4\) and the six retained points as its edges. Incidence is now the edge function of the once-subdivided \(K_4\). Two retained points on the same completed line must correspond to disjoint edges of \(K_4\). There are precisely three such complementary pairs and no three pairwise disjoint edges. Every surviving prefix point OR is therefore a copy or one of three binary ORs. The exact search below proves that even when all three ORs are free the ten-input core needs fifteen paid gates. The total is at least \(36+9+4+15=64\).

## Eight stars and five unfinished lines

When four of the five unfinished lines are concurrent, keep their common point and their four intersections with the fifth line. Eight private points are discarded, and no completed line contains two retained points. The core consists of four internally disjoint three-edge paths between two hubs. The search excludes fifteen gates, and the five stars on one shore cost sixteen. This gives \(32+8+8+16=64\). [Geometry and control checks](four-line-core-search.py) verify all 715 choices of four lines, divided into 468 with a triple, 13 pencils and 234 without a triple, and all 117 choices of five lines containing a pencil. They check the 16-gate circuit on all 1,024 inputs.

## Exhaustive search on live wires

The [C++ search](four-line-live-search.cpp) checks the minimal positives and maximal negatives. There are 12 positives in both cores. The once-subdivided \(K_4\) has 12 maximal negatives and the four-path graph has 17. A wire stores its value on each of these rows. A pair consisting of a positive row and a negative row must be distinguished in the direction \(1,0\) by at least one currently live wire, since the remaining program is monotone. The [Lean file](NinePlaneStars.lean) proves this condition and verifies the found 15-gate circuit at every input.

A search state is the ordered list of produced wires that have a future use, or the designated output when done. For each AND or OR of two distinct live wires, consume each operand on its last read, otherwise keep it. If \(r\) sources start live, a circuit with \(s\) gates can keep an operand on exactly \(s-r+1\) reads, counting both operands if both are kept. The recursion tries every choice within that budget. It rejects any state leaving a positive/negative pair undistinguished. Gates redundant on the test rows can be removed from a minimum circuit. For an OR one can also bypass it by an operand agreeing on all positive rows, since this only decreases the negative values. The dual test applies to an AND on all negative rows.

Order ready gates by increasing larger operand, then smaller operand. A newly ready gate reads a later wire, so a minimum circuit admits this ordering. Relabel the initial gate by the graph symmetries preserving the chosen helpers, taking the least first pair over all schedules and permitted relabelings. Each raw source is required, even with complementary helpers: saturate the free OR by its opposite point and choose an incident line at the point being toggled. Choose the subset of helpers the putative minimum actually uses. All subsets of a fixed size are equivalent under permutations of the four lines. Distinct earlier wires with identical values on every boundary row can be merged. A globally minimum circuit with any allowed helpers is therefore covered by one of these four runs.

To reproduce, compile the C++ source with a C++17 compiler and optimization. Its arguments are the number of available-and-used helpers, gate limit, state limit and wall-time limit in seconds. For the last row append the argument `theta`. A run hitting either limit reports `INCOMPLETE`. `python3 -B notes/four-line-core-search.py --geometry` verifies the geometry and the second positive control. The Python file also contains an independent, optional SAT formulation; the exhaustive exclusions in the table use the C++ search.
