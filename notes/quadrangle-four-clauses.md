# Five-clause terms on the order-three projective plane

A product of four raw disjunctions can separate a prescribed family of incident pairs from every quadrangle input if and only if that family has a vertex cover of size at most two. Five clauses imply a cover of size at most three. More generally, a binary monotone circuit that rejects all quadrangles and whose accepted incidence graph has cover size at most three needs at least $|E|+\tau-1$ gates when $E$ is nonempty. These statements give a 64-gate lower bound for an OR of private terms with at most five clauses per term. The accompanying 66-gate example satisfies the nesting and strictness conditions in L-meta-2588. General circuits with shared gates are still open.

## Exact heptad cover counts

A flag is the pair of raw variables consisting of a point and an incident line. A quadrangle input consists of four points with no three collinear together with the three lines missing those points. There are 52 flags and 234 quadrangles. Call a term safe if it rejects every quadrangle. Every same-shore pair, singleton and nonincident point-line pair extends to a quadrangle input, as verified in the [three-clause argument](quadrangle-three-clause.md). A safe monotone term therefore rejects all of them.

Fix a matching of $t$ flags. Each clause in a product accepting those flags contains at least one endpoint of every flag. Shrink it to exactly one endpoint per flag. This preserves the specified positives and can only remove other positives. There are $2^t$ choices. A quadrangle must miss at least one selected clause. We computed the minimum number of choices that suffice, equivalently the least $k$ in the project's `HeptadCover.HasCover` for that matching. Complements of the clauses are exactly the free sets in this formulation, with an arbitrary free set enlarged to the complement of a canonical clause.

| Matching size | Minimum clauses and number of matchings |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | 3 on 468, 4 on 702 |
| 3 | 5 on 6,084, 6 on 6,084, 7 on 1,872, 8 on 936 |
| 4 | 6 on 1,521, 7 on 9,360, 8 on 52,416, 9 on 29,016, 10 on 19,656, 11 on 5,616, 12 on 2,808, 15 on 234 |

The [enumeration](quadrangle-four-clauses.py) starts with every matching on the 52 actual flags. For each ordered quadrangle it extends a fixed projective frame by repeated joins and intersections, checks that every line maps to a line and retains the induced permutation of flags. It checks that the 5,616 permutations are distinct and that the resulting orbits partition all matchings. There are two, nine and 39 orbits respectively. The three-matching counts sum to 14,976 and the four-matching counts to 120,627. For four flags the search keeps the inclusion-minimal sets of clauses that each quadrangle can miss. It branches on every clause in a least-size remaining set, deleting just the constraints it satisfies. This recurrence attains the minimum and checks its chosen cover on all 234 rows.

For all three matching sizes the program also finds a lower certificate attaining the exact computed minimum. Choose quadrangles so that the union of any two contains both endpoints of a required flag. No free set can cover two chosen quadrangles. [Lean](QuadrangleFourClauses.lean) proves the injection into every covering family and checks explicit certificates against the project definitions for all fifty representatives. The upper witnesses and the orbit coverage are verified by the Python enumeration.

By König's theorem, four clauses now imply a vertex cover of size at most two and five clauses imply a cover of size at most three. For the four-clause converse assign the desired edges to two cover vertices. Their full edge function is the union of two stars, $(c\land A)\lor(d\land B)$, where $A,B$ are ORs of the respective assigned neighbors. Distribute to obtain the four clauses $c\lor d,c\lor B,A\lor d,A\lor B$. A single star uses two clauses, and the empty target can use a trivially rejecting product on the promise, for example three distinct collinear point inputs conjoined. The eight-flag maximum for a four-clause product is attained by two full line stars.

## Gate cost for an arbitrary safe cone with a small cover

Let $H$ contain **every** accepted flag. Every endpoint is essential, since switching it off in a two-input positive leaves a rejected singleton. Fix the inputs outside the endpoints to zero and keep a live binary output cone. Pinning $s$ additional distinct live sources to zero while retaining a nonconstant function loses at least $s$ gates. Indeed the undirected multigraph of a binary cone with $g$ gates and $n$ sources has cycle rank $g-n+1$. Constant propagation, bypassing unary gates and trimming dead paths produce a connected minor with no larger cycle rank.

A read-once tree rejecting singletons has a star forest as its graph of positive pairs if that graph is bipartite and four-cycle-free. At an OR root both children reject singletons and no cross pair is accepted. At an AND root no internal pair is accepted and the cross pairs form a rectangle on the singleton supports of the two children. Such a rectangle is a star. All gates are monotone and there are no constants.

Suppose first $H$ is a forest with $m$ edges, $c$ nonempty components and matching number $t\le3$. It has $m+c$ essential vertices, so the raw source count gives $m+c-1$ gates. This suffices when $c=t$. If $t=c+1$ at least one component contains a disjoint pair of edges and is not a star. A tree circuit is impossible, giving $m+c$ gates. The remaining case is one connected tree with $t=3$.

Such a tree contains one of two induced six-vertex trees. Diameter at least five gives a six-vertex path. Otherwise root at a center of radius at most two. At least two branches contain a second-level vertex, since all other edges would meet the center. There must also be a third branch, since the two first-level vertices otherwise cover all edges. Retain two long branches and one short branch. The cores have edges $01,12,23,34,45$ and $01,02,03,14,25$ respectively.

Each core needs at least seven gates even if all inputs of weight three or more are unspecified. With six essential sources a six-gate live circuit has one excess use. Exactly one wire is read twice and the rest once. If the repeated wire is internal, its subtree uses a private block of at least two inputs. A proper block with edges to the outside must have a singleton making the wire true. With every other input zero the downstream output therefore rejects a true block wire, since it rejects singletons. The block has no internal positive pair. Every essential vertex in the block has to accept a singleton at that wire to participate in a cross pair, giving identical outside neighborhoods. Both cores are connected and twin-free. A block containing all inputs could be replaced by its own output since every later gate depends only on that wire. This leaves a read-once circuit, already excluded.

If the repeated wire is raw, split it into two leaves. The program tests all six possible repeated inputs for each core. For a fixed leaf subset it forms the exact truth signatures on the six singletons and fifteen pairs. Every full binary formula splits its leaves into two disjoint nonempty subsets and joins their signatures by intersection or union. Conversely each such join is a valid formula on those leaves. The two occurrences use the same truth column. None of the twelve searches accepts exactly the required edges. Restoring the other essential vertices by the pinning bound gives $m+2$ gates for the whole tree.

Finally, if $H$ contains a cycle then $t=3$ forces a six-cycle. A three-vertex cover on it must be one of its two alternating shores. Those three vertices already share distinct cycle neighbors in pairs, so any extra edge is a private leaf and there is no separate nonempty component. The three cycle points are noncollinear and the three cycle lines nonconcurrent. The two full shores extend to negative quadrangles, as do the three opposite nonincident pairs. For the points choose a fourth outside the three sides. For the lines take the four points outside their union, which form a quadrangle: a further line meeting the union in fewer than two points would meet all three sides at a concurrent point. These five sets are the maximal independent sets of the cycle. Its entire function is forced by monotonicity on the restricted variables. The [six-cycle bound](plane-ten-star-equality.md) gives eight gates. Each private leaf adds at least one, giving $m+2$ again. This proves the stated gate bound for every safe cone with $t\le3$.

## Combining terms and accounting for overlap

Let the output be the OR of $r$ nonempty safe terms with no shared internal gates between terms. Even arbitrary internal circuitry is allowed if its accepted flag graph $H_i$ has matching number at most three. For example, any safe product of up to five raw clauses qualifies, with arbitrary sharing inside the term. The gate lower bounds add, and the output needs another $r-1$ ORs. This gives at least $\sum_i |E(H_i)| + \sum_i \tau(H_i)-1$. The first sum covers all 52 flags. The chosen vertex covers together cover a 13-edge perfect matching, so the second sum is at least 13. The result is 64. Singleton terms or empty terms that contribute no positive edges can be omitted on the promise.

There is also a sufficient condition with shared gates. Count a gate used by $j$ distinct term cones as $j-1$ shared uses, with the final OR tree outside those cones. Subtract this total from the displayed lower bound. Thus the 64-gate result still holds whenever the shared uses are at most the sum of the excess edge counts over 52 and the excess cover counts over 13. General sharing need not meet this condition.

## A 66-gate circuit satisfying the desk hypotheses

Choose a point $p$ on line $L$. On the other three lines through $p$, choose their intersections $b_1,b_2,b_3$ with a transversal missing $p$. In each of those three ordinary line stars build the point disjunction starting with $p\lor b_i$. Combine those three existing wires with two AND gates and substitute the resulting wire for the occurrence of $p$ in the star of $L$. On a singleton point input it equals $p$. On quadrangles it also equals $p$, since a quadrangle cannot contain the three collinear $b_i$. The rest of the ordinary 64-gate construction stays valid. The two added ANDs and the final AND of $L$ give three ordered AND-ancestor pairs. The program checks legal distinct earlier operands, all 52 flags, all 234 quadrangles and a separating quadrangle for each of the 30 AND operands. Lean independently verifies the 66-gate size, binary legality, distinct operands, the required lower bound of two on the project's ordered AND-pair count, the 52 positive and 234 negative outputs, and the strictness of every AND operand. Its word interpreter proves each bit agrees with the real circuit's evaluator. The full assignment allows arbitrary nested and shared circuits.
