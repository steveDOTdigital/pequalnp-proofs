# Shared gates under the degenerate tests for the order-three plane

Independent research by steveDOTdigital on L-meta-1684. The 52 positive inputs are individual flags of the cyclic plane on 13 points and 13 lines, with \(p-w\in\{0,1,3,9\}\). The desk requires rejection on 236 maximal incidence-free inputs. Thirteen ordinary point stars use 64 binary gates.

The following circuit classes all require **at least 64 gates**. A shared signal is an internal gate used at least twice in the live output cone. A safe signal is false on every desk negative. An inactive signal is false on every positive flag. Any number of other shared signals may be safe or inactive in each result.

* All remaining shared signals have their value on each positive flag determined by the singleton at its point. They may depend on both shores away from those 52 inputs. The dual statement holds on the line shore.
* There is one remaining shared signal. It is true on at least one singleton point and at least one singleton line. Its responses on the flags and all other inputs can be arbitrary subject to monotonicity.
* There is one remaining shared signal that rejects all singletons and whose private formula tree reads at most nine distinct raw inputs.
* There are up to three remaining shared gates, each an OR of two raw inputs. Four such ORs also satisfy the bound if each pair lies entirely on one shore. These conclusions allow replacement by gates having exactly the same singleton and flag responses as the indicated ORs.

The unrestricted 63-gate question remains open. The local circuit search below is a separate attempt to find a counterexample.

## Which values can a circuit choose?

Of the \(2^{26}\) inputs, 67,070,134 contain a flag. Of the 38,730 incidence-free inputs, 37,326 lie below a desk negative. Among the remaining 1,404, exactly 936 are a noncollinear point triple paired with a nonconcurrent line triple. Their strict incidence-free supersets consist of 234 triangle–quadrangle pairs and 234 pairs of the dual shape. The [enumerator](degenerate-plane-profiles.py) checks all incidence-free inputs, the original closure and degeneracy tests, and every optional containment.

An independent input is forced false precisely when its points are collinear or its lines concurrent. For example, at most three concurrent lines have no other line inside their union. Every other line meets that union in at most three of its four points. Taking all exterior points therefore gives a maximal negative with exactly the selected lines. Four concurrent lines leave no selected points, so the all-lines negative suffices. Apply duality for points. For the converse, incidence-free subsets of a degenerate negative inherit its degeneracy. A noncollinear point triple misses exactly four lines, no three concurrent. The [triangle calculation](plane-arc-triangles.md) gives the dual statement and the four-point exterior counts.

The free inputs give a bipartite graph with 234 vertices on each shore. Its vertices are the maximal free inputs. Each free triple–triple input is an edge joining its two maximal extensions, obtained by taking the full exterior on one shore. The graph is simple and 4-regular. Choosing a monotone completion means choosing any subset \(S\) of its 468 vertices and then any subset of edges with both endpoints in \(S\). Thus the exact number of completions is \(\sum_{S}2^{|E(S)|}\). A maximal free input can be activated on its own. These are descriptions of Boolean functions; realizing one within the gate budget is a further condition.

## A lower bound from positive flags on one shore

Let \(F\) be any nonempty subset of flags, with matching number \(\nu(F)\). Keep all the original negative tests. Suppose each shared signal is safe, inactive on \(F\), or satisfies \(h(p,w)=h(\{p\})\) for every \((p,w)\in F\). Here a pair denotes an input with just those two variables set. Then the live circuit size satisfies
\[
s\ \ge\ |F|+\nu(F)-1.
\]

Simplify constants, invalid reads, unary identities, repeated operands and unused gates. An output which is constant or raw cannot separate a flag from its two pure-shore negatives. In the binary live DAG let \(a\) count raw operand slots and let \(\sigma\) sum excess fanout (fanout minus one) over non-output internal gates. Counting the \(2s\) operand slots gives \(s+1=a+\sigma\). Cutting at shared internal outputs makes private formula trees for each shared gate and for the output.

For each helper let \(Q\) be its set of positive singleton points. A point-profile helper with \(Q=\varnothing\) is inactive on \(F\). If \(Q=\{p\}\), replace its output by raw \(p\). This only decreases values globally and preserves every retained positive row. Processing such replacements in circuit order preserves all point-singleton values and all retained flag values, and can only shrink the live DAG. Helpers with at least two positive points can remain in the graph. Classify gates satisfying more than one condition as safe or inactive first. Assign each row to the first safe shared gate true on it, or the output if none. All earlier safe terminals in its chosen tree are false on this row.

Fix a maximum matching \(M\) of \(F\). For each point \(p\) incident to \(F\), maintain a set \(A_p\) of other points. Initially these sets are empty. Process the remaining point-profile helpers in topological order. Whenever \(p\in Q\), test this actual helper on the point-only assignment \(A_p\). If true, this helper is already covered. Otherwise compare singleton \(p\), where the helper is true, to \(A_p\) through its private formula. All earlier point helpers true at \(p\) are already true on \(A_p\). An earlier inactive terminal is false at \(p\), by monotonicity to an incident retained flag. Earlier safe terminals are false at \(p\) because the all-points assignment is a desk negative. The comparison must therefore end at a raw occurrence of \(p\) in this tree. Charge that slot. Distinct points charge different slots, and different helpers have disjoint trees.

Choose \(q\in Q-\{p\}\), outside the line matched to \(p\) if possible, and add it to \(A_p\). Monotonicity now makes the helper true and preserves all previous coverage. Remove the flag from \(p\) to the joining line \(pq\) if it was retained. At most one flag is removed per charged raw slot. A matched flag can be lost only if every point of the current \(Q\) lies on its matched line. Since two points determine one line, at most one matched flag is lost per such helper. Write \(J\) for the resulting graph, \(t\) for the number of these helpers and \(a_0\) for the raw slots in the point-helper trees. Then \(|J|\ge |F|-a_0\), \(\nu(J)\ge\nu(F)-t\), and \(t\le\sigma\).

In the assigned proof trees, route each positive row and negative column to their first differing leaf by the usual OR/AND comparison. Each leaf receives a Cartesian rectangle. The chosen tree depends only on the row. At a point \(p\) the column with point set \(A_p\) and line set \(N_J(p)\) forces every incident retained row to report raw \(p\). The column is independent by the removals, and its lines are concurrent. At a line \(w\) the all-points column forces every retained row to report raw \(w\).

For a retained edge \(pw\) consider witness rows \(pb\) and \(aw\) with different opposite endpoints. The column \(A_p\cup\{a\}\) on the point shore and \(\{b\}\) on the line shore is independent. A four-cycle would be required for \(ab\) to be a flag. Every partner in \(A_p\) avoids \(b\). This column forces raw \(p\) on \(pb\) and raw \(w\) on \(aw\). In the second row, point \(a\) itself covers every active point helper. All these comparison columns are below desk negatives.

The [leaf count for degree at most four](cubic-formula-leaves.md#charging-the-leaves) now applies to the raw leaf rectangles in the assigned proof trees. At each endpoint a forcing column meets the pattern of each incident row in one leaf. If the endpoint has \(r<d\le4\) raw slots, at least \(d-r+1\) rows contain another incident row's pattern. The witness column rules out this domination at both ends of the same edge. Counting gives at least \(|J|+\nu(J)\) proof-tree raw slots, disjoint from \(a_0\). Consequently
\[
s+1\ \ge\ |F|+\nu(F)+\sigma-t.
\]
The surplus refers to the normalized circuit. In particular \(F\) equal to the whole plane gives 64 gates.

For any unrestricted circuit, remove a set \(X\) of flags covering every failure of the chosen point-profile condition at the shared nodes other than safe or inactive nodes. Then \(s\ge51-|X|+\nu(E-X)\) whenever \(E-X\) is nonempty. The four cyclic perfect matchings partition the flags, so \(\nu(E-X)\ge 13-\lfloor |X|/4\rfloor\). This gives \(s\ge64-|X|-\lfloor |X|/4\rfloor\), also trivially valid when \(X=E\).

## A shared signal active on both shores

Let \(H\) be the sole shared signal outside the safe and inactive classes. Write \(A,B\) for its positive singleton points and lines, both nonempty. Denote the set of distinct raw labels in its private tree by \(U\). It contains \(A\cup B\). To see this, compare each such singleton to the empty input. Every earlier shared terminal is cold at the singleton. Hence the differing leaf is raw. The private tree spends at least \(|U|\) raw slots and \(H\) contributes at least one excess reader.

Every proof-tree row at a vertex has the full opposite shore as a forcing column. This masks \(H\) at both shores. For a retained flag \(pw\) and possible witness rows \(pb,aw\), start with point \(a\) and line \(b\). If some literal in \(A\cup B\) can be added while maintaining independence, the resulting column masks \(H\) on both rows. It has at most three vertices and lies below a desk test. An obstruction requires \(A\subseteq b\) and \(B\subseteq N(a)\). For this nonincident pair \(a,b\), the four candidate central flags are disjoint. Each joins one point of \(b\) to its unique line through \(a\).

For such a pair mark a possible conflict only when at least one witness row may need to mask \(H\). A row containing a positive singleton may do so. A row containing neither can need masking only if **both of its raw labels lie in \(U\)**. Indeed, in any chosen proof tree containing \(H\), all safe shared gates earlier than \(H\) are false on that row, as are all inactive gates. If \(H\) is true on the row and false on each singleton, compare the row to each singleton inside its private tree. The two differing leaves must be the two raw endpoints. If the row's proof stops before \(H\), masking \(H\) is unnecessary.

Let \(J\) be the retained flags after any voluntary omissions, \(C\) the edges with at least one remaining possible conflict, and \(V_C\) their endpoints. The [exceptional-edge charging inequality](plane-shared-formulas.md#disjunctions-of-raw-pairs-on-both-shores) gives the following gate lower bound, including the helper contribution:
\[
 |J|+|V_C|-|C|+\nu(J-V_C)+|U|.
\]
It permits many witness pairs at an exceptional edge. If \(|A|,|B|\ge2\), two points determine \(b\) and two lines determine \(a\). There is at most one obstructing pair, so \(C\) is a matching of size at most four. Deleting its endpoints destroys at most \(2|C|\) edges of a fixed perfect matching. With all flags retained the displayed bound is at least \(65-|C|+|U|\ge65\). If \(A\) is noncollinear or \(B\) nonconcurrent, there is no obstructing pair at all.

This leaves a singleton on one shore and two to four collinear or concurrent choices on the other. Translate the singleton point to zero and use duality when needed. The [helper checker](degenerate-plane-helper.py) enumerates every orbit of the other shore. It branches on the as-yet unknown raw labels that could occur in a witness row, charging one slot per known label and allowing the undecided labels to create conflicts for free. A successful bound prunes all its descendants. At a leaf the checker also tries omitting one or two witness flags. Every orbit for two, three and four singleton lines reaches 64 with arbitrary additional flag responses. Five or more lines cannot all belong to a pencil. With exactly one singleton of each kind, the two possible geometries are an incident or a nonincident pair. If the private tree spends at least nine raw slots, a 63-gate circuit has at most 54 proof-tree raw slots. For each geometry the checker gives every possible witness row permission to activate the helper and enumerates the local containment profiles with one through four raw slots per vertex. Four slots with no required containments also relax every larger budget. Forced witness pairs forbid the corresponding two containments from holding simultaneously. CaDiCaL with internal proof checking returns UNSAT at 54 raw slots for both geometries. For fewer than nine distinct raw labels, the subset branch calculation closes every remaining case, visiting 62,105 and 105,335 nodes respectively, including pruned nodes. Repeated raw labels can only increase the private cost. This proves the bound for arbitrary flag responses also in the one-plus-one case.

## A singleton-free shared signal on nine raw labels

Suppose the sole additional shared signal \(H\) is false on every singleton. Let \(U\) be the distinct raw labels in its private tree and \(T\) the flags with both endpoints in \(U\). On a positive row assigned to a proof tree after \(H\), two comparisons to its cold singletons force both raw leaves in the private tree whenever \(H\) is true. Thus every flag outside \(T\) can be routed in its original proof tree without reporting \(H\). The raw-leaf bound on this graph plus the private cost gives
\[
s\ge 52-|T|+\nu(E-T)+|U|.
\]
The [support checker](degenerate-plane-helper.py) enumerates all choices through twelve raw labels using verified symmetries. Every choice through eight gives at least 64 immediately. At nine, 936 of the 3,124,550 choices give just 63. In each, \(T\) consists of ten connected edges on all nine vertices and \(E-T\) has a matching of size twelve.

Equality would force exactly nine private raw slots. If any row of \(T\) can be routed without \(H\), restoring it increases the raw bound by at least one. In the remaining case every one of the ten rows activates \(H\) before its proof-tree root, with every earlier safe or inactive terminal cold there. Setting all nonraw terminals in the private tree to zero therefore gives a read-once monotone formula on the nine raw inputs, true on all ten pairs and false on singletons. This formula is a lower bound for \(H\) on every input.

The graph of its true two-element inputs on the nine labels is a disjoint union of complete bipartite graphs and isolated vertices. Simplify constants first. At a root OR its children have disjoint variables and neither accepts a singleton. At a root AND that rejects singletons, a two-element input must activate one singleton in each child. These facts give the assertion inductively. The ten required edges are connected on all nine vertices, so the whole component is one complete bipartite graph. Its shores are fixed by the ten edges. Consequently \(H\) is true whenever the chosen input hits both the point and line parts of \(U\).

For every one of the 936 layouts the checker finds an edge \(e\) of \(T\) to restore. At both endpoints of \(e\) it finds an actual desk negative containing every retained opposite neighbor and hitting both parts of \(U\). For every double-domination comparison with \(e\) as a witness row it finds a desk negative containing both required opposite endpoints, missing both central endpoints and again hitting both parts. The other rows can already be routed without \(H\). Thus all the raw-leaf conditions hold with 43 retained edges and a matching of at least twelve, giving \(s\ge43+12+9=64\). This establishes the bound for all singleton-free helpers using at most nine distinct private raw labels. It allows arbitrary gate structure behind the other safe or inactive terminals.

## Several shared raw pairs

For three raw-pair helpers the previously [checked pair and domination bounds](plane-shared-formulas.md#three-shared-raw-ors) use two kinds of columns. A comparison for double domination is an independent pair plus the partners of its two central endpoints. Each helper can add at most one other vertex, so these columns have at most five vertices. A helper joining the two central endpoints would insert a forbidden endpoint into the comparison and is already counted as a possible conflict, unless oriented to remove an obligation. A vertex forcing column consists of its retained neighbors plus its own partners. If it has three partners on its own shore, all three helpers have been used, and all selected opposite vertices belong to its pencil or line. Otherwise its own shore has at most two selected vertices. Thus every independent column used by those certificates lies below a degenerate desk negative. Removing rows or orienting a helper only shrinks the columns. The finite profile certificates for three cross-shore helpers use these same columns.

For four pure pairs the same-shore result follows from the first theorem. In the three-plus-one split a comparison uses at most two vertices on one shore, and the forcing columns have a pencil or line on one shore. In the two-plus-two split, a change is possible only if both point pairs meet at their central point and both line pairs meet at their central line, and the central pair is incident. The [two-plus-two checker](degenerate-plane-pairs.py) verifies the incidence relabeling to the earlier certificate and transitivity on the 52 flags. At one fixed flag it checks all 4,356 layouts in 67 orbits, testing degeneracy explicitly. The smallest bound there is 66. All other layouts inherit the earlier 64-gate certificate.

A signal having exactly the singleton and flag responses of a raw pair OR can be replaced by that OR at the same gate location. The replacement is globally at most the original, agrees on every positive flag and preserves the necessary singleton responses of later replacements. Dead private gates may be discarded. Thus the pair results admit arbitrary additional behavior off these inputs.

## Direct search below 64

Use the [five unfinished point stars](shared-pencil-search.py) with the three available line disjunctions from the completed point-6 star. Their boundary has 20 positive and 44 maximal negative projected inputs. Each local template starts at 24 gates. The [generator](degenerate-pencil-search.py) builds 243 independently balanced first-level layouts, together with 60 layouts using an unbalanced four-neighbor OR at exactly one star and canonical balanced trees at the others. Each layout is combined with all 105 rooted unordered binary trees joining the five stars.

In each of the resulting 31,815 ordered gate lists, bypass any gate with any earlier wire. At a different position, on either side of the bypass, allow an arbitrary AND or OR of any two earlier wires, including repeated operands. The [search](degenerate-pencil-repairs.cpp) clamps the changed wire at zero and one and propagates both values through all uses. The rows on which the output depends on that wire give its exact required bits. All 110,507,688 feasible windows were exhausted without a 23-gate suffix. A deliberately damaged positive control was repaired. This search leaves other topologies, edits and prefixes open.

Run the three geometry/helper checkers with Python 3. The single-helper check also calls the proof-checking CaDiCaL binary installed with the repository’s Lean toolchain. Run the local search with Python 3 and a C++17 compiler. [Lean](DegeneratePlaneProfiles.lean) checks the private-slot implication, monotone mask update, optional-input closure characterization and the arithmetic bounds. To reproduce, compile the existing PlaneSharedFormulas.lean to a temporary object using the repository's Lean environment, then run Lean on the new file with that directory prepended to LEAN_PATH. The graph-to-circuit proofs above are written arguments supported by these component checks.
